I am writing to report suspected fraud, waste, and abuse of

controlled by Brian Roderick Banks (DOB: [date of birth withheld]), who serves

management organization that manages them.

This complaint is based on publicly available records, including
school check registers posted on the MacDowell Preparatory Academy
website, audited financial statements, Michigan corporate filings,
campaign finance disclosures, court records, and investigative
reporting. Supporting documents are listed at the end of this
letter.

I. SUPERINTENDENT WITH EIGHT FELONY CONVICTIONS

Brian Roderick Banks is the superintendent of:

1. MacDowell Preparatory Academy — approximately 400 students,

II. SELF-DEALING THROUGH CHARTER MANAGEMENT ORGANIZATION

Banks owns The Purpose Group Michigan LLC, a charter management
organization (CMO) that manages both schools. CMOs typically
extract 10-15% of per-pupil funding as management fees.

Banks simultaneously:

- Controls the CMO (presumed managing member of The Purpose Group
  Michigan LLC)
- Serves as superintendent of both schools
- Sits on the governing boards of both schools

This creates a self-dealing loop in which Banks sets his own
compensation and management fees using public education funds.
MacDowell Preparatory Academy reported $667,426 in "School
Administration Services" in FY2025 audited financial statements
(Alan C. Young & Associates, P.C.). Purpose Charter Academy's
Year 1 per-pupil funding is estimated at approximately $2.16
million, flowing through Banks' management company.

The Purpose Group Michigan LLC is registered to Banks' home
address at 1968 Severn Road, Grosse Pointe Woods, MI 48236.

III. GIFT CARD EMBEZZLEMENT SCHEME

The MacDowell Preparatory Academy check register for FY2023-24
(a public document posted on the school's website) shows $13,970
in payments directly to Brian Banks, mostly described as "gift
cards":

| Date       | Check # | Amount  | Description                    |
|------------|---------|---------|--------------------------------|
| 08/08/2023 | 10056   | $1,890  | Staff Gift Cards               |
| 11/20/2023 | 1026    | $400    | Gift cards for travel          |
| 12/20/2023 | 1033    | $9,945  | Staff gifts cards              |
| 01/11/2024 | 1035    | $265    | Reimbursement                  |
| 06/24/2024 | 10044   | $1,470  | Staff Gift Cards for NCSC24    |
| **Total**  |         | **$13,970** |                            |

Gift cards are a classic embezzlement vector. Once purchased,
they are untraceable and convertible to cash without an audit
trail. A single check for $9,945 for "staff gift cards" at
Christmas is not consistent with normal school operations. Banks
received these payments while serving as superintendent and
exercising budget authority over the school's approximately
$5.9 million in annual expenditures.

VII. FEDERAL FUNDS AT RISK

Both schools almost certainly receive federal education funding,
including:

- Title I, Part A (schools serving high-poverty student
  populations)
- Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) funds
- National School Lunch Program reimbursement

MacDowell Preparatory Academy serves approximately 400 students in
a high-poverty Detroit neighborhood. Its FY2025 audited financial
statements report total expenditures of $5,896,918 and a deficit
of $968,295.

If Banks is diverting federal education funds through his CMO
management fees, gift card purchases payable to himself, or
suspicious vendor payments described in Sections III and IV, this
constitutes potential fraud against the United States under 18
U.S.C. § 1341 (mail fraud), 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (wire fraud), and
31 U.S.C. § 3729 (False Claims Act).

REQUEST FOR INVESTIGATION

I respectfully request that the Office of Inspector General
investigate the use of federal education funds at MacDowell
Preparatory Academy and Purpose Charter Academy, including:

1. A full audit of all Title I and IDEA disbursements to both
   schools for the past five fiscal years, tracing funds from
   federal allocation through CMO management fees to ultimate
   expenditure.

2. Review of all management contracts between The Purpose Group
   Michigan LLC and both schools, including fee structures,
   related-party disclosures, and board approval documentation.

3. Verification that written board approval exists under Michigan
   Compiled Laws § 380.1230(9) for Banks' employment as
   superintendent, given his eight felony convictions.

4. Background checks and conflict-of-interest review of all board
   members of both schools, with particular attention to Judges
   Miller and Yancey.

5. Forensic review of the MacDowell check register payments
   described in Sections III and IV, including gift card purchase
   receipts, vendor invoices, and board authorization records.

6. Review of Joseph Holland Jr.'s financial role at Purpose
   Foundation and any compensation received from school-related
   entities.
