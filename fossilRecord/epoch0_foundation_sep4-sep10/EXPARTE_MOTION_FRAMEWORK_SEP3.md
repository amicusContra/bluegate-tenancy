## WHAT IS AN EX PARTE MOTION?

Under MCR 2.119(H), a court may enter ex parte orders only when:
1. Allowed by statute or court rule
2. There is a showing of **immediate and irreparable injury**
3. The movant certifies the efforts made to give notice to opposing counsel

The most common ex parte motion in a fraud case is a **Temporary Restraining Order (TRO)** under MCR 3.310.

## OPTION 3: NOTICE TO JUDGE STOKES (Informal)

If you go to the clerk's office and ask to speak with the judge's clerk about scheduling, you can:

1. **File both the original complaint and amended complaint** (if not already in her system)
2. **Request a scheduling conference** — this puts it on the docket and forces a response timeline
3. **File a motion for expedited hearing** — given the post-service intimidation and active school operations

This is NOT ex parte in the legal sense — it's just getting the case moving.

## CAUTION

Ex parte communications with a judge are **strictly limited** in Michigan. You cannot:
- Discuss the merits of the case without the other side present
- Ask the judge to rule on anything without proper notice
- Present evidence outside a filed motion

What you CAN do:
- File motions with the clerk
- Ask the clerk about scheduling
- Request an emergency hearing through proper channels
- File a TRO motion which the court can rule on ex parte under MCR 3.310

**Everything goes through paper. File first, talk second.**
