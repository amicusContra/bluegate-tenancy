                    I. INTRODUCTION

    1. This is a civil racketeering action brought
under the Michigan Prevention of Racketeering Act
("Michigan RICO"), MCL 750.159i et seq. Plaintiff
seeks treble damages, attorney fees, costs, and
injunctive relief against an association-in-fact
enterprise that has operated for over twenty-eight
(28) years to defraud the public, exploit vulnerable
communities, and enrich its members through a
coordinated pattern of racketeering activity.

    2. At its center, this enterprise is a convicted
felon with eight (8) felony convictions for financial
fraud who — with the protection of sitting judges,
the Wayne County Treasurer, and the Wayne County
Executive — operates publicly funded charter schools
as vehicles for data harvesting, financial exploitation,
and a school-to-revenue pipeline that converts
vulnerable Detroit families into government revenue
streams.

    3. Plaintiff, a United States Army veteran and
resident of Ingham County, Michigan, personally
witnessed the enterprise's operations, was directly
threatened by its front man, and suffered direct
harm when the enterprise enrolled his partner's
children in a school run by a convicted felon
operating under false academic and professional
credentials.


              II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE

    4. This Court has jurisdiction over this action
pursuant to MCL 750.159j(1), which provides that
"[a] person who sustains injury or damage by reason
of a violation of [MCL 750.159i]" may bring a civil
action in circuit court.

    5. Venue is proper in Ingham County pursuant to
MCL 600.1629 because Plaintiff is a resident of
Ingham County and a substantial part of the events
giving rise to this action occurred in, or the
effects were felt in, Ingham County.

    6. This Court has personal jurisdiction over
each Defendant because each has conducted business
in or committed acts within the State of Michigan
that form the basis of this Complaint.

       V. THE ENTERPRISE'S BUSINESS MODEL:
          SCHOOL-TO-REVENUE PIPELINE

    24. Purpose Charter Academy's stated mission is to
serve "at-risk" youth with chronic absenteeism and
behavioral issues, preparing them for "law and public
service." This is not charity. This is inventory
acquisition.

children's Social Security numbers, birth certificates,
medical records, parents' income data, employment
information, addresses, family structure, custody
status, and emergency contacts — providing a convicted
financial fraud felon with comprehensive financial
data on every enrolled family.

    26. The enterprise generates revenue through
multiple streams:

    a. STREAM 1 — PER-PUPIL PUBLIC FUNDING: The State
       of Michigan pays approximately $9,000 or more
       per student per year. At-risk designations may
       qualify for additional funding. This revenue
       flows through Purpose Group LLC and Purpose
       Foundation — entities controlled by Banks with
       finances managed by Holland.

    b. STREAM 2 — CRIMINAL JUSTICE PIPELINE: At-risk
       students feed into the criminal justice system.
       Miller's courtroom (Third Judicial Circuit,
       Criminal Division) processes them. As documented
       in Bryant v. Miller (2:26-cv-10917), every
       conviction generates mandatory fees under MCL
       769.1k flowing to the Wayne County General Fund,
       which services $121.8 million in annual bond
       debt. Yancey's courtroom (36th District) serves
       as the arraignment gateway.

    c. STREAM 3 — FAMILY FINANCIAL EXPLOITATION:
       Families already in distress become bankruptcy
       and tax foreclosure targets. Sabree's office
       handles their tax liens. Banks has their
       financial data from enrollment. The same PSCU
       lending ecosystem serves both Banks and Yancey.

    d. STREAM 4 — POLITICAL CAPITAL: "Serving at-risk
       youth" generates political goodwill. Evans
       endorses Banks. Miller's judicial credibility
       legitimizes the school. The enterprise's
       community-service veneer provides cover for
       its actual operations.


          VI. PATTERN OF RACKETEERING ACTIVITY

    27. Defendants have engaged in a "pattern of
racketeering activity" as defined by MCL 750.159f(c),
consisting of not less than two (2) incidents of
racketeering that are related to the affairs of the
enterprise and that are not isolated, having occurred
within ten (10) years of each other. The pattern
consists of at least the following predicate acts:

    PREDICATE ACT 1 — FINANCIAL FRAUD (Banks)

    28. In 2017, Banks pled guilty to eight (8) felony
counts of financial fraud in Case No. 16-008948-FH,
Wayne County Circuit Court. These convictions
constitute racketeering under MCL 750.159f(c), which
incorporates violations of MCL 750.218 (false
pretenses) and related financial crime statutes.

    PREDICATE ACT 2 — BANKRUPTCY FRAUD (Banks)

    29. Banks filed for bankruptcy twice (Case Nos.
98-49073 and 06-55281, E.D. Mich.) while
simultaneously maintaining undisclosed assets and
financial relationships. Upon information and belief,
Banks' bankruptcy schedules contain material omissions
regarding assets, income, and financial relationships
with co-defendants, in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 152,
which is incorporated as a RICO predicate under both
federal and Michigan law.

    PREDICATE ACT 3 — UNAUTHORIZED PRACTICE OF LAW
    (Banks)

    30. Banks has continuously and repeatedly
represented himself as a licensed attorney, using
the credential "J.D." in professional communications,
on school websites, and in direct conversations.
On August 29, 2026, Banks personally stated to
Plaintiff "I'm a lawyer" at Purpose Charter Academy.
Banks is not licensed to practice law in any
jurisdiction. This constitutes a felony under MCL
450.681.

    PREDICATE ACT 4 — CAMPAIGN FINANCE VIOLATIONS
    (Banks, Yancey)

    31. Banks owes $14,500 in outstanding campaign
finance fines to the State of Michigan, evidencing
a pattern of violations of MCL 169.215 et seq.
Yancey's judicial campaign paid approximately $15,750
to Banks' PAC for services from a convicted felon's
political operation, with Holland (5 federal
bankruptcies) serving as PAC Treasurer.

    PREDICATE ACT 5 — BANK FRAUD (Banks, Holland)

    32. Banks obtained a $232,000 mortgage and
subsequently a $93,700 HELOC from Public Service
Credit Union on the 1968 Severn property despite
eight felony convictions and two prior bankruptcies.
Upon information and belief, Banks' mortgage
application contained material omissions regarding
his criminal history, in violation of 18 U.S.C.
§ 1344 and MCL 750.411.

    PREDICATE ACT 6 — WIRE FRAUD (Banks, Miller,
    Yancey, Holland, Ellis)

    33. Defendants used electronic communications
(email, websites, electronic enrollment systems,
electronic banking) to solicit school enrollment,
collect personal financial data, transmit PAC
solicitations, and coordinate enterprise operations,
in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1343.

    PREDICATE ACT 7 — MONEY LAUNDERING (Banks,
    Holland, Ellis)

    34. The enterprise employed a layered financial
structure to obscure the flow of funds:
    a. Banks transferred his primary asset (1968
       Severn) to the Brian Banks Living Trust
       (August 2024);
    b. Purpose Foundation was incorporated two months
       later (December 2024);
    c. Purpose Group LLC was formed as the school
       operating entity;
    d. A $93,700 HELOC was obtained against the trust
       property one month after PCA opened (October
       2025).
    This structure constitutes money laundering in
violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1956 and MCL 750.411c.

    PREDICATE ACT 8 — EXTORTION UNDER COLOR OF
    RIGHT (Miller, Yancey, Sabree, Evans)

    35. Miller, Yancey, Sabree, and Evans used their
official government positions to benefit the
enterprise:
    a. Miller used her judicial office to co-found
       PCA with a convicted felon;
    b. Yancey used her judicial position as Board
       Chair of MacDowell;
    c. Sabree used his Treasurer's office to certify
       property transactions, provide tax protection,
       and endorse enterprise members;
    d. Evans used his Executive office to endorse
       enterprise members and, upon information and
       belief, facilitate appointments benefiting the
       enterprise.
    These acts constitute extortion under color of
right in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1951 (Hobbs Act).

    PREDICATE ACT 9 — PUBLIC CORRUPTION / HONEST
    SERVICES FRAUD (Miller, Yancey, Sabree)

    36. Miller, Yancey, and Sabree deprived the
public of their honest services as government
officials by participating in the enterprise while
exercising official authority:
    a. Miller co-founded a school with a convicted
       felon while serving as a Criminal Division
       judge whose courtroom generates revenue that
       services county bond debt;
    b. Yancey serves as Board Chair of a felon's
       school while presiding over the arraignment
       gateway;
    c. Sabree certified property transactions for
       enterprise members, endorsed a convicted felon
       for public office, and placed family members
       (Adam Sabree, Aliyah Sabree) in judicial
       positions within the same county system.
    These acts constitute honest services fraud in
violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1346.

    PREDICATE ACT 10 — CONSPIRACY (All Defendants)

    37. All Defendants participated in a conspiracy
to defraud the public by operating charter schools
under the direction of a convicted felon, providing
official cover for the enterprise, and coordinating
their activities for mutual benefit, in violation
of 18 U.S.C. § 371.

    PREDICATE ACT 11 — FALSE STATEMENTS (Banks,
    Sabree)

    38. Banks submitted an application for Michigan
School Administrator Certificate No. AD0000048782.
Upon information and belief, this application
contained false or materially incomplete statements
regarding Banks' criminal history, academic
credentials, and/or fitness for certification.
Sabree certified tax documents knowing the
circumstances of the transactions. These constitute
false statements on official documents.
