## I. INTRODUCTION

This section demonstrates that Plaintiff Shayla Smith lacked standing to invoke Wayne County venue when she filed this custody action on June 17, 2026, while Defendant/Counter-Plaintiff DaSean Mitchell — who actually maintained a legitimate Wayne County residence during the same period — deliberately filed his protective proceedings in Ingham County, where the minor children resided. The contrast is dispositive: one party followed Michigan's child-residence venue rules in good faith; the other manufactured a Wayne County address to forum-shop against a father who lives ninety miles away.

## II. LEGAL STANDARD

### A. Custody Venue Is Tied to the Child's Residence — Not the Parent's Convenience

Under the Child Custody Act of 1970, when no court has prior continuing jurisdiction, "[t]he action shall be submitted to the circuit court of the county where the child resides or may be found." **MCL 722.26(2)**. Custody disputes "shall have precedence for hearing and assignment for trial over other civil actions." **MCL 722.26(1)**. Venue is determined as of the date the action is instituted. *Kubiak v Steen*, 61 Mich App 410, 412–413 (1974).

The UCCJEA reinforces the same principle for initial custody determinations. A child's "home state" is "the state in which a child lived with a parent or a person acting as a parent for at least 6 consecutive months immediately before the commencement of a child-custody proceeding." **MCL 722.1102(g)**. Michigan courts may decline jurisdiction as an inconvenient forum when another county is better positioned to access evidence, protect the parties, and serve the child's interests. **MCL 722.1207**.

Critically, **custody venue is not PPO venue**. Under **MCR 3.703(E)(1)**, a petitioner against an adult respondent may file a personal protection action in any Michigan county regardless of residency. That permissive PPO rule exists precisely because the Legislature and Supreme Court did not extend it to custody. Custody must be filed where the child lives — not where a parent wishes to litigate.

### B. "Residence" Means Actual, Established Dwelling — Not a Mailing Address on a Complaint

Michigan courts distinguish a party's actual residence from a temporary location or an address of convenience. In *Kubiak*, the Court of Appeals held that although the child was hospitalized in Wayne County at filing, venue properly remained in Macomb County because that was where the child actually lived before the hospitalization. *Id.* at 413–414. A parent's or grandparent's house is not automatically the child's "residence" unless the child has genuinely established a home there — with the indicia of residency that Michigan law and common sense require.

### C. Related Proceedings Must Be Consolidated: One Family, One Judge

When multiple proceedings affect the same family, Michigan law demands coordination:

- **MCL 600.1023**: "Whenever practicable," matters within the Family Division involving members of the same family pending in the same judicial circuit must be assigned to the judge who received the first matter.
- **MCR 3.204(A)**: If a circuit court action involving custody, parenting time, or support is pending — or the court has continuing jurisdiction from a prior action — a new complaint affecting a different child of the same parents must be filed in that court and assigned to the same judge.

DaSean's PPO proceedings are already pending in Ingham County. Shayla's unilateral choice of Wayne County fractures a single family dispute across two judicial circuits, two Friend of the Court offices, and two dockets — in direct tension with **MCL 600.1023** and **MCR 3.204**.

## VI. COURT ACCESS, EMERGENCY RELIEF, AND THE BURDEN OF DUAL FORUMS

### A. Custody Must Be Heard Where the Children Live — and Ingham Is That County

As of June 17, 2026, the minor children's established residence was **Ingham County**. They had lived with DaSean at [REDACTED_ADDR_2]nue since June 2026. Before that, their home state under **MCL 722.1102(g)** was **Macomb County** (Bradford Road, six consecutive months). At no point does the record support Wayne County as the county where the children "reside or may be found." **MCL 722.26(2)**.

Shayla's Wayne County filing forces a father whose children live in Lansing to litigate custody in downtown Detroit — approximately **ninety miles** and **ninety minutes** each way — for every hearing, emergency motion, status conference, and Friend of the Court appointment.

### B. Emergency Relief: Wayne County's Docket vs. Ingham County's Responsiveness

DaSean's experience in this very case illustrates the practical cost of Shayla's venue selection:

| Court | Proceeding | Filed | Result |
|-------|-----------|-------|--------|
| **Wayne County** (this case) | Emergency motion | Sep 3, 2026 | Accepted Sep 4; as of Sep 8, **not yet on the docket** for judicial review — two business days consumed by clerk backlog plus Labor Day |
| **Ingham County** | PPO petitions (multiple) | Prior to Jun 17, 2026 | Filed and routed to judicial review in the county where DaSean and the children reside |

Wayne County's Third Circuit Family Division serves a population of more than 1.7 million. Its domestic relations docket is chronically congested. Emergency custody motions compete with thousands of pending family cases across twelve domestic relations judges. Ingham County's 30th Circuit, by contrast, serves a smaller population and has already absorbed the related PPO proceedings involving these same parties and the same underlying conduct.

When a child's safety is at issue, venue in a distant, backlogged urban docket is not neutral — it is a structural disadvantage to the out-of-county parent. Shayla selected that disadvantage deliberately.

### C. PPO Proceedings Are Already in Ingham — Consolidation Is Mandatory in Principle

Multiple personal protection proceedings involving Shayla, Leon Smith, Marquita Smith, Simone Smith, and Brian Banks are pending in **Ingham County**, where DaSean resides and where the children lived for the three months preceding Shayla's complaint. Under **MCR 3.204(A)**, new custody actions affecting children of the same parents should be filed in a court that already has jurisdiction over related matters. Under **MCL 600.1023**, all practicable family matters should be aligned before a single judge.

Shayla's Wayne County filing creates:

- **Split dockets** — custody in Detroit, PPOs in Lansing
- **Split Friend of the Court** — two counties conducting overlapping investigations of the same family
- **Split judicial oversight** — no single judge with full picture of the abuse history, the PPO record, and the custody dispute
- **Split burden on DaSean** — ninety-mile travel for custody while simultaneously defending PPO proceedings in Ingham

The Legislature did not intend this fragmentation. **MCL 600.1023** exists precisely to prevent it.

### D. Inconvenient Forum Analysis Favors Ingham

Under **MCL 722.1207**, a court may decline jurisdiction when another forum is significantly more convenient. Every factor points to Ingham:

| **MCL 722.1207 Factor** | **Ingham County** | **Wayne County** |
|--------------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Location of evidence (school, medical, FOC records) | Children attended school in Macomb; resided in Ingham Jun–Sep 2026 | No established connection |
| Distance from parties' homes | DaSean lives in Lansing; children lived there | Shayla lived in Eastpointe (Macomb); Dresden is parents' house |
| Related proceedings | PPOs pending; civil actions pending | Only this manufactured custody case |
| Domestic violence history | PPO record already before Ingham courts | Assault at Dresden Sep 2 — post-filing |

Wayne County is an inconvenient forum. Ingham County — or, alternatively, Macomb County, where the children resided for six years — is the proper venue.

## VIII. CONCLUSION

The venue record in this case inverts every principle Michigan law protects:

1. **DaSean Mitchell** had a legitimate Wayne County address at XXXX Cornerstone Boulevard — his mother's home — from February through June 2026. He did not file there. He filed in Ingham County, where the children resided, because **MCL 722.26(2)** demands it.

2. **Shayla Smith** had no established residence in Wayne County. She lived in Macomb County after separation. Her Medicaid, her school choices, her family's bankruptcies, mortgages, and rentals — all Macomb. She filed in Wayne County anyway, listing her father's house as her own, to impose a ninety-mile litigation burden on DaSean and to sever custody from the Ingham County PPO proceedings already underway.

3. **The children** resided in Macomb County for six years and in Ingham County with DaSean for three months before Shayla's complaint. Wayne County was never their home.

4. **Consolidation** under **MCL 600.1023** and **MCR 3.204** requires that related proceedings involving this family be heard together — in the county with the most significant connection to the children and the existing docket. That county is Ingham, not Wayne.

DaSean had Wayne County standing and chose Ingham. Shayla had no Wayne County standing and chose Wayne. The Court should transfer this action to Ingham County Circuit Court — or, in the alternative, to Macomb County Circuit Court — where venue is proper under **MCL 722.26(2)**, where the children's residence and home-state connections point, and where this family can be heard by one court instead of two.
