## I. LEGAL DUTY TO PRESERVE

Upon commencement of litigation — and upon receipt of this Notice — you and each person and entity acting on your behalf have an immediate, affirmative, and ongoing **legal duty to preserve** all documents, electronically stored information ("ESI"), and tangible things that are relevant, or that may become relevant, to the claims and defenses in this action and all related proceedings.

This duty arises from:

1. The **pending litigation** identified above;
2. Your **actual or constructive knowledge** that documents and ESI in your possession, custody, or control may be relevant to these cases;
3. Michigan law governing discovery, spoliation of evidence, and the duty of parties to preserve potentially relevant materials once litigation is reasonably foreseeable or commenced.

**This obligation extends to you individually and collectively, and to all of your agents, employees, officers, directors, members, managers, contractors, consultants, volunteers, representatives, and any other person or entity acting on your behalf or under your direction or control** — including, without limitation, all persons associated with MacDowell Preparatory Academy, Purpose Charter Academy, The Purpose Group Michigan LLC, Purpose Foundation, Banks Strategy & Consultants LLC, Bank on Banks for Michigan PAC, and any other entity owned, operated, managed, or controlled by Brian Roderick Banks or Joseph Holland Jr.

You must **immediately suspend** any routine or automatic destruction, deletion, overwriting, or modification of potentially relevant materials. This includes, but is not limited to:

- Email retention and auto-deletion policies
- Text message and instant messaging auto-delete settings
- Cloud storage sync-and-purge routines
- Social media post deletion or archiving settings
- Backup tape rotation and overwrite schedules
- Document shredding or disposal programs
- Temporary file and cache clearing that removes relevant data

Failure to preserve relevant evidence may constitute **spoliation of evidence** under Michigan law and may result in:

- **Adverse inference instructions** to the jury
- **Evidentiary sanctions**, including exclusion of testimony or documents
- **Monetary sanctions** and attorney fees (where applicable)
- **Contempt sanctions**
- **Criminal liability** for obstruction of justice or related offenses, where applicable

This duty applies to **paper records and electronic records alike**, including data stored on personal devices, work devices, cloud accounts, removable media, servers, and third-party platforms.

**NOTE:** Any materials that should have been preserved under the September 2, 2026 Spoliation Notice and that have been destroyed, altered, or allowed to be deleted since that date are subject to spoliation sanctions regardless of the expanded scope of this Supplemental Notice.
