## I. NEW EVIDENCE: BANKS' PUBLISHED ADMISSIONS ABOUT JUDGE YANCEY

### A. Same Street, Same Schools — Childhood Friends

Banks describes his lifelong relationship with Yancey in
Chapter 13 (Friends How Many of Us Have Them):

> "One of my elementary school friends was Tenisha. Tenisha and
> I lived on the same street and went to elementary and high
> school together. Tenisha and I would often get in trouble for
> talking in class and would often talk on the phone in the
> evening after school."
— Ch. 13, pp. 15-17

This is not a professional acquaintance. Banks and Yancey grew
up together — **same street, same elementary school, same high
school.** This friendship predates any political or professional
connection by decades.

### B. Reconnected as Adults — Same Complex, Then Same Street

> "There was a short period after high school where Tenisha and
> I didn't see or connect with each other. When we did run back
> into each other for a while, we picked up like there was no
> break in our communication. Later in life as adults, we both
> somehow ended up living downtown in the same complex."
— Ch. 13, pp. 18-20

After initially reconnecting, they became neighbors again:

> "Currently, we live one street over from each other and
> regularly discuss legislative and policy issues, community
> concerns or just to catch up."
— Ch. 13, pp. 25-27

**As of the book's 2020 publication,** Banks and Yancey lived
one street apart and "regularly discuss legislative and policy
issues." A sitting judge regularly discussing "legislative and
policy issues" with a convicted felon who operates charter
schools — while chairing the board of one of those schools —
is a direct violation of judicial independence.

### C. Banks Recruited Yancey as His Successor

Banks describes personally selecting Yancey to replace him in
the Michigan House after his resignation:

> "I reached out to my childhood friend, Tenisha, and asked
> her to think about it ... I would support her and be with
> her every step of the way."
— Ch. 12 (When the Bottom Falls Out), pp. 152-155

> "Tenisha won and became my successor."
— Ch. 12, p. 162

> "This is the same Tenisha who succeeded me after I resigned
> from office."
— Ch. 13, p. 23

Banks did not merely endorse Yancey or support her campaign.
He **recruited** her. He "reached out" and "asked her to think
about it." He promised to "be with her every step of the way."
Yancey's political career exists because Banks personally
selected her to succeed him.

A judge who owes her entire political trajectory to a convicted
felon — who then pays that felon $15,750 from her campaign
funds and chairs the board of his school — cannot claim
independent judgment.

### D. Banks Claims Yancey Had "Felonies and Misdemeanors"

In the most explosive passage, Banks writes about Yancey's
own criminal history:

> "As a young adult, Tenisha ended up having some troubles of
> her own which caused her to spend a month or two in jail.
> Luckily, we both got our stuff together and Tenisha graduated
> and received her bachelor's and law degrees. She then became
> an assistant prosecuting attorney even with felonies and
> misdemeanors."
— Ch. 13, pp. 22-24

Banks publicly states — in a commercially available book — that
a sitting judge had "felonies and misdemeanors" and spent time
in jail.

**This creates two devastating outcomes:**

1. **If true:** Judge Yancey has undisclosed felony convictions.
   The first supplement (Sep 8) documented stalking and retail
   fraud convictions from 1995 via public records. Banks' claim
   of "felonies and misdemeanors" — plural — suggests additional
   offenses beyond what has been publicly documented. A sitting
   judge with undisclosed felonies raises fundamental questions
   about her eligibility and candor.

2. **If false:** Banks defames a sitting judge in a published
   book — and the judge remains his friend, neighbor, political
   ally, Board Chair, and $15,750 campaign payee despite this
   defamation. This suggests that the "felonies and misdemeanors"
   claim is accurate and that Yancey tolerates its publication
   because challenging it would expose her.

**Either way, the Commission should investigate whether Judge
Yancey's full criminal history has been disclosed to the
appropriate authorities.**
