## III. RULES IMPLICATED

### A. MRPC 8.3(a) — Failure to Report Known Misconduct

MRPC 8.3(a) provides:

> "A lawyer having knowledge that another lawyer has committed
> a significant violation of the Rules of Professional Conduct
> that raises a substantial question as to that lawyer's honesty,
> trustworthiness, or fitness as a lawyer in other respects,
> shall inform the appropriate professional authority."

**Application:** While Banks is not a "lawyer" in the licensed
sense, his use of "J.D." on all professional materials and his
repeated claims of being "an attorney" constitute unauthorized
practice of law — a violation that any bar member who knows of
it has a duty to report. Judge Langford Morris has known since
at least 2010 (Banks' graduation) that Banks had a J.D. but
was not admitted to the bar. Her duty to report arose the moment
Banks began using the credential publicly.

Additionally, MRPC 8.3(a) applies because licensed attorneys
in Banks' orbit (Schmidtke, Fadel, Giallonardo, Perkins, Martin)
have been enabling Banks' UPL — and Judge Langford Morris, as
their fellow bar member, had knowledge of this enabling network
and failed to report it.

### B. MRPC 8.4(a) — Assisting Misconduct

Judge Langford Morris actively endorsed Banks' credential fraud
by **writing the foreword** for a book titled "Brian Banks, J.D."
— a title that implies attorney status to the public. This is
not passive failure to report. This is active endorsement of
the unauthorized credential.

### C. MRPC 8.4(c) — Dishonesty, Fraud, Deceit, or Misrepresentation

A former Assistant U.S. Attorney — with specific expertise in
false representations and criminal fraud — endorsed a convicted
felon's fraudulent credential in a commercially published book.
This endorsement lends the credibility of a 30-year judge and
former federal prosecutor to a man she knows is not licensed
to practice law.

### D. MRPC 8.4(d) — Conduct Prejudicial to the Administration of Justice

A 21-year personal relationship with a convicted felon —
including encouraging him to attend law school, accepting his
campaign volunteer labor, and writing the foreword for his
"J.D." book — is conduct prejudicial to the administration
of justice when it enables that felon to impersonate a lawyer
while operating publicly funded charter schools.
