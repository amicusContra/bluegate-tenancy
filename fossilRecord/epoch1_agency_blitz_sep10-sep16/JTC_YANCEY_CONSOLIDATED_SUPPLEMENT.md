## I. CAMPAIGN PAYMENT TO BANKS: $15,750 (44.9% OF TOTAL SPEND)

Public campaign finance records establish that Judge Yancey's
judicial campaign paid **Banks Strategy and Consultants** $15,366
during the 2022 election cycle — **44.9%** of her total campaign
expenditures ($34,212). Across all cycles, total payments to Banks
Strategy & Consultants were **$15,750**.

Banks Strategy and Consultants LLC (LARA ID 802070120) is Brian
Banks' personal consulting company, formed **eight days** after his
February 2017 resignation from the Michigan House of Representatives
following a guilty plea for loan fraud. In the 2022 cycle, Banks
Strategy & Consultants received **$0 from any other client** — Judge
Yancey was Banks' only consulting customer that year.

The sequence:

1. Banks resigned in disgrace (February 2017) after guilty plea
2. Banks formed Banks Strategy & Consultants LLC eight days later
3. Yancey won Banks' vacated seat in House District 1 (November 2017)
4. Yancey paid Banks' consulting firm $15,366 from her judicial
   campaign (2022)
5. Yancey now chairs the board of Banks' school (MacDowell)

A sitting judge paid nearly half her campaign budget to a convicted
felon's consulting firm, then assumed a governance role at his
school. This alone implicates Canon 2 and Canon 4.

**Source:** TransparencyUSA; Michigan LARA business entity filings.

## VIII. CANON VIOLATIONS (Consolidated)

**Canon 1 — Integrity and Independence.** A judge with her own
felony convictions, who paid a convicted felon nearly half her
campaign budget and now chairs his school board, undermines public
confidence in the integrity and independence of the judiciary.

**Canon 2 — Impropriety and Appearance of Impropriety.** The $15,750
payment, the unresolved residency question (filing Detroit for
judicial eligibility while claiming Harper Woods for tax purposes),
and the fiduciary failures at MacDowell create multiple appearances
of impropriety.

**Canon 3 — Impartiality.** Judge Yancey's personal, political, and
financial relationships with Banks predate and permeate her judicial
service. The closed-loop campaign finance network raises questions
about impartiality in any matter involving network-connected parties.

**Canon 4 — Extrajudicial Activities.** Board service at MacDowell —
a school where the superintendent pays himself in gift cards and
employs a convicted drug trafficker as financial officer — casts
reasonable doubt on judicial capacity.
