# JTC EQUITY FRAME — Strategic Framing for All Future Filings
# September 16, 2026

## THE ANTICIPATED DEFENSE

The Association of Black Judges of Michigan (ABJM) raised concerns
to the Michigan Supreme Court that JTC sanctions disproportionately
affect Black judges. The NCSC confirmed statistical overrepresentation
at three decision points. AO 2023-2 authorized an independent audit.
Phase I (Jul 2024) and Phase II (2025) documented the disparities.

**Expected response from respondent judges or their advocates:**
"These complaints are part of a pattern of disproportionate targeting
of Black judges. The NCSC equity report confirms this systemic bias.
These filings should be viewed in that context."

---

## THE DISARMAMENT

The equity defense assumes the complaints are ABOUT the judges.
They are not. They are about the COMMUNITY being destroyed.

### The Frame:

**These judges are not being targeted because of their race.
They are being reported because they used their positions of
trust within the Black community to shield a convicted felon
who is actively harming Black children, Black families, and
Black neighborhoods — while enriching himself and his associates.**

The racial equity argument protects judges from unfair scrutiny.
It was never designed to protect judges who exploit their standing
within the community to enable fraud against that same community.

## THE STRUCTURAL ARGUMENT

The ABJM equity report actually SUPPORTS our complaints:

1. **The report found that powerful judges receive less scrutiny.**
   The very disparity the report documents — that outcomes depend
   on institutional position — explains how these judges avoided
   accountability for years. They are powerful. They are connected.
   Their misconduct was visible. No one acted.

2. **The report found that the JTC dismisses 94% of complaints
   without public action.** That is exactly what happened with
   Miller's gun case — dismissed with a private admonition
   containing factual errors (per Clutch Justice's de-anonymization).
   The equity problem isn't that Black judges face too many
   complaints. It's that the system protects ALL powerful judges
   from accountability — and when those judges are actively
   harming their own community, the protection becomes complicity.

3. **The true equity issue is who gets protected BY the judges,
   not who the judges ARE.** Banks is protected by multiple
   judges across two courts. Holland is protected. The enterprise
   is protected. The Black children in bottom-3% schools are not
   protected by anyone.

## WHAT THIS IS NOT

This is not an argument against racial equity in judicial discipline.
The ABJM was right to raise the concern. The NCSC findings are real.
Black judges DO face disproportionate scrutiny in some contexts.

This is not an argument about where anyone lives. People are free
to live where they choose. It is an observation about where public
education dollars are collected, where they are spent, and where
they ultimately arrive — and about the fiduciary duty of those
who control that journey.

This is an argument that racial equity cannot become racial immunity.
When judges use their community standing to enable the extraction
of resources from that community's most vulnerable members, the
equity mandate requires MORE scrutiny, not less — because the harm
falls disproportionately on the community the equity mandate exists
to protect.

**The children in these schools are the equity interest.**
**Not the judges on the boards.**
