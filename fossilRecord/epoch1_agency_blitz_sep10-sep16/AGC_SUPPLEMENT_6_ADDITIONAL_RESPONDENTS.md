## I. INTRODUCTION

I respectfully submit this Supplemental Request for Investigation to the Attorney Grievance Commission, identifying six additional members of the State Bar of Michigan who, through their professional and institutional association with Brian Roderick Banks, have violated MRPC 8.3 (duty to report known misconduct) and/or MRPC 8.4 (assisting or enabling misconduct).

This supplement should be read together with my original Request for Investigation filed September 10, 2026, against Hon. Cylenthia LaToye Miller, Mr. Lamar Moreland, and Mr. Todd J. Perkins. The factual background regarding Mr. Banks — including his felony conviction (Case No. 14-006194-FH), his J.D. from Michigan State University College of Law (2010), his lack of admission to the State Bar of Michigan, and his documented pattern of unauthorized practice of law — is set forth in that original complaint and is incorporated here by reference.

### A. The Subject of the Enabling: Brian Roderick Banks

For the Commission's convenience, the core facts are summarized below. Full documentation appears in the original complaint and its exhibits.

Mr. Banks is a convicted felon who holds a law degree but was never admitted to the bar. He repeatedly holds himself out as an attorney to law enforcement, parents, and the public — including on the campuses of charter schools he operates.

### B. The Hospital Analogy

As set forth in the original complaint, these respondents are the equivalent of licensed medical professionals who allowed an unlicensed person with a medical degree — and a prior conviction — to practice medicine under the implied authority of their credentials. A hospital board chaired by a licensed physician and a state health official would not permit an unlicensed operator to examine patients while wearing a lab coat, tell nurses and police that he is a licensed doctor, and use that false claim to control access to patients — all while the licensed professionals on the board take no corrective action.

**Every professional in the network inherited the liability the moment they chose not to act.** No medical board would tolerate this. The State Bar should not tolerate it either.

### C. Purpose of This Supplement

The original complaint identified three bar members whose governance roles and professional relationships placed them in direct proximity to Mr. Banks' unauthorized practice of law. Further investigation has identified six additional bar members — including four sitting judges, one Attorney Discipline Board hearing panelist, and one corporate attorney who formed Mr. Banks' management entity — whose conduct implicates the same Rules of Professional Conduct.

Including the original three respondents, this complaint now identifies **nine members of the State Bar of Michigan** who enabled, facilitated, or failed to report Mr. Banks' unauthorized practice of law. This is not coincidence. It is a pattern of institutional enablement.
