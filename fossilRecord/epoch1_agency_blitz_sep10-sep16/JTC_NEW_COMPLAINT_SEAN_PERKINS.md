## II. THE PERKINS LAW GROUP CONFLICT

### A. Todd Perkins and Judge Miller

In June 2024, TSA officers discovered a loaded, unregistered .380
caliber Smith & Wesson handgun in Judge Cylenthia Miller's purse
at the DTW security checkpoint. Judge Miller initially told police
the gun belonged to "her brother" — a statement her attorney,
**Todd Perkins**, later admitted was **false**.

Miller pleaded no contest on September 16, 2024, in 34th District
Court. She received 90 days probation and $1,400 in fines.

### B. The Evans Murder Dismissal

In December 2025, Judge Miller dismissed *People v. Charles Evans*
**with prejudice** — a felony murder and child abuse case involving
the death of a nine-month-old infant. The defense attorney was
affiliated with **Perkins Law Group PLLC** — the same firm
(operated by Todd Perkins) that had represented Judge Miller
personally just months earlier.

Neither Judge Miller nor defense counsel disclosed this prior
attorney-client relationship. Wayne County Prosecutor Kym Worthy
stated: "There is no such thing as dismissal with prejudice in
criminal law" and "In all of my years of practice I have never seen
a judge behave like this."

The appeal remains **pending**.

### C. Todd and Sean: Former Law Partners

Todd Perkins and Sean Perkins were **partners** at Perkins Law Group
PLLC before Sean's election to the bench. Todd Perkins' LinkedIn
shows the firm was established in January 1996 — spanning 30 years.
Sean Perkins was elected to the 36th District in November 2022.

When a lawyer's former law partner simultaneously:
- Represents a convicted felon (Banks)
- Represents a sitting judge on another bench (Miller)
- Appears before that same judge in a murder case
- Creates an undisclosed conflict that results in a murder dismissal

...the former partner who now sits as a judge on a related bench
has, at minimum, an appearance of impropriety that warrants
investigation.

## V. CANON VIOLATIONS

**Canon 1 — Integrity and Independence.** A judge whose brother
simultaneously represents a convicted felon, a sitting judge on
another bench, and creates undisclosed conflicts in felony murder
cases undermines public confidence in judicial integrity.

**Canon 2 — Impropriety and Appearance of Impropriety.** The
Perkins Law Group connection creates an appearance of impropriety.
Todd Perkins' representation of both Banks and Miller, combined
with the Evans murder dismissal, raises questions about the firm's
influence across multiple courts. Judge Sean Perkins' former
partnership at the same firm compounds this appearance.

**Canon 3 — Impartiality.** Should any matter involving Banks,
Banks' schools, the Perkins Law Group, Judge Miller, or network-
connected parties come before the 36th District Court, Judge Sean
Perkins' ability to act impartially would be compromised by his
brother's extensive involvement.

**Canon 3(C)(1) — Disqualification.** MCR 2.003 requires
disqualification where a judge's impartiality might reasonably be
questioned. Given the Perkins Law Group's documented conflicts,
Judge Sean Perkins should be examined for any failure to recuse in
matters involving his brother's clients or network-connected
parties.
