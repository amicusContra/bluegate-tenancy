## III. THE 20-YEAR ARC — IN BANKS' OWN WORDS

The following narrative is reconstructed entirely from Banks'
memoir, "It Had 2 Happen: The Agony of Success" (2020). Page
references cite the OCR transcript of the book.

### Phase 1: Sentencing (1999)

Banks appeared before Judge Langford Morris in Oakland County
Circuit Court on "multiple felony charges" (Banks' words). His
attorney was **Mr. Condino**. Banks describes the sentencing:

> "People of the State of Michigan versus Brian Banks," the
> court clerk announced my case. My attorney, Mr. Condino,
> was dressed in a navy blue suit, arose and I, dressed in a
> black suit, quickly followed him up to the podium.
— Ch. 5 (Revelation Day), p. 50-55

Banks received probation with six months on a tether. During
sentencing, Judge Langford Morris made statements Banks describes
as profoundly personal:

> The judge began to tell me that I was too intelligent to make
> the decisions that I'd been making. She shared that I needed
> to go back to school and get my GED and go on further with
> my education. The judge also told me that I needed to stop
> hanging with the wrong people. She said, "Look, you come from
> a good family. Your family has been here with you in court.
> You're well-dressed and well-spoken."
— Ch. 5, p. 94-103

> Even though it was only a two-minute conversation, I felt like
> the judge put everyone else out of the courtroom and was
> talking to me as if I was the only person in the room.
— Ch. 5, p. 104-106

**Canon Note:** While encouraging a defendant to pursue education
is commendable, the subsequent 20-year personal relationship
that grew from this sentencing reveals that this was not routine
judicial encouragement — it was the beginning of an improper
personal bond.

### Phase 2: The Encouragement to Attend Law School (~2001)

After Banks obtained his GED and filed a pro se motion to remove
his tether, Judge Langford Morris made a pivotal statement:

> "Now," she emphasized, "you need to get back in school and go
> to someone's law school!"
— Ch. 5, p. 160

**Canon Violation:** A sentencing judge encouraged a convicted
felon — who was still on probation before her — to attend law
school. She knew his criminal record. She knew that felony
convictions would create severe obstacles at Character and
Fitness review. She encouraged this trajectory regardless,
creating an expectation that her endorsement would carry weight
in the admissions process.

### Phase 3: The Lunch Meeting at Brandy's Restaurant (~2007)

Years later, Banks orchestrated a personal meeting with Judge
Langford Morris through an intermediary named "Roeiah":

> A few days later, Roeiah let me know that she and Judge
> Langford Morris had spoken and had a lunch date scheduled.
> Roeiah told me that she asked the judge if one of her friends
> could join them and the judge agreed. Roeiah said, "Brian,
> this is your opportunity to let the judge know everything
> that you've done."
— Ch. 5, pp. 143-148

Banks describes his hesitation — and his awareness that this
meeting was improper:

> I didn't know if it was proper for a prior defendant to show
> up and have a conversation with his sentencing judge, nor did
> I know if the judge would be nervous or scared of me.
— Ch. 5, pp. 149-150

**Banks himself recognized this was inappropriate.** He went
anyway.

The lunch took place at **Brandy's, a restaurant on Telegraph in
Pontiac**. During the meal:

### Phase 4: Judge Discusses Active Docket with Civilians

> Judge Langford Morris was describing her morning docket to
> Roeiah and the other young lady and the various cases that
> she had heard.
— Ch. 5, pp. 170-172

**Canon 3 Violation:** A sitting judge discussed her active
docket — specific cases she had heard that morning — with
civilians at a restaurant. This is a clear violation of the
duty to maintain confidentiality of court proceedings and to
avoid ex parte communications about pending matters.

### Phase 5: Banks Reveals Identity; Judge Returns Him to Courtroom

Banks then revealed himself to Judge Langford Morris:

> I interjected and said, "Judge, I think this is the perfect
> time for me to tell you, 'thank you'. Seven and a half years
> ago I stood before you as a lost, young, black male with
> multiple felony charges."
— Ch. 5, pp. 196-199

> Judge Langford Morris was totally shocked. She sat at the
> table right across from me with tears welling up in her eyes
> while I spoke. She told me that no litigant had ever come
> back with a story like mine. She explained how proud she was
> of me and all of my accomplishments.
— Ch. 5, pp. 180-184

Judge Langford Morris then **brought Banks back to her courtroom**:

> She indeed had the same staff and invited me to come back to
> the courthouse after lunch to see them. She said she couldn't
> wait to share with them my good news.
> When we arrived to Judge Langford Morris' courtroom, I was
> greeted by two of her staff members. One of them said, "You
> look familiar." Judge Langford Morris asked her staff if they
> remembered Brian Banks.
— Ch. 5, pp. 186-195

**Canon 2 Violation:** A sitting judge brought a former felony
defendant — who had appeared before her in criminal proceedings
— back to her courtroom to celebrate his accomplishments with
her staff. This is an extraordinary breach of the appearance of
impartiality.

### Phase 6: Law School Events and Ongoing Mentorship

> As the years went by, I made good on my promise and maintained
> contact with Judge Langford Morris. I began to invite her to
> several events, especially those events hosted by the Black
> law students at my law school. Judge Langford Morris graciously
> accepted the invitations and attended many of the events.
— Ch. 5, pp. 205-208

**Canon 2, 4 Violation:** A sitting judge regularly attended
events at the law school of her former defendant. Her presence
at these events would have been known to the law school
community, creating an implicit endorsement of Banks as a
favored former litigant.

### Phase 7: Graduation Dinner with Multiple Judges

> When I graduated from law school, Judge Langford Morris and
> several other judges and friends treated me to dinner to
> celebrate.
— Ch. 5, pp. 210-212

**Canon 2, 4 Violation:** Multiple sitting judges (unnamed)
participated in a private celebratory dinner for a convicted
felon's law school graduation. The fact that "several other
judges" attended suggests this was a coordinated social event
within the judicial community centered on a former criminal
defendant.

### Phase 8: Banks Volunteers on Judge's Supreme Court Campaign (2010)

> By this time, Judge Langford Morris was pursuing a run for
> the Michigan Supreme Court. I, without hesitation, volunteered
> to assist and work on her campaign. I knew a little bit about
> campaigning from my friend, Judge Vonda Evans, so I thought
> this would be a great opportunity for me to learn more and to
> meet new people.
— Ch. 5, pp. 213-217

**OSINT VERIFICATION:** Judge Langford Morris was the **Democratic
nominee** for the Michigan Supreme Court in the **November 2, 2010
general election.** She received **812,485 votes (17.28%)** but
lost to incumbent Justice Robert P. Young Jr. (1,310,986 votes,
27.88%). (Source: Wikipedia, 2010 Michigan elections; Ballotpedia;
MLive, October 2010.)

Banks graduated from MSU College of Law in **spring 2010** and
the campaign ran through **November 2010** — the timeline is
perfectly consistent with his claim of volunteering immediately
after graduation.

**Canon 5 Violation:** A sitting judge accepted volunteer labor
from a former defendant she had sentenced. Banks — a convicted
felon with no bar admission — was working on the judicial
campaign of the judge who sentenced him. This creates an
appearance that judicial favor is exchanged for political support.

**Additional concern:** At the time Banks volunteered (2010), he
had just graduated from law school and had NOT been admitted to
the bar. Judge Langford Morris knew he had felony convictions
that would complicate Character & Fitness review. By accepting
his campaign labor, she was embedding a person she knew might
never be licensed into her political and professional network.

### Phase 9: "Mentor/Mentee Relationship Which Still Exists"

> In all of my endeavors this judge had been the most supportive
> and a constant right role model. Volunteering on Judge Langford
> Morris's Supreme Court campaign afforded me a great opportunity,
> plus it allowed the judge and I to foster a great mentor/mentee
> relationship which still exists to this day.
— Ch. 5, pp. 218-222

**"This day" = 2020**, the date of publication. The relationship
spans from 1999 sentencing through at least 2020 — a 21-year
improper personal relationship between a judge and the felon she
sentenced.

### Phase 10: The Foreword — Endorsing "Brian Banks, J.D."

Judge Langford Morris wrote the **foreword** for Banks' memoir.
The book's title page, cover, copyright page, and biography all

By writing the foreword, Judge Langford Morris:

## V. CANON VIOLATIONS — CONSOLIDATED

### Canon 1 — Integrity and Independence of the Judiciary

A 21-year personal relationship with a former defendant who
became a convicted felon (8 additional felonies after law school)
undermines public confidence in the integrity of the judiciary.
Writing the foreword for "Brian Banks, J.D." — knowing he was
never admitted to the bar — is a direct assault on the
profession's credibility.

### Canon 2 — Avoiding Impropriety and the Appearance of Impropriety

| Incident | Impropriety |
|----------|------------|
| Personal lunch with former defendant at Brandy's restaurant | Former defendant/sentencing judge socializing |
| Returned Banks to her courtroom to celebrate with staff | Turned courtroom into venue for personal celebration |
| Attended law school events at Banks' invitation | Regular social contact with former defendant |
| Participated in graduation dinner | Private social event with former defendant |
| Accepted campaign volunteer labor from Banks | Political benefit from former defendant |
| Wrote foreword for "Brian Banks, J.D." | Endorsed credential fraud in commercial publication |
| Maintained "mentor/mentee relationship" for 21+ years | Ongoing improper personal bond |

### Canon 3 — Performing Duties Impartially

Judge Langford Morris discussed her **active docket** with
civilians at a restaurant lunch. She described specific cases
she had heard that morning. This violates the duty to maintain
impartiality and the confidentiality of court proceedings.

If Banks, Holland (Banks' "brother"), or any network member ever
appeared before her or in her court system during this period,
the mentor/mentee relationship would have required recusal. There
is no evidence she ever disclosed this relationship.

### Canon 4 — Extrajudicial Activities

Regular attendance at Banks' law school events, the graduation
dinner, and the foreword authorship are all extrajudicial
activities that cast doubt on the judge's impartiality.

### Canon 5 — Political Activity

Accepting campaign volunteer labor from a former felony defendant
violates the restrictions on political activity. Banks' volunteer
work on Judge Langford Morris's Michigan Supreme Court campaign
created a quid pro quo appearance: judicial leniency exchanged
for political support.
