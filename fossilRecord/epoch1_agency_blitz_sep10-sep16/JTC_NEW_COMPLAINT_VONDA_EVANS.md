## III. JUDGE EVANS IN BANKS' OWN WORDS

### A. Named as "Mentor" — The Acknowledgments Page

Banks names Judge Evans as one of four mentors:

> "To my mentors — Judge Denise Langford Morris, Retired Judge

— Acknowledgments, p. 4

Judge Evans is listed alongside the sentencing judge who began
the 20-year relationship (Langford Morris), the current Board

four mentors form the core of the judicial-political network
that enabled Banks' trajectory.

### B. Present at Grandfather's Deathbed

Banks describes the death of his grandfather in Chapter 9.
Two sitting judges — Evans and Miller — rushed to the hospital:

> "My mother, Justin, and I were placed in a family waiting
> room. Shortly afterwards, my cousin, Ingrid, arrived along
> with my friends Keith, Judge Vonda Evans and Judge Cylenthia
> LaToye Miller. We were all waiting and praying. I was
> expecting God to stabilize my grandfather and keep him here."

After the grandfather was pronounced dead:

> "My friends tried to comfort me, but they really couldn't.
> I eventually landed on the floor, bawling. Cylenthia got
> down on the floor with me, hugged me."
— Ch. 9, pp. 138-140

**Two sitting judges** responded to a personal family medical
crisis of a convicted felon. They were in the hospital waiting
room with his family. They were among the first people called.
This is not a professional relationship — it is a deeply
personal bond that placed sitting judges in the most intimate
family moments of a man they knew to be a convicted felon.

## V. CANON VIOLATIONS

### Canon 1 — Integrity and Independence

A retired judge who mentored, supported, visited, comforted,
and endorsed a convicted felon over a 15+ year period —
including visiting him the night before his arrest, attending
his graduation, and endorsing his candidacy for public office
— undermines public confidence in the integrity of the judiciary.

The relationship was not incidental. Banks names Evans as one
of four "mentors" alongside two other judges and a state senator.
This was a deliberate, sustained personal investment in a
convicted felon by a sitting judge.

### Canon 2 — Impropriety and Appearance of Impropriety

| Incident | Impropriety |
|----------|------------|
| Present at grandfather's deathbed with Miller | Two sitting judges at family crisis of convicted felon |
| Visited Banks night before AG surrender | Judge providing comfort to criminal suspect |
| Attended law school graduation dinner | Judge celebrating felon's J.D. acquisition |
| Endorsed Banks for Commissioner (2022) | Judge endorsing convicted felon for office while he uses "J.D." |
| Named as "mentor" in Banks' "J.D." book | Judge's name in a book that constitutes credential fraud |

Each incident alone would merit scrutiny. Together, they
establish a sustained pattern of improper personal involvement.

### Canon 3 — Impartiality

Judge Evans maintained a deep personal relationship with Banks
throughout her tenure on the bench. If Banks, Joseph Holland Jr.
(Banks' "brother"), or any network-connected individual ever
appeared before her or in her court, the relationship would
have required recusal. There is no evidence she disclosed this
relationship.

Additionally, Judge Evans' presence the night before Banks'
surrender suggests she may have been aware of the AG
investigation before it became public — raising questions about
whether she used or shared nonpublic information.

### Canon 4 — Extrajudicial Activities

A sitting judge attending the law school graduation of a
convicted felon, visiting a criminal suspect the night before
his surrender, and endorsing that person for public office are
extrajudicial activities that cast doubt on judicial impartiality
and demean the judicial office.

### Canon 5 — Political Activity

The 2022 endorsement of Banks for Wayne County Commissioner —
knowing his full criminal history and credential fraud —
violates the restrictions on political activity by judges and
former judges.
