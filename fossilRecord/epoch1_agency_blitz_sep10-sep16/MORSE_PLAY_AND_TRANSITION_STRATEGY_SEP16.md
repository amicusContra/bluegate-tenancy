### THE KEY DISTINCTION:

**"Here's what I've found" = safe.**
**"Here's what I plan to do with it" = hold.**

The credential collapse letter already strikes the right balance:
it shares facts and invites recipients to draw their own
conclusions. It doesn't tell Morse to sue anyone. It doesn't
tell Pahara what to do. It presents information and says
"assess your own obligations."

### SHOULD KEVIN SHARE THE FILED COMPLAINTS WITH MORSE?

**Yes — but not proactively.** The letter says:

> "I am available to share documentation, including:
> - Filed civil complaints (public record, Ingham County)"

This means: if Morse asks, Kevin provides. Kevin doesn't attach
the complaints to the letter. Why?

1. **If Kevin sends the complaints unsolicited,** it looks like
   Kevin is recruiting Morse as an ally. That changes the power
   dynamic — suddenly Morse is doing Kevin a favor.

2. **If Morse REQUESTS the complaints,** it means Morse sees
   the opportunity independently. Now Morse is acting in his
   own interest, which is vastly more powerful.

3. **The complaint language is compelling** — it tells a story
   that any PI attorney would recognize. But it's more powerful
   when the attorney asks to see it than when Kevin pushes it.

**Exception:** If Elrick specifically suggests that Kevin share
the complaints with Morse, then do it — Elrick's judgment on
Detroit power dynamics is better than anyone's.
