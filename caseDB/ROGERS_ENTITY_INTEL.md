## The CISPA Problem

Rogers authored **CISPA** (Cyber Intelligence Sharing and Protection Act, H.R. 3523/H.R. 624). What it would do:

- Create an **exemption to all privacy laws** for companies sharing data with government
- Allow sharing of **email content, browsing history, PII** without user consent
- Grant **complete liability immunity** to companies that share — no accountability for mishandling
- Allow data to be used for **any lawful purpose** as long as cybersecurity or national security is "a significant purpose"
- Give the **NSA/DoD** access without adequate oversight
- Permanent program with only annual IG review

**Opposition:**
- ACLU: "fatally flawed" — "creates a cybersecurity loophole to existing privacy laws"
- EFF, Reporters Without Borders, American Library Association
- Obama White House threatened veto
- 41 civil liberties organizations
- House passed it twice (2012, 2013). Senate killed it both times.

**Rogers' response to privacy concerns:** "We feel that the bill clearly deals with privacy, that the checks and balances are there, but [we] know there's still a perception and we're still trying to deal with that." (The Hill)

**The ACLU's response:** "Everyone, from the privacy community to the president, agrees that CISPA is bad on privacy — the problem isn't our perception."

**Connection to enforcement ecosystem:** The same government apparatus that can't coordinate UPL enforcement across two offices (State Bar + AGC) wanted Rogers-level surveillance authority over all Americans' private communications.
