## LEGAL NOTES ON SHARING DISCOVERY

**Can Kevin share subpoenaed materials with Elrick?**

**YES** — with one caveat.

| Scenario | Can Kevin share? |
|----------|-----------------|
| Discovery materials Kevin receives (not filed with court) | **YES** — they belong to Kevin as a litigant. No Michigan rule prohibits a party from sharing their own discovery with third parties. |
| Documents filed with the court (exhibits, motions) | **YES** — they become public court records automatically (MCR 8.119). Anyone can access them. |
| Documents under a protective order | **NO** — if the court enters a protective order (MCR 2.302(C)), Kevin must comply. |

**Can defendants get a protective order?**

They can try. MCR 2.302(C) requires "good cause" — typically
trade secrets, highly personal information, or competitive harm.

**Kevin's arguments against a protective order:**

1. These entities receive **$6 million/year in public education
   funding** — the public has a right to know how tax dollars
   are spent
2. Banks has **8 felony convictions for financial fraud** — his
   financial records are directly relevant to whether public
   funds are being mismanaged
3. Banks' administrator certificate explicitly **prohibits him
   from handling cash** — financial records are needed to monitor
   compliance with a state-imposed condition
4. The Purpose entities are structured as **nonprofits and
   501(c)(3) organizations** — they have reduced privacy
   expectations compared to private businesses
5. Banks is a **former elected official** — public figures have
   reduced privacy expectations
6. No trade secrets exist in the management of a charter school

**Bottom line:** Kevin subpoenas the records. Kevin analyzes them.
