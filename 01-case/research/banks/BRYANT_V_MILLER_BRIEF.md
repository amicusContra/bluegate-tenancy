## THE CORE THEORY: "Five-Link Structural Financial Conflict"

Bryant alleges Wayne County Circuit Court operates within a financial architecture where criminal convictions directly fund the courthouse itself:

### Link 1: Revenue Generation
Court imposes fines, fees, costs, and restitution under MCL 769.1k. The Third Judicial Circuit Court's own 2024 Annual Report confirms **"nearly $1.7 million was collected in court costs and late fees in the Criminal Division"** in FY 2024 — described as **"one of the top priorities of the Court."**

### Link 2: GASB 34 Receivable Booking
The moment a judge imposes financial assessments, they are booked as **"Accounts Receivable"** on Wayne County's balance sheet — a county asset. Wayne County ACFR discloses **$69,651,127** in "Deferred inflows — unavailable receivables."

### Link 3: General Fund Revenue
Receivables flow into the Wayne County General Fund, which generated **$147,104,334** in "Charges for Services" in the most recent fiscal year.

### Link 4: Bond Debt Service
The General Fund services **$121,779,583** in annual debt on the **$200M Wayne County Building Authority bonds (Series 2010)** through "Cash Rentals" that are **"irrevocably pledged"** and **"not subject to setoff or abatement for any cause."** The $143.33M Term Bond at 10.00% matures Dec 1, 2040 — **mandatory sinking fund redemptions begin December 1, 2026.**

### Link 5: Courthouse Funding
Those bonds funded the **Criminal Justice Center**, which opened September 2024. **Miller presides in this bond-funded facility.**

### The Closed Loop (¶39):
> "Every guilty plea generates mandatory MCL 769.1k fees → fees flow to the Wayne County General Fund → the General Fund simultaneously pays Schulman's salary AND services the $200M bond debt → creating a structural alignment of financial interests among judge, prosecutor, and defense counsel."

**67.2% guilty plea rate** — 6,137 of 9,137 dispositions in FY 2024.

## THE STATE'S OWN ADMISSION

The **Michigan Judicial Council 2025 Task Force Report on Trial Court Funding** (Exhibit 5):
- 26.2% of trial court administration funding comes from criminal defendant assessments
- 58% of court costs borne by local governments whose General Funds receive that revenue
- **Explicitly stated** the current system creates **"perverse incentives"** and a **"real or perceived conflict of interest between the judge's duty to impartiality and the obligation to generate revenue"**
- Recommended centralized statewide court funding model — **not implemented**
