## THE CONSTITUTIONAL ARGUMENT

### It's the 14th Amendment, Not the 4th — But Both Apply

**14th Amendment — Due Process (Primary):**

The Supreme Court recognizes parental rights as a **fundamental liberty interest** protected by the Due Process Clause. *Troxel v. Granville*, 530 U.S. 57 (2000); *Santosky v. Kramer*, 455 U.S. 745 (1982); *Stanley v. Illinois*, 405 U.S. 645 (1972).

Under *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976), procedural due process requires a balancing of:

| Factor | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **(1) Private interest affected** | DaSean's fundamental parental rights — the "oldest of the fundamental liberty interests recognized by this Court." *Troxel*, 530 U.S. at 65. He has not seen his children meaningfully since Sep 2. No hearing on custody has occurred in 70 days. |
| **(2) Risk of erroneous deprivation** | EXTREME. Children are enrolled without consent at a school run by a 9-time convicted felon (Banks) who employs a drug trafficker (Holland, MDOC #443789). No GAL appointed despite 33-day-old motion. Emergency motion filed by BOTH parties — neither heard. The status quo the court is preserving IS the deprivation. |
| **(3) Government interest** | Wayne County's interest is administrative efficiency — managing its caseload. But the government's interest in protecting children cuts the OTHER way. Delay serves the status quo, and the status quo places children in documented danger. |

**The argument:** Wayne County's systemic delay is not merely inconvenient — it is an **ongoing deprivation of a fundamental right without adequate process.** Every day the case sits without a hearing, DaSean's parental rights are being infringed by operation of the court's inaction.

**4th Amendment — Seizure of Children:**

Federal courts have recognized that removing children from a parent's custody constitutes a "seizure" under the 4th Amendment. *Doe v. Heck*, 327 F.3d 492 (7th Cir. 2003); *Tenenbaum v. Williams*, 193 F.3d 581 (2nd Cir. 1999).

The seizure here:
- Sep 2, 2026: Leon physically assaulted DaSean; Shayla entered and removed both children
- Children have not been returned
- No court order authorized the removal — it was private violence, then ratified by filing an emergency motion in a court that hasn't acted

The state becomes complicit in the seizure when it:
1. Fails to hold a hearing within a reasonable time after the seizure
2. Allows the status quo to persist without judicial review
3. Places the children in a forum where the father cannot effectively be heard

This is not the typical CPS-removal 4th Amendment case. But the principle applies: **the children were seized, and the state has not provided a prompt hearing to adjudicate the seizure.** Nineteen days after an emergency motion, sixty-plus days of a custody system that processes nothing — this is not due process.
