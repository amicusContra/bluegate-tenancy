## WHAT "AGGRESSIVE LITIGATION" ACTUALLY MEANS

Courts have defined the line. It's not about speed. It's not about
filing a lot. It's about *how* and *why*.

### AGGRESSIVE (bad — judges punish this)

Courts identify "aggressive litigation" as conduct that is:

- **Frivolous** — filing claims with no factual basis, just to
  harass or drain the other side's money
- **Intransigent** — refusing to comply with court orders, hiding
  documents, ignoring discovery, making the process harder than
  it needs to be
- **Vexatious** — filing the same claim over and over after losing,
  or filing motions purely to delay
- **Alienating** — using litigation to poison the children against
  the other parent, or portraying the other parent in the worst
  possible light to anyone who will listen

In *Conley v. Rugh* (WA 2020), a court drew the line explicitly:
"There is a line between aggressive litigation and intransigence,
and respondent crossed it." The father in that case filed 239
interrogatories, refused to answer the GAL's questions, and ran up
$300K in legal fees over a parenting plan. THAT is aggressive.

In *Sangji v. Bendapudi* (MI COA 2024), the Michigan Court of
Appeals noted that both parties "engaged in aggressive litigation
tactics that were not helpful to the court." The court's remedy:
each party bears their own fees. The key phrase: **"not helpful
to the court."**

Under MCL 722.23(j) — Factor (j) — Michigan courts look at whether
a parent is willing to "facilitate and encourage a close and
continuing parent-child relationship." Filing complaints with
agencies is not automatically a failure of Factor (j). Filing
*frivolous* complaints to harass — that is.

### FAST (neutral or positive — judges respect this)

Speed is not aggression. A litigant who:

- Files on time
- Serves properly
- Responds to deadlines
- Organizes evidence clearly
- Makes the court's job easier

...is not aggressive. They are **prepared**. Judges appreciate
prepared litigants because they move the docket forward.

### DEFENSIVE / PROTECTIVE (positive — judges reward this)

A parent who:

- Reports safety concerns to appropriate agencies
- Documents violations of parental rights
- Files complaints with oversight bodies (State Bar, MDE, JTC)
- Seeks emergency custody when children are at risk
- Researches schools and presents alternatives

...is not aggressive. They are **protecting their children**.

MCL 722.23(j) explicitly carves out protection: *"A court may
not consider negatively for the purposes of this factor any
reasonable action taken by a parent to protect a child."*

### Tactic 2: Cross-Implication (Why It's Expensive to Defend)

This is the part the AI won't tell you, because it's strategy.

through overlapping facts. That creates **conflicts of interest**

| Defendants | Why they can't share a lawyer |
|------------|------------------------------|
| Shayla vs. Leon/Marquita | Marquita's best defense on mail fraud is "Shayla told me to." Leon's best defense is "I wasn't involved." Both throw Shayla under the bus. |
| Shayla vs. Simone | Simone served DaSean on Father's Day. If she testifies truthfully, she helps DaSean, not Shayla. Her interests diverge. |
| Leon vs. Marquita | Leon is a USPS employee. Marquita committed mail fraud at his workplace. His defense may require distancing from her. |
| Banks vs. Shayla | Banks and Shayla are co-defendants on the conspiracy count. Banks will say "I didn't know about the custody case." Shayla will say "Banks acted on his own." Each blames the other. |
| Banks vs. Holland | If the amended complaint adds Holland, Banks and Holland can't share counsel — Banks will claim Holland acted independently, Holland will claim Banks directed everything. |
| Banks vs. Purpose entities | Banks personally AND his LLC AND his school are all defendants. His personal defense may conflict with the entities' defense (especially if he self-dealt). |

**Result:** Each defendant needs their OWN lawyer. A lawyer who
represents two co-defendants with conflicting interests commits
an ethics violation (MRPC 1.7 — conflict of interest). Any
competent attorney will identify the conflict and refuse joint
representation.

**The math:**
- 4 Smith defendants × $5K-$15K retainer each = $20K-$60K
- Banks (individual) needs separate counsel from Purpose Group
  and PCA = 2-3 more retainers
- Holland (if added) = another retainer
- Total defense cost across all parties: **$60K-$150K+**

Kevin's filing cost: **~$500** (filing fees + service).

This is not aggressive. This is **asymmetric litigation** — the
same strategy used by every plaintiffs' firm in America. The
plaintiff's costs are low (facts + filing fees). The defendants'
costs are high (because each one needs a separate lawyer to protect
their individual interests against the other defendants).

Kevin didn't create the conflicts. The defendants created them by
committing overlapping acts together. Kevin just filed accurately.

### Tactic 3: Interrogatories That Create Records

When a filing alleges something the other side disputes — that's
by design. Every allegation forces one of three responses:

| Response | What it means |
|----------|---------------|
| **Admit** | It's on the record. Can't take it back. |
| **Deny** | Now we have evidence they lied (if we can prove the allegation). That's perjury. |
| **Ignore** | Under MCR 2.312, requests for admissions are **deemed admitted** if not answered within 28 days. Silence = confession. |

So when Kevin alleges that Shayla enrolled the girls without
telling DaSean — she has to respond. If she admits it, it's
Factor (j) evidence. If she denies it, we have the text messages
proving she never told him. If she ignores it, it's automatically
admitted.

There is no safe answer. That's not aggressive — that's what
litigation is. The other side doesn't get to choose which facts
exist.
