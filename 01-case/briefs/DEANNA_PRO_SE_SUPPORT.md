# Deanna Pro Se Support Plan

**Purpose:** Deanna files pro se. Kevin and DaSean provide support.
The Smiths' greatest fear is Deanna on the stand. The case is
designed for a fast settlement to put money in her hands NOW,
while Kevin's non-settling case grinds on.

---

## THE LEGAL LINE — What Kevin and DaSean CAN do

Helping a pro se litigant prepare their case is NOT unauthorized
practice of law in Michigan. The line is:

| ALLOWED | NOT ALLOWED |
|---------|-------------|
| Help Deanna draft her own documents | Sign documents for her |
| Explain court procedures to her | Appear in court on her behalf |
| Organize exhibits and evidence | Give legal advice for compensation |
| Prepare her for testimony | Represent her at hearings |
| Sit in the gallery during hearings | Speak for her to the judge |
| Drive her to court | Negotiate on her behalf without her present |
| Research law and share findings | Hold out as her attorney |
| Help her set up MiFILE account | File documents in her name without her knowledge |

**Kevin's role:** Document preparer, evidence organizer, strategic
advisor. The complaint was drafted with Deanna's facts, in
Deanna's voice, for Deanna's claims. She reviews it, confirms
it's accurate, signs it, and files it. Kevin helped prepare it
the same way a paralegal helps an attorney — except Deanna is
her own attorney.

**DaSean's role:** Fact witness. He was there. He saw the
childcare. He saw the EBT use. He saw the Father's Day ambush.
He can testify to everything. He's also the link between the
cases — his custody case generates evidence that feeds Deanna's.

---

## DEANNA ON THE STAND — Why the Smiths Should Be Terrified

Picture this for the jury:

A grandmother. Section 8 housing. Fixed income. EBT card to
feed herself. She opened her home for YEARS to her grandchildren,
to her son's partner's children, to neighborhood kids. She fed
them with her own food stamps. She cared for them while the
Smith family — a USPS carrier, a bank employee, a homeowner —
built careers, built equity, built wealth.

And then:

- The bank employee **stole her food stamps** and submitted the
  receipts to court as her own exhibits
- The bank employee's mother **intercepted legal mail** at a
  federal post office
- The bank employee's family called her son **"Sean"** — a name
  his mother never gave him
- The bank employee's sister **served her son with a lawsuit on
  Father's Day** while the matriarch watched from inside the house
- The bank employee's family **never paid Deanna one dollar**

Now the attorney for the Smiths has to cross-examine this
grandmother. What do they ask her?

- "Didn't you WANT to watch your grandchildren?" → Yes, and you
  should have paid her for it. Wanting to see your grandchildren
  doesn't make slave labor voluntary.
- "Wasn't the childcare informal?" → Informal doesn't mean free.
  Market rate is $182/week per child. For years.
- "Did you ever ASK for compensation?" → She's a grandmother in
  Section 8 housing. They're a family with a federal salary and
  a bank career. The power dynamic makes asking impossible.

**There is no good cross-examination of Deanna Mitchell.** Every
question makes the Smiths look worse. Every answer makes the
jury angrier. The Smiths' attorney knows this. That's why they
settle.

## THE SETTLEMENT PLAY

### Timing

1. **Aug 27:** Kevin files in Ingham. The Smiths start panicking.
2. **Sep 1:** Kevin's suit is served at Dresden. The Smiths now
   know this is real. They start calling attorneys.
3. **Sep 8-12:** Deanna files in Wayne. The Smiths get a SECOND
   suit. Different plaintiff, different court, different claims.
4. **Sep 12-15:** Deanna's suit is served at Dresden. Now the
   Smiths are defending TWO lawsuits in TWO counties.
5. **Sep 15-22:** The Smiths' attorney calls. "Can we settle the
   grandmother's case?" YES. That's the point.

### The offer

Deanna's attorney (Deanna, pro se, with Kevin's coaching) offers:

> "I will dismiss my case with prejudice for $35,000, payable
> within 30 days. This resolves all of Deanna Mitchell's claims
> against all defendants. It does not resolve, affect, or address
> any other pending or future litigation by any other party."

**Why the Smiths take it:**
- $35,000 is less than hiring attorneys for two courts ($30K-$80K)
- It removes one front (they think)
- They avoid Deanna on the stand
- They avoid discovery into EBT records, childcare hours, household finances

**Why $35,000 is the number:**
- It's enough to transform Deanna's life (a year+ of financial stability)
- It's low enough that the Smiths can scrape it together
- It's high enough that it constitutes an admission of liability
  in Kevin's case (paid $35K to the grandmother they exploited)
- It lands in the range where their attorney says "just pay it"

### What settlement does NOT do for the Smiths:
- Kevin's case continues (he won't settle, ever)
- Children's case continues (separate plaintiff)
- Custody case continues (separate proceeding)
- OIG investigation continues (federal)
- MDHHS investigation continues (state)

They pay $35,000 and still face five other fronts. But Deanna
has her money. That's the goal.

## PREP STEPS — Before Filing

### 1. Deanna reviews the complaint
Kevin reads it to her. She confirms every fact. She corrects
anything that isn't right. This is HER story, in HER voice.

### 2. Deanna signs the complaint
Physical signature, scanned. Or electronic signature via MiFILE.

### 3. Kevin helps set up MiFILE
Create account, walk through the interface, upload complaint.
Deanna clicks "submit." Deanna pays (or files fee waiver).

### 4. DaSean prepares a witness statement
Written account of what he saw: Deanna's childcare, the EBT
use, the Father's Day ambush, the lack of compensation. This
becomes Deanna's first exhibit if discovery opens.

### 5. Kevin prepares Deanna for questions
If the case doesn't settle and goes to deposition or trial:
- What did you do for the children?
- How often?
- Were you paid?
- Do you know about the food stamps?
- How do you feel about what happened?

Simple, honest, devastating questions with simple, honest,
devastating answers.
