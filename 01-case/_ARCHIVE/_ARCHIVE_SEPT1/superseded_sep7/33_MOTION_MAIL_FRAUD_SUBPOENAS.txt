BASIS

1. MCR 2.305(A)(1) provides that an unrepresented party may
move the court for issuance of non-party discovery subpoenas.

2. Defendant has previously filed three motions for non-party
discovery subpoenas: (1) individual non-parties, (2) institutional
record holders, and (3) financial institutions. This third
supplemental motion is necessitated by NEW EVIDENCE discovered
on August 25, 2026, after the previous motions were prepared.

3. On August 25, 2026, Defendant's designated representative
Kevin Mok received return receipt cards (PS Form 3811) for five
pieces of Certified Mail Restricted Delivery sent on August 18,
2026, to members of Plaintiff's family. The return receipts
reveal that Plaintiff's mother, Marquita L. Smith, signed for
and collected Restricted Delivery mail addressed to THREE
different family members — Plaintiff Shayla Smith (x2) and
non-party Leon A. Smith — at the Harper Woods, Michigan post
office on August 21, 2026.

4. Under USPS Restricted Delivery service, ONLY the named
addressee or a formally authorized agent (via PS Form 3801 or
written letter to the Postmaster) may sign for and receive the
mail piece. Marquita L. Smith signed under the addressees'
names — not her own — and no authorization documents are
believed to exist.

5. On August 25, 2026, Defendant attempted to contact Leon A.
Smith about this matter. Leon A. Smith gave his phone to
Plaintiff Shayla Smith, who responded through Leon's device:
"Listen very carefully, for as long as an adult sign for it,
it's legal. When no one is home to sign, you can use an
authorized person to sign, otherwise no one will receive it.
You guys are not that smart, but continue, God don't like ugly."

6. Plaintiff's statement is factually false as a matter of
federal postal regulation. Her response (a) admits knowledge
of the mail interception, (b) confirms no formal authorization
exists, (c) demonstrates that she controls communications
through Leon A. Smith's device, and (d) constitutes intimidation
directed at Defendant and his representative during active
contested litigation.

7. This evidence is directly relevant to the custody case
because it demonstrates: (a) a pattern of communication
interception and control by Plaintiff and her family, (b) the
suppression of legal correspondence intended to facilitate
resolution of this custody dispute, (c) Plaintiff's willingness
to provide false legal information to Defendant, and (d) the
inability of Defendant to communicate with Plaintiff's family
without Plaintiff's interference — directly relevant to
MCL 722.23(j).
