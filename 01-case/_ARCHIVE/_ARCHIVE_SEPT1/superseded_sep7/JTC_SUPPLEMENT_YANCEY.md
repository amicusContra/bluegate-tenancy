**Source:** TransparencyUSA — Committee to Elect Tenisha Yancey

Banks Strategy and Consultants LLC (Michigan LARA ID 802070120) is

Representatives following a guilty plea for loan fraud. In the 2022
cycle, Banks Strategy & Consultants received **$0 from any other
client** — Judge Yancey was Banks' **only** consulting customer
that year.

Across all election cycles, Yancey's campaign paid Banks Strategy &
Consultants a total of **$15,750**.

The sequence of events is not coincidental:

1. Banks resigned in disgrace (February 2017) after a guilty plea
   for loan fraud
2. Banks formed Banks Strategy & Consultants LLC eight days later
3. Yancey won Banks' vacated seat in Michigan House District 1
   (November 2017)
4. Yancey paid Banks' consulting firm $15,366 from her judicial
   campaign (2022)
5. Yancey now chairs the board of Banks' school (MacDowell
   Preparatory Academy)

A sitting judge paid nearly half her campaign budget to a convicted
felon's consulting firm, then assumed a governance role at his
school. This conduct, standing alone, implicates Canon 2 and Canon 4
on their face.

**Source:** TransparencyUSA campaign finance data; Michigan LARA

## III. RESIDENCY FRAUD QUESTION — UNRESOLVED ELIGIBILITY

In 2022, plaintiff Leigh Reed-Pratt filed **COC 22-000060-MM** in
the Michigan Court of Claims challenging Judge Yancey's eligibility
to serve on the 36th District Court. Reed-Pratt presented evidence
that:

- Yancey claimed a **Principal Residence Exemption (PRE)** on a home
  in Harper Woods
- Yancey filed a **Detroit address** on her Affidavit of Identity
  for the 36th District Court race
- **36th District Court jurisdiction covers the City of Detroit
  ONLY**
- The Michigan Department of Education listing shows Yancey's
  **"City of Residence: Harper Woods"**

The Court of Claims allowed Yancey to proceed on **procedural
grounds** — the plaintiff did not establish a "clear legal right" to
relief. The **underlying factual question of Judge Yancey's actual
residence was never resolved**.

If Judge Yancey's primary residence is Harper Woods — as her PRE
filing tells the State of Michigan — she is ineligible for her seat
on the 36th District Court. This mirrors the document-fraud pattern
that led to Banks' own felony convictions: filing statements with the
state that misrepresent one's actual circumstances.

**Source:** COC 22-000060-MM (Michigan Court of Claims); Michigan
Department of Education public listing; Wayne County assessor records.

## VII. UPDATED CANON VIOLATIONS

The supplemental evidence supports the following violations of the
Michigan Code of Judicial Conduct, in addition to those alleged in
the original complaint:

**Canon 1 — Upholding the Integrity and Independence of the
Judiciary.** A judge with her own felony convictions governing a
school run by an eight-time convicted felon — financed by campaign
funds routed through a closed political network — undermines public
confidence in the integrity and independence of the judiciary.

**Canon 2 — Avoiding Impropriety and the Appearance of
Impropriety.** The $15,366 payment alone creates an appearance of
impropriety: a judge paying a convicted felon nearly half her
campaign budget, then chairing his school board. The unresolved
residency question — filing a Detroit address for judicial
eligibility while claiming a Harper Woods residence for tax purposes
— compounds this concern.

**Canon 4 — Conducting Extrajudicial Activities to Minimize Risk
of Conflict with Judicial Obligations.** Judge Yancey's extrajudicial
board service at MacDowell Preparatory Academy — a school where the
superintendent pays himself in gift cards and employs a convicted
drug trafficker as financial officer — casts reasonable doubt on her
capacity to act impartially. Her personal and financial relationship
with Banks predates and permeates her judicial service.
