## I. FEDERAL CIVIL RIGHTS LAWSUIT: BRYANT v. MILLER

A federal civil rights action has been filed against Judge Miller
and Wayne County officials:

**Bryant v. Miller et al**, Case No. 2:26-cv-10917-LVP-EAS
U.S. District Court, E.D. Michigan (Southern Division)
Filed: March 19, 2026
Presiding: Hon. Linda V. Parker
Magistrate: Elizabeth A. Stafford

Lead Plaintiff's Counsel: **Thomas Nolan, Butzel Long**
150 W. Jefferson, Suite 100, Detroit, MI 48226

The complaint (Doc 30, Amended Complaint filed August 17, 2026)
alleges a "Five-Link Structural Financial Conflict" within the
Third Judicial Circuit Court:

1. **Revenue Generation:** The court collected nearly $1.7 million
   in criminal court costs and late fees in FY 2024, described in
   the court's own annual report as "one of the top priorities of
   the Court."

2. **GASB 34 Receivables:** Criminal financial assessments are
   booked as accounts receivable — county assets. Wayne County
   ACFR discloses $69.6 million in "Deferred inflows — unavailable
   receivables."

3. **General Fund Revenue:** Receivables flow into the Wayne County
   General Fund ($147.1 million in "Charges for Services").

4. **Bond Debt Service:** The General Fund services $121.8 million
   in annual debt on $200M Wayne County Building Authority bonds
   (Series 2010). The $143.3M Term Bond at 10.00% matures December
   1, 2040. Mandatory sinking fund redemptions begin December 1,
   2026.

5. **Courthouse Funding:** Those bonds funded the Criminal Justice
   Center that opened September 2024 — where Judge Miller now
   presides.

In short: the judge who generates the revenue presides in the
building that revenue financed.

Judge Miller was named as a defendant but was terminated on
May 11, 2026 (likely on judicial immunity grounds). The systemic
claims against the State Court Administrator and the Chief Justice
remain active. The Amended Complaint was filed August 17, 2026.

**Relevance to JTC:** The Bryant litigation establishes that Judge
Miller's conduct in the Criminal Division — including the *People
v. Charles Evans* murder dismissal documented in my September 2
supplement — occurs within a financial system where the same judge
benefits financially from the convictions she processes. This is
not a peripheral policy dispute. A sitting federal case is
litigating the structural conflict of interest under which Judge
Miller operates daily.

**Source:** PACER, Case 2:26-cv-10917-LVP-EAS, Docs 1, 1-1, 1-2,
30 (all attached).
