## II. FULL ETHICS TIMELINE — THREE INCIDENTS IN TWENTY-SIX MONTHS

Since filing the original complaint, I have documented three separate
judicial ethics incidents occurring between June 2024 and June 2026,
each escalating in severity.

### Incident 1: Loaded Gun at Detroit Metro Airport (June 8, 2024)

On June 8, 2024, Transportation Security Administration officers at
Detroit Metropolitan Airport discovered a loaded, unregistered .380
caliber Smith & Wesson handgun in Judge Miller's purse at the security
checkpoint. The firearm was "not artfully concealed," with a round
chambered.

Judge Miller told police the gun belonged to "her brother" in Ohio.
Her attorney, Todd Perkins of Perkins Law Group, later admitted this
statement was **false** — the gun did not belong to a relative.

Judge Miller pleaded no contest on September 16, 2024, in the 34th
District Court. She received ninety days probation and was ordered to
pay $1,400 in fines and costs. Wayne County Prosecutor Kym Worthy
recused her office from the prosecution due to a conflict of interest;
the case was referred to the Monroe County Prosecutor's Office.

**Sources:** WXYZ-TV Detroit; FOX 2 Detroit; MLive; Belleville
Independent.

### Incident 2: Dismissed Nine-Month-Old Murder Case (December 8, 2025)

In *People v. Charles Evans*, the defendant was charged with Felony
Murder and Child Abuse First Degree in connection with the death of
his nine-month-old daughter, Kinsley. On December 8, 2025, Judge
Miller dismissed the case **with prejudice**, citing a discovery
violation regarding body camera footage.

Wayne County Prosecutor Kym Worthy stated: "There is no such thing
as dismissal with prejudice in criminal law." She further stated:
"In all of my years of practice I have never seen a judge behave
like this." The Wayne County Prosecutor's Office filed an appeal,
which remains **pending** as of September 2026.

**Hidden conflict of interest:** The defendant's attorney was
affiliated with **Perkins Law Group PLLC** — the same firm (attorney
Todd Perkins) that represented Judge Miller in her 2024 airport gun
case. Neither Judge Miller nor defense counsel disclosed this prior
attorney-client relationship to the prosecution or the court record.

**Sources:** WXYZ-TV Detroit; Detroit Free Press; Wayne County
Prosecutor's Office press release.

### Incident 3: Improper Court Appearance for OWI Case (June 2026)

In June 2026, Judge Miller appeared virtually in the East Lansing
54-B District Court on behalf of a former ward facing Operating While
Impaired and related charges. Judge Miller described the individual
as "like my daughter" and claimed she was appearing in her capacity
as an attorney for a "limited purpose."

**Judge Molly Hennessey Greenwalt** stopped the appearance and
characterized it as **"WHOLLY IMPROPER."** Judge Greenwalt stated
that the appearance "could raise the appearance of impropriety" and
that she had "a duty now to report this to the Michigan State Bar."

Former Michigan Judicial Tenure Commission Executive Director **Lynn
Helland** publicly confirmed that judges are barred from practicing
in other courtrooms.

**Sources:** WXYZ-TV Detroit; Belleville Independent.

## III. THE PERKINS LAW GROUP CONFLICT

judicial conflict of interest.

Todd Perkins and Perkins Law Group PLLC represented Judge Miller in
her 2024 airport gun case. In December 2025, an attorney from Perkins
Law Group appeared before Judge Miller as defense counsel in the
*People v. Charles Evans* felony murder case. Judge Miller dismissed

for the defense.

Neither the defense counsel nor Judge Miller disclosed the prior
attorney-client relationship between Judge Miller and Perkins Law
Group. This failure to disclose and recuse violates the duty of
judicial impartiality and the obligation to avoid conflicts of
interest.

## VI. UPDATED CANON VIOLATIONS

The supplemental evidence supports the following violations of the
Michigan Code of Judicial Conduct:

**Canon 1 — Upholding the Integrity and Independence of the
Judiciary.** Judge Miller's Board Chair role under a convicted felon,
combined with three separate ethics incidents in twenty-six months,
undermines both the integrity and independence of the judiciary.

**Canon 2 — Avoiding Impropriety and the Appearance of
Impropriety.** Three separate criminal and ethical incidents — the
loaded gun at the airport, the improper OWI court appearance, and
the undisclosed Perkins Law Group conflict in a murder case — plus
her fiduciary governance role at PCA, create impropriety and the
appearance of impropriety in all of Judge Miller's activities.

**Canon 3 — Performing the Duties of Judicial Office
Impartially and Diligently.** The Perkins Law Group conflict in the
*People v. Charles Evans* murder case is a direct violation of the
duty to disclose conflicts and act impartially. Judge Miller
dismissed a felony murder charge with prejudice in favor of a client
of the law firm that previously represented her, without disclosure or
recusal.

**Canon 4 — Conducting Extrajudicial Activities to Minimize
Risk of Conflict with Judicial Obligations.** Judge Miller's Board
Chair role at PCA, her personal gun incident, and her unauthorized
appearance in another judge's courtroom are extrajudicial activities
that cast reasonable doubt on her capacity to act impartially as a
Criminal Division judge.
