## Guiding Principle

Do not build a case around allegations regarding the other parent. Build a case
around documented evidence that the father is a stable, involved, capable
caregiver with safe housing, established routines, and a long history of
parenting involvement. The goal is to remove vulnerabilities, establish
continuity, and be prepared if formal custody proceedings occur.

## 3. PARENTING RECORD — ONGOING JOURNAL

This is the backbone of any custody position. Courts care about **patterns
over time**, not single events.

### What to Log (daily when applicable)

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| **Parenting time** | Dates/times children are with DaSean, overnight stays |
| **School involvement** | Drop-off, pick-up, parent-teacher conferences, homework help |
| **Medical** | Doctor visits, dental, prescriptions, sick care |
| **Activities** | Sports, lessons, park trips, family outings |
| **Exchanges** | When/where/how children are exchanged with other parent |
| **Communication** | Texts/calls about the children with other parent |
| **Daily routines** | Meals, bedtime, morning routine — shows stability |

### How to Log

- Use a simple dated notebook, Notes app, or Google Doc
- Write entries the same day — don't backfill from memory weeks later
- Factual, not emotional: "Picked up [child] from school at 3:15. Made dinner.
  Homework done. Bedtime at 8:30." Not "The other parent didn't bother to..."
- Store digital copies in `../records/`

### What to Preserve (do not delete)

- Text messages about the children (screenshot and save)
- Emails about school, medical, or scheduling
- Calendar entries showing parenting time
- School communications (report cards, event notices, teacher emails)
- Medical records (vaccination records, appointment summaries)
- Receipts for children's clothing, food, school supplies, activities
