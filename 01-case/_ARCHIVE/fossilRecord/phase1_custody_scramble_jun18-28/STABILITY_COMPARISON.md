## School Stability — Legal Framework

Michigan's Child Custody Act (MCL 722.23) defines the "best interests of the
child" as twelve factors. Several are directly relevant to school changes:

### Factor (d) — Stability and Continuity

> "The length of time the child has lived in a stable, satisfactory environment,
> and the desirability of maintaining continuity."

This is consistently cited as the most significant factor in school disputes.
Courts are reluctant to disrupt an established educational environment without
compelling justification.

### Factor (h) — School and Community Record

> "The home, school, and community record of the child."

The children's existing school records, friendships, teacher relationships, and
community involvement are all evidence under this factor.

### What the Case Law Says

**Pierron v. Pierron** (Mich. Ct. App. 2009, Docket No. 282673):
- The court need not determine which school district is *better* in general
- The question is whether the *existing* school serves the children's needs and
  whether there is reason to disrupt that environment
- The parent requesting the change carries the burden of proof

**Shimel v. McKinley** (Mich. Ct. App., Docket No. 329144):
- A school change that alters parenting time constitutes a change in the
  established custodial environment
- Requires the moving party to prove by **clear and convincing evidence** (not
  merely preponderance) that the change is in the child's best interests
- Court must analyze all twelve best-interest factors

### The Higher Standard

If an established custodial environment exists (MCL 722.27(1)(c)):
- Changing it requires **clear and convincing evidence** — a high bar
- The custodial environment is established when "over an appreciable time the
  child naturally looks to the custodian in that environment for guidance,
  discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort"

If no prior custody order exists and the children have been primarily in
DaSean's care, that history *is* the established custodial environment. A
unilateral school change by the other parent may constitute a disruption of it.
