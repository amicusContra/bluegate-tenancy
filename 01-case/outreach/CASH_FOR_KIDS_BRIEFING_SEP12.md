# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Brian Banks, an eight-time convicted felon (per Elrick, *Free Press*,
2022; Wayne County court records), operates Purpose Charter Academy — a
$1.6 million publicly funded charter school — through The Purpose Group,
LLC, registered at his home address. He also manages MacDowell
Preparatory Academy (~$5.9M in expenditures per audited financials)
through the same LLC. Two sitting Wayne County judges serve on the
respective boards: Judge Cylenthia Miller (3rd Circuit) chairs PCA's
board; Judge Tenisha Yancey (36th District) appears as Board Chair on
MacDowell's website.

The PCA management agreement — signed by Banks as President/CEO of
The Purpose Group LLC — gives his company a fee equal to **10% of the
school's net state school aid allocation** (Gross SSA), plus
reimbursement for all operating costs. Every teacher and staff member
is an employee of his LLC, not the school.

The FY 2026-27 budget adopted by PCA's board projects a fund balance
of **$21,936** — despite the management agreement's own §4.3 requiring
a **$100,000 minimum reserve.** The budget header lists the wrong
school's address (MacDowell's, not PCA's), and PCA has no budget
transparency page on its website as required by Michigan law.

The management agreement, the budget, the Eventbrite events, and the
board composition are all public records. The source documents are
attached. The questions they raise — about fund flows, board oversight,
and the use of school-affiliated branding for personal revenue — are
for reporters and regulators to investigate.

## The Financial Structure — How Banks Gets Paid

### Stream 1: Management Fee (§5.1a)
> "For each school year Management Company shall receive as compensation
> for its services a fee equal to **ten percent (10%)** of the Academy's
> Gross SSA."

- State School Aid to PCA: **$1,312,000/year**
- After DPSCD oversight deduction (~3%): ~$1,272,640
- **Banks' annual management fee: ~$127,000**

The budget line item "General Administration: $125,060" matches this
calculation almost exactly.

### Stream 2: Grant Fee (§5.1a) — Discretionary
> "Management Company **may also** receive as compensation for its services
> a fee equal to **ten percent (10%) of grant funds** that it acquires
> for the Academy **if permitted by the grant and applicable law.**"

- Federal grants to PCA: **$150,000**
- **Potential additional fee: up to $15,000** (not guaranteed — depends on grant terms and Board approval)

### Stream 3: Full Cost Reimbursement (§5.3)
> "In addition to the fee described in Section 5.1, the Academy shall
> reimburse Management Company for such costs... including, but not
> limited to, **salaries** of Management Company employees, curriculum
> and instructional materials, textbooks, library books, computer and
> other equipment, software, supplies, **marketing and development**,
> food service, transportation, special education, psychological
> services and medical services"

Banks gets reimbursed for EVERYTHING he spends "on behalf of" the school —
on top of the 10% fee.

### Stream 4: Additional Program Revenue (§3.15) — Board Approval Required
> "**If approved by the Board**, Management Company may retain the full
> amount of any and all revenue collected from or for such additional
> programs"

Additional programs = summer school, pre-kindergarten, academic camps,
latch-key programs. If the Board approves, 100% of that revenue stays
with Banks' LLC. Whether any such programs exist at PCA is unknown.

### Stream 5: Staff Control (§9.5)
> "All teaching, support staff, and other non-teaching personnel
> performing on behalf of the Academy shall be employees of
> **Management Company.**"

Every teacher, administrator, janitor, and counselor at PCA is employed
by The Purpose Group LLC — not by the school. Banks is their employer.
He sets compensation (§9.2). The Principal answers to Banks, not the Board.

## What the Judges' Presence Provides (Structural Analysis)

1. **Legitimacy** — A sitting judge's name on a school board lends
   credibility with regulators, parents, and donors
2. **Reporting gap** — Both judges have a legal duty under MRPC 8.3 to
   report known attorney misconduct. Neither has reported Banks' UPL
   to the State Bar (as far as public records show). Whether they knew
   is the question.
3. **Oversight question** — Board members have fiduciary oversight duty.
   The question is whether that oversight is being exercised — given
   the budget violation, missing transparency page, and copy-paste errors.
4. **Political networking** — Banks hosts high-profile events (CBC Week,
   galas). Whether board members benefit from this network is unknown.

## What Banks Provides the Judges

- Board positions (community prestige, "public service")
- Political access events (CBC Week, Black Tie Galas)
- Campaign ecosystem — Banks' PAC and consulting firm have historically
  moved money through connected campaigns
- Yancey paid Banks Strategy & Consultants **$15,366 (44.9% of her 2022
  campaign spend)** — Banks' only client that year
- **Unknown:** Direct compensation to judges or their families from
  Purpose Group LLC, PCA, or MacDowell?

## Structural Comparison — NOT an Equivalence Claim

The Pennsylvania "Kids for Cash" scandal (2008) involved **proven kickbacks**
— judges received $2.8M for sentencing juveniles to private detention. **We
are NOT alleging kickbacks or the same crime.** What we ARE noting is a
structural parallel worth investigating:

| Element | Pennsylvania (Ciavarella) | Detroit (Banks) — Questions |
|---------|--------------------------|-----------------|
| **Judges** | Took proven kickbacks | Chair school boards — **compensation unknown** |
| **Private operator** | Juvenile detention facility | Charter school management LLC |
| **Children** | Sentenced to detention | Enrolled for per-pupil funding |
| **Public money** | County detention contracts | State per-pupil education funding |
| **Financial flow** | Kickbacks ($2.8M total) | Management fees (~$125K/yr PCA) |
| **Judicial duty to report** | N/A | MRPC 8.3 — neither judge reported Banks' UPL |
| **Academic outcomes** | Children incarcerated | MacDowell: 3% math, 74% uncertified teachers* |

*MacDowell data. PCA is too new for academic performance data.

**The question is not whether this IS Kids for Cash. The question is:
what do the judges get for chairing these boards, and does the Board
provide meaningful oversight of the financial structure it governs?**
