## HER BACKGROUND — WHY IT MATTERS TO YOU

### She was a family law attorney for 25 years
Before becoming a judge, she practiced family law and criminal
defense. She has sat where you're sitting — on the other side
of the bench, representing people in custody and divorce cases.
She knows what parental alienation looks like. She knows what
"no custody order for 9 years" means. This is her specialty.

### She ran the Lansing ACLU
She was president of the American Civil Liberties Union in Lansing.
The ACLU protects people's civil rights against institutional power.
She understands what it looks like when a system fails someone —
when institutions protect the wrong people and leave the right
people without recourse.

### She spent 10 years monitoring Michigan's prisons
She was appointed as a Special Master on a federal class action
covering 40,000 inmates in the Michigan Department of Corrections.
For a decade, she reviewed evidence, monitored compliance, and
held institutions accountable. She is not intimidated by thick
files. She is not overwhelmed by complex cases. She has read
worse than what you're bringing today.

### She served on the Innocence Project
The Innocence Project works to free people who were wrongfully
convicted. She understands that the system gets it wrong. She
understands that sometimes the person without a lawyer is the
one telling the truth.

### She runs Truancy Court
Right now, she presides over Ingham County's Truancy Court —
a program that works with local schools to keep kids in class
and out of trouble. She cares about children's education. When
you tell her your daughters are enrolled at a school run by a
man with 9 felony convictions and 3% math proficiency — she
will hear that as a judge who works with schools every day.

### She volunteers at a legal clinic for seniors
She's on the board of a legal clinic at Cooley Law School that
helps people over 60 who can't afford attorneys. She helps
vulnerable people navigate the system. She is not going to
look down on you for not having a lawyer.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU IN THAT COURTROOM

**She will listen.** She's spent her career listening to people
who don't have power — inmates, pro se litigants, seniors
without lawyers, kids missing school. You are exactly the kind
of person she became a judge to help.

**She can handle the evidence.** You have a binder with tabs,
a USB with video, and 124 sworn admissions from the other side.
Some judges would be overwhelmed. She spent 10 years reading
federal prison compliance reports. She can process your binder.

**She cares about the children.** This is not a judge who sees
PPOs as paperwork. She runs Truancy Court. She knows what
happens when the system fails kids. When you tell her your
daughters are at a school run by a felon — that registers.

**She will follow the rules.** She's a former ACLU president.
She believes in due process. She will give the other side their
chance to speak. She will be fair. But fair is what you want —
because the facts are on your side.

**She won't punish you for being pro se.** Michigan law says
pro se litigants are held to the same standard as lawyers. But
judges are also allowed to help pro se parties understand the
process and be heard. Koenig is the kind of judge who does that.

## HOW TO TALK TO HER

**Be respectful:** "Your Honor" — every time.

**Be organized:** She will notice that you have a binder. She
will notice that you know your exhibit tabs. She will notice
that you can cite specific RFA numbers. That tells her you're
serious and prepared.

**Be honest:** Don't exaggerate. Don't guess. If you don't know
something, say "I don't know, Your Honor" or "I'd like to defer
to my witness on that." She respects honesty more than polish.

**Be a father:** You are not a lawyer. You are not pretending
to be one. You are a father who raised his daughters for 9 years,
got assaulted at his mother's house, and is asking the court
for protection so he can keep being a father. That is the most
powerful thing you can be in her courtroom.

**Don't over-lawyer it:** Kevin has the legal citations. You have
the story. Tell YOUR story. She wants to hear from the man who
named his daughters, who fed them every day, who is asking for
help. Let Kevin handle the statute numbers if the judge asks.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

You drew a good judge. She is a family law expert who cares
about children, protects civil rights, isn't intimidated by
complex evidence, and respects people who show up prepared
even without a lawyer.

Be calm. Be honest. Be organized. Be a dad.

She'll do the rest.
