DEFENDANT'S REPLY IN SUPPORT OF
MOTION FOR TEMPORARY ORDER

    Defendant DaSean Mitchell, appearing pro se, files this Reply
to Plaintiff's Response in Opposition to Defendant's Motion for
Temporary Order (filed August 12, 2026; received August 14, 2026),
pursuant to MCR 2.119(E), and states as follows:

PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

1. Defendant's Motion for Temporary Order was filed on July 10,
2026. Plaintiff received it by certified mail on July 14, 2026.
She filed no response for 29 days. Only after receiving
Defendant's Motion for Default Judgment (mailed August 10, 2026)
did Plaintiff file her opposition — on August 12, 2026.

2. Plaintiff's Response is not an independent motion. It is
solely an opposition to Defendant's Motion. Any proposed terms
Plaintiff included in her Response carry no independent procedural
force — they are merely alternative relief suggestions to be
considered only if this Court decides to modify Defendant's
proposed order. If this Court denies Defendant's Motion, Plaintiff
has no pending motion of her own upon which the Court can act.

THE PARTIES SUBSTANTIALLY AGREE

3. Plaintiff's Response proposes eleven (11) terms. Eight (8)
of those eleven terms are substantively identical to, or less
specific versions of, terms Defendant already requested. The
parties agree on:

    (a) Joint legal custody (Plaintiff retreated from her
        Complaint's request for sole legal custody);
    (b) No unilateral relocation of the children's residence;
    (c) No unilateral school enrollment changes;
    (d) Direct communication between parents regarding children;
    (e) Equal access to school, medical, and extracurricular
        records;
    (f) No disparagement of either parent in the children's
        presence;
    (g) Notification of out-of-state travel;
    (h) Mutual passport restriction.

4. The only substantive disputes between the parties are:
    (a) Which parent shall have primary physical custody; and
    (b) The structure of parenting time.

5. This level of agreement could have produced a Consent
Temporary Order at any time since July 14, 2026. Plaintiff
had Defendant's proposed terms for 29 days. She chose silence
over resolution. Her refusal to negotiate — while now proposing
substantially similar terms — demonstrates that her priority was
delay, not her children's stability.

PLAINTIFF'S PROPOSED TERMS VIOLATE HER OWN CONDUCT

6. "No unilateral school enrollment." Plaintiff proposes that
"[n]either parent enrolls children in a different school without
agreement or court order." Plaintiff enrolled both children in
Purpose Charter Academy on July 22, 2026 — eight (8) days
after receiving Defendant's Counterclaim and Motion — without
any notice to Defendant. Plaintiff also secretly explored
enrollment at Most Holy Trinity Catholic Academy from February
through July 2026 without ever informing Defendant. Plaintiff's
own Exhibit (Doc 4) proves both. Plaintiff requests this Court
to restrain conduct she has already completed.

7. "Direct communication." Plaintiff proposes that "both parents
communicate directly regarding the children." Plaintiff blocked
Defendant's phone number. She routes communication through her
mother, her sister, and the children's electronic devices.
On August 11, 2026, Defendant sent a written communication
protocol offering direct coordination. On August 11-12, 2026,
Plaintiff rejected this offer, telling Defendant's designated
contact: "Please do not contact me regarding co-parenting
matters." Plaintiff made her rejection the same days she was
preparing this Response.

8. "Equal access to records." Plaintiff proposes "equal access
to school, medical, and extracurricular records." As of the date
of this Reply, Defendant does not know the name or address of the
school where Plaintiff enrolled the children on July 22, 2026.
Plaintiff has not communicated this information despite promising
on August 11 that "school information will be communicated
directly to their father." She signed her Response the next day.
As of today, that promise remains unfulfilled.

9. "Neither parent relocates." Plaintiff proposes a mutual
no-relocation provision. Plaintiff has moved the children through
three addresses in approximately sixteen months: XXXXX Bradford
Rd, Sterling Heights → XXXXX Virginia Ave, Eastpointe → XXXXX
Dresden St, Detroit. Defendant has maintained a single address
([REDACTED_ADDR_2], Lansing) with a documented lease since
June 22, 2026.

10. "Third parties shall not make parenting decisions." Plaintiff
has routinely delegated parenting logistics to her mother, her
sister Simone, and Defendant's mother, while simultaneously
refusing direct communication with Defendant.

PLAINTIFF'S DOCUMENT LANGUAGE IS INADEQUATE

11. Defendant's Motion requests Plaintiff surrender vital documents
"within fourteen (14) days." Plaintiff proposes she is "willing to
provide copies reasonably necessary" — with no deadline and a
self-defined scope. This language perpetuates the document control
that necessitated this Motion. Plaintiff currently possesses the
children's birth certificates, Social Security cards, immunization
records, and medical records. Defendant requires these documents to
enroll the children in school and obtain medical care.

PLAINTIFF'S SILENCE WAS A CHOICE — CONSENT WAS AVAILABLE

12. Defendant filed this Motion on July 10, 2026 and served
Plaintiff with its specific terms. Plaintiff acknowledged receipt
on July 15, 2026 — texting Defendant "see you in court." She
demonstrated she had the document, understood it was a legal
filing, and chose not to engage.

13. From July 15 through August 10, 2026 — twenty-six days —
Plaintiff made no filing with this Court, no counteroffer to
Defendant, no attempt to negotiate terms, and no communication
of any kind regarding the Motion. She chose silence.

14. On August 12, 2026 — two days after receiving Defendant's
Motion for Default Judgment — Plaintiff filed a Response
proposing eleven terms. Eight of those eleven terms are
substantively identical to Defendant's original Motion. The
three disputed terms (primary physical custody, parenting-time
structure, and document-production timeline) could have been
narrowed through a single conversation at any point during
twenty-nine days of inaction.

15. A Consent Temporary Order protecting both parents and the
children was available from July 14, 2026 onward. Defendant's
offer to meet in person on July 15 demonstrates willingness to
resolve these matters without court intervention. Plaintiff
refused.

16. The children have been without any governing legal structure
since this case was filed on June 13, 2026. As of the date of
this Reply, that is over sixty (60) days of uncertainty — no
order governing custody, parenting time, document production,
travel, or school enrollment. This instability is attributable
entirely to Plaintiff's strategic silence.

17. Plaintiff's Response is not a good-faith opposition. It is
a delayed acknowledgment that the framework Defendant proposed
was reasonable. Her failure to engage for twenty-nine days —
followed by substantial agreement once consequences appeared —
demonstrates that her priority was obstruction, not the
children's stability. This conduct is directly relevant to MCL
722.23(j): willingness to facilitate a close and continuing
relationship between the children and the other parent.

CHILD SUPPORT

18. Plaintiff admits in her Answer that she has been the
"breadwinner" throughout the parties' relationship. Regardless of
which parent receives primary physical custody, the Michigan Child
Support Formula requires support to flow from the higher-income
parent to the lower-income parent. Plaintiff has never disclosed
her income in any filing.

RELIEF REQUESTED

WHEREFORE, Defendant respectfully requests:

A. That this Court GRANT Defendant's Motion for Temporary Order
   as filed on July 10, 2026;

B. In the alternative, that this Court enter a Temporary Order
   incorporating the eight (8) agreed provisions identified in
   Paragraph 3, and schedule an expedited hearing on the two
   disputed issues (primary physical custody and parenting time
   structure);

C. That any Temporary Order include a specific deadline (14 days)
   for the production of the children's vital documents, rather
   than Plaintiff's open-ended "willing to provide" language;

D. That this Court set a date for hearing on the Temporary Order
   Motion, which has been pending without action since July 10,
   2026;

E. Such other and further relief as this Court deems just and
   equitable.
