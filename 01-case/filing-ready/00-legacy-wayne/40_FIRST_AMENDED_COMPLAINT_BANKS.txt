COUNT IV — CIVIL CONSPIRACY
(Against Defendants Brian Roderick Banks and Joseph Holland Jr.)

18. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates all preceding
    paragraphs.

19. Defendants Banks and Holland, acting in concert, engaged in
    a civil conspiracy to establish and operate a network of
    entities designed to:

    (a) Enable a convicted felon (Banks) to exercise control
        over millions of dollars in public education funds
        in violation of the cash-handling restriction on his
        MDE administrator certificate;

    (b) Shield Banks' personal assets from creditors and
        future judgments through a living trust, while
        extracting cash through a home equity line of credit;

    (c) Staff fiduciary positions with financially compromised
        individuals (Holland, Ellis) whose dependence on Banks
        ensures their loyalty and silence;

    (d) Operate a political action committee (Banks Strategy
        & Consultants PAC) through which Banks channels
        payments to and from elected officials, including
        sitting judges, with Holland as nominal Treasurer.

20. Defendant Holland's participation in this conspiracy is
    established by:

    (a) His acceptance of the roles of PAC Treasurer,
        Foundation Secretary, and Successor Trustee despite
        his five bankruptcies, MDOC record, and documented
        history of financial default;

    (b) His residence at Banks' home, creating total
        financial dependence on Banks;

    (c) His co-ownership of property with Banks since 2007,
        establishing a nineteen-year financial relationship;

    (d) His willingness to serve as the nominal financial
        officer for organizations he is manifestly unqualified
        to oversee.

21. Under Michigan law, each co-conspirator is jointly and
    severally liable for the tortious acts committed in
    furtherance of the conspiracy. Admiral Ins. Co. v. Columbia
    Cas. Ins. Co., 194 Mich App 300 (1992).

22. As a direct and proximate result of the conspiracy,
    Plaintiff has suffered damages including:

    (a) Denial of his authorized representative role by an
        enterprise that exists to enable Banks' fraudulent
        operations;

    (b) Exposure to intimidation by a defendant (Banks) who
        invokes "judges and law enforcement" to suppress
        opposition;

    (c) The ongoing threat that Banks' compromised network
        will continue to interfere with the father's
        parental rights and Plaintiff's representative role
        through the institutional power of PCA.

COUNT V — BREACH OF FIDUCIARY DUTY
(Against Defendants Joseph Holland Jr. and Tracey D. Ellis)

23. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates all preceding
    paragraphs.

24. Defendant Holland, as Secretary of Purpose Foundation and
    Treasurer of Banks Strategy & Consultants PAC, owed
    fiduciary duties to the organizations' stakeholders,
    including the families and children served by PCA, and to
    the public whose tax dollars fund PCA's operations.

25. Defendant Ellis, as Treasurer and CFO of Purpose Foundation,
    owed fiduciary duties to the same stakeholders and to the
    public.

26. Defendants Holland and Ellis breached their fiduciary duties
    by:

    (a) Accepting and serving in fiduciary roles for which
        they are unqualified — Holland by reason of five
        bankruptcies, MDOC record, and demonstrated inability
        to manage his own finances; Ellis by reason of
        performing CPA-level functions without CPA licensure;

    (b) Failing to ensure that Purpose Foundation and the
        Banks PAC comply with applicable financial reporting,
        governance, and disclosure requirements;

    (c) Enabling Defendant Banks to exercise financial control
        over public education funds in violation of the
        cash-handling restriction on his MDE certificate;

    (d) Failing to disclose to PCA's authorizer (DPSCD), to
        the families served by PCA, or to any regulatory body,
        the true nature of Banks' criminal history, financial
        condition, and fitness to operate a publicly funded
        school.

27. As a direct and proximate result of Defendants Holland and
    Ellis' breach of fiduciary duty, Plaintiff has suffered
    damages including:

    (a) The entrustment of the minor children to an
        institution operated by an enterprise with no
        functioning governance or financial oversight;

    (b) The denial of Plaintiff's authorized representative
        role by an enterprise whose officers failed to
        prevent or correct Banks' unlawful conduct.
