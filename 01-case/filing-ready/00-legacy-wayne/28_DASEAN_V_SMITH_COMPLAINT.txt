FACTUAL BACKGROUND

The Nine-Year Extraction

13. Plaintiff DaSean Mitchell worked for nine years. He held
    jobs at Workbox, Vascor, Refined, Expedient, and Uber. He
    worked. He earned. He provided.

14. Every dollar Plaintiff earned flowed into a household
    economy controlled by Defendant Shayla Smith. Defendant
    managed the finances. Defendant controlled the bank
    accounts. Defendant decided how money was spent. Defendant
    called herself the "breadwinner" — her own word — while
    Plaintiff worked jobs that generated income Defendant
    directed and consumed.

15. After nine years of this arrangement, when the relationship
    ended, Plaintiff had:
    (a) No savings;
    (b) No credit history in his own name;
    (c) No retirement account;
    (d) No property;
    (e) Two collections on his credit report — both originating
        from Defendant Shayla Smith's obligations:
        (i) Insurance on Defendant's vehicle;
        (ii) Debt from Defendant's wedding.

16. Plaintiff worked for nine years and was left with less than
    nothing. The Smith household — the owned home at Dresden,
    Leon's federal salary, Shayla's banking career, Marquita's
    equity — absorbed nine years of Plaintiff's labor and
    returned nothing.

The Locked Safe

17. Throughout the relationship, Defendant Shayla Smith
    maintained a safe in the household. The safe was controlled
    by a single key. Defendant held the key. Plaintiff did not
    have a key. Plaintiff did not have independent access.

18. Inside the safe, Defendant stored Plaintiff's most critical
    personal documents:
    (a) Plaintiff's DD-214 (Certificate of Release or Discharge
        from Active Duty) — the single most important document
        of Plaintiff's military service, required for VA
        benefits, employment verification, and veteran status;
    (b) Plaintiff's Social Security card;
    (c) Plaintiff's birth certificate;
    (d) The minor children's birth certificates;
    (e) The minor children's Social Security cards;
    (f) The minor children's immunization records.

19. Defendant kept a veteran's DD-214 in a locked safe. She
    controlled his ability to prove his own military service.
    She controlled his ability to apply for VA benefits, to
    verify his identity for employment, to open bank accounts
    in his own name. His identity was hers to dispense or
    withhold.

20. The historical parallels are exact. Throughout human
    history, the powerful have controlled the vulnerable by
    holding their documents: passports confiscated by employers
    in the kafala system, freedom papers withheld from freed
    persons, identification documents seized by traffickers.
    The mechanism is always the same: if you hold a person's
    documents, you hold the person.

21. Defendant is a banking professional trained in PATRIOT Act
    compliance — she knows what identity documents are, what
    they are worth, and what happens to a person who cannot
    produce them. She held Plaintiff's documents with full
    knowledge of the consequences.

The Communication Blockade

22. During the custody dispute, Defendant blocked Plaintiff's
    phone number. Plaintiff could not call Defendant about the
    children's health, education, schedules, or welfare.

23. Instead, Defendant routed all communication through family
    intermediaries — specifically through Defendant Marquita
    Smith, Defendant Simone Smith, and through Kevin Mok (a
    non-party family friend). Defendant represented to the
    Court that this arrangement "works" (Doc 1, Factor J).

24. The arrangement does not work. It is a control mechanism.
    Defendant chooses who Plaintiff communicates through.
    Defendant controls the timing. Defendant controls whether
    messages are relayed accurately. Plaintiff is dependent on
    intermediaries chosen and controlled by Defendant.

25. On August 25, 2026, when Plaintiff attempted to communicate
    with Defendant Leon A. Smith about the mail interception
    felonies discovered by Kevin Mok, Leon handed his phone to
    Defendant Shayla Smith. Shayla responded through Leon's
    device with false legal information and intimidation. The
    "communication through intermediaries" system Defendant
    created is not communication — it is a filter that
    Defendant operates.

The Eavesdropping

26. In her sworn filing in Case No. 26-108221-DC, Defendant
    admitted that she "personally overheard" private
    conversations between Plaintiff and Kevin Mok conducted
    via FaceTime. These conversations took place in Plaintiff's
    home at [REDACTED_ADDR_2], Lansing, MI 48910.

27. Defendant monitored these conversations through the minor
    children's devices. She did not have consent from Plaintiff.
    She did not have consent from Kevin Mok. She used the
    content of these monitored conversations in her sworn
    custody filing.

28. Monitoring private conversations without consent is a felony
    under MCL 750.539c (eavesdropping). The content of
    illegally intercepted communications is inadmissible under
    MCL 750.539e.

29. Defendant's eavesdropping was not passive. She used what she
    heard to craft her custody filing. She weaponized private
    conversations between a father and his friend — conversations
    occurring in the father's own home, in Ingham County — as
    evidence against the father in a custody case.

The Father's Day Ambush

30. On June 21, 2026 — Father's Day — Defendant lured Plaintiff
    to XXXXX Dresden St under the pretense of a custody
    exchange. Prior exchanges had occurred at Plaintiff's
    mother's home on Cornerstone Blvd, Detroit. Defendant
    changed the location to Dresden without explanation.

31. When Plaintiff arrived at Dresden for the exchange, Defendant
    Simone Smith served Plaintiff with a custody complaint at
    the front door — in front of Plaintiff's minor children.

32. Defendant Marquita L. Smith was inside the house during the
    service. She watched from inside as Plaintiff — the man who
    had worked for nine years to support the household, the man
    whose mother had provided years of free childcare — was
    ambushed with a lawsuit on Father's Day.

33. The Father's Day service was not an accident. The location
    change was deliberate. The timing was deliberate. The choice
    to serve on Father's Day — a day Plaintiff should have spent
    with his children — was deliberate. The choice to use
    Simone Smith as the server, in front of the children, was
    deliberate.

The School Enrollment

34. On or about July 22, 2026, during Plaintiff's custody week,
    Defendant enrolled the minor children at Purpose Charter
    Academy (PCA) in Detroit without notifying Plaintiff. In
    her sworn filing, Defendant stated "DaSean is aware girls
    are enrolled."

35. Plaintiff was not aware. He was not consulted. He was not
    notified. Kevin Mok — the family's only functioning
    communication channel — was not informed until August 12,
    2026, when he asked directly.

36. PCA is located in Detroit. Plaintiff lives in Lansing.
    Defendant enrolled the children in a school 90 miles from
    their father's home, during their father's parenting time,
    without their father's knowledge.

The Senegal School Shopping

37. While traveling internationally in Senegal, West Africa,
    Defendant emailed a different school in Detroit about
    enrollment for the minor children — children who were
    living with Plaintiff in Lansing, Michigan. The school's
    tuition exceeded Defendant's demonstrated ability to pay.

38. Defendant was not in the country. She was not in the state.
    She was not in the children's daily lives. Yet she was
    making custody-level educational decisions for children in
    Plaintiff's care, from another continent, without telling
    Plaintiff.

39. Defendant submitted these email exchanges as her own exhibits
    in Case No. 26-108221-DC — documenting her pattern of
    unilateral decision-making from 4,000 miles away while the
    children ate breakfast with their father in Lansing.

The Mail Interception

40. On August 21, 2026, Defendant Marquita L. Smith intercepted
    Certified Mail Restricted Delivery addressed to multiple
    members of the Smith household at the Harper Woods, Michigan
    post office — the workplace of Defendant Leon A. Smith.

41. Marquita signed for restricted delivery mail addressed to
    Shayla Smith (twice), Leon A. Smith, and Simone Smith. She
    signed under the addressees' names, not her own. No PS Form
    3801 or agent authorization was on file.

42. This interception occurred at a federal postal facility. It
    constitutes mail theft (18 USC 1702) and forgery of mail
    receipts (18 USC 1708).

43. Defendant Leon A. Smith was warned by Plaintiff about these
    felonies on August 25, 2026. Leon's response was to hand
    his phone to Defendant Shayla Smith, who responded through
    Leon's device with false legal information ("as long as an
    adult sign for it, it's legal") and intimidation ("You guys
    are not that smart, but continue, God don't like ugly").

44. The mail that was intercepted contained legal information
    relevant to the custody dispute. The Smith family's
    decision to intercept, rather than receive, legal
    correspondence affected Plaintiff's ability to communicate
    and resolve the custody matter.

The Household Economy

45. The Smith household at 17370 Dresden operates as a single
    economic unit. All four Defendants reside at the same
    address. Their finances are intermingled — the mortgage is
    through Shayla's employer (Lake Trust CU), Leon's federal
    salary supports the household, Marquita co-owns the
    property, and the household's legal and communication
    affairs are managed collectively.

46. Plaintiff's nine years of labor subsidized this household
    economy. His wages flowed to Defendant Shayla Smith. His
    mother Deanna Mitchell provided years of free childcare
    that enabled the Smith household to function. Kevin Mok
    provided services, support, and resources that benefited
    the household.

47. The flow of value was unidirectional: from the Mitchells to
    the Smiths. For nine years. The Smiths returned nothing —
    no compensation, no equity, no documents, no dignity.

COUNT I — CONVERSION OF PERSONAL PROPERTY
(Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

48. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-47.

49. Defendant Shayla Smith exercised unauthorized dominion and
    control over Plaintiff's personal property by retaining
    sole possession of:
    (a) Plaintiff's DD-214 (Certificate of Release or Discharge
        from Active Duty);
    (b) Plaintiff's Social Security card;
    (c) Plaintiff's birth certificate;
    (d) The minor children's birth certificates;
    (e) The minor children's Social Security cards;
    (f) The minor children's immunization records.

50. These documents were stored in a safe controlled by Defendant
    with a single key. Plaintiff had no independent access.
    Plaintiff has demanded their return. They have not been
    produced.

51. Defendant's retention of Plaintiff's DD-214 prevented
    Plaintiff from accessing VA benefits, verifying employment,
    and proving his veteran status. Defendant — a PATRIOT Act-
    trained banking professional — knew the significance of
    these documents and the consequences of withholding them.

52. Under Michigan law, conversion is any distinct act of
    dominion wrongfully exerted over another's personal
    property in denial of or inconsistent with the rights
    therein. Foremost Ins. Co. v. Allstate Ins. Co., 439 Mich
    378 (1992).

COUNT II — FINANCIAL EXPLOITATION AND UNJUST ENRICHMENT
(Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

53. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-47.

54. Over the course of approximately nine years, Plaintiff
    worked at multiple employers (Workbox, Vascor, Refined,
    Expedient, Uber) and earned wages that flowed entirely into
    a household economy controlled by Defendant.

55. Defendant managed all finances. Plaintiff built no credit,
    no savings, no retirement, and no property in his own name.
    At the end of nine years, Plaintiff had less than nothing:
    two collections on his credit report, both from Defendant's
    obligations.

56. Defendant was unjustly enriched by Plaintiff's labor. Her
    career at Lake Trust Credit Union — her salary, her 401(k),
    her HSA, her credit history — was built on a foundation that
    included Plaintiff's domestic labor (as primary caregiver
    for the children) and Plaintiff's earnings (which Defendant
    directed). Without Plaintiff's contributions, Defendant
    would have needed to pay for childcare, reduce her work
    hours, or both.

57. The value conferred by Plaintiff includes:
    (a) Nine years of primary childcare (fair market value:
        $8-$12/hour per child in Wayne County);
    (b) Nine years of household labor;
    (c) Wages from multiple employers, all flowing to
        Defendant-controlled accounts;
    (d) Credit damage absorbed by Plaintiff from Defendant's
        obligations.

58. Defendant retained the full benefit of Plaintiff's nine
    years of labor and returned nothing. Under Barber v. SMH
    (Pontiac), 202 Mich App 366 (1993), unjust enrichment is
    established.

COUNT III — UNJUST ENRICHMENT
(Against Defendants Leon A. Smith and Marquita L. Smith)

59. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-47.

60. Defendants Leon A. Smith and Marquita L. Smith benefited
    directly from Plaintiff's labor over nine years. Plaintiff's
    domestic work (childcare, household labor) and Plaintiff's
    earned income (flowing through Defendant Shayla Smith) both
    contributed to the Dresden household economy that Defendants
    Leon and Marquita own and operate.

61. Defendants Leon and Marquita's home was not burdened by
    childcare costs because Plaintiff absorbed them. Their
    household income was not reduced by the costs of raising
    grandchildren because Plaintiff bore those costs.

62. Neither Defendant Leon A. Smith nor Defendant Marquita L.
    Smith ever compensated Plaintiff.

COUNT IV — EAVESDROPPING
(Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

63. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-47.

64. Defendant monitored Plaintiff's private FaceTime
    conversations with Kevin Mok through the minor children's
    devices without the consent of either participant.

65. Plaintiff's conversations took place in his home at
    [REDACTED_ADDR_2], Lansing, MI 48910 — in Ingham County.
    He had a reasonable expectation of privacy in his own home.

66. Defendant admitted to this monitoring in her sworn filing
    (Doc 1, Factor J): she "personally overheard" the
    communications.

67. Under MCL 750.539c, eavesdropping on private conversations
    is a felony. Under MCL 750.539d, the use of devices to
    intercept private communications is unlawful. Under MCL
    750.539e, evidence obtained through eavesdropping is
    inadmissible.

68. Plaintiff suffered damages including:
    (a) Loss of privacy in his own home;
    (b) Weaponization of his private conversations in a custody
        proceeding;
    (c) Chilling effect on his ability to communicate freely
        with family and friends about the custody situation;
    (d) Emotional distress from learning his former partner was
        monitoring his calls through their children's devices.

COUNT V — CUSTODIAL INTERFERENCE
(Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

69. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-47.

70. Defendant made multiple unilateral custody-level decisions
    about the minor children without notifying Plaintiff or
    obtaining his consent, including:

    (a) Enrolling the children at Purpose Charter Academy
        (Detroit) on or about July 22, 2026, during Plaintiff's
        custody week, without notice;

    (b) Emailing a different school about enrollment from
        Senegal, West Africa, while the children were in
        Plaintiff's care in Lansing;

    (c) Scheduling and completing medical physicals at Advance
        Health (Clinton Township) without Plaintiff's knowledge;

    (d) Applying for government benefits (SNAP, Medicaid, FIP)
        in the children's names at false addresses without
        informing Plaintiff;

    (e) Filing a change of address redirecting mail away from
        the address where the children resided.

71. Each of these unilateral decisions was a custody-level
    determination made without the other parent's knowledge,
    consent, or participation. Collectively, they demonstrate a
    pattern of exclusion: Defendant made every significant
    decision about the children's lives alone, while swearing
    to the Court that Plaintiff was "aware."

72. Plaintiff suffered damages including:
    (a) Loss of participation in his children's education;
    (b) Loss of participation in his children's healthcare;
    (c) Uncertainty about the children's benefits status and
        identity records;
    (d) The cost and burden of correcting false enrollments,
        obtaining replacement documents, and untangling benefits
        fraud committed in the children's names.

COUNT VI — CIVIL CONSPIRACY
(Against All Defendants)

73. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-47.

74. Defendants Shayla Smith, Leon A. Smith, Marquita L. Smith,
    and Simone Smith acted in concert to exploit Plaintiff's
    labor, isolate Plaintiff from his children, and deprive
    Plaintiff of his property and parental rights. Their
    coordinated conduct includes:

    (a) Extracting nine years of Plaintiff's labor and earnings
        into the Smith household economy while returning nothing;

    (b) Retaining Plaintiff's personal documents (DD-214, SSN
        card, birth certificate) in a safe Plaintiff could not
        access;

    (c) The Father's Day ambush (June 21, 2026): Defendant
        Shayla Smith engineered the location change to Dresden,
        Defendant Simone Smith served the papers, Defendant
        Marquita L. Smith watched from inside the house;

    (d) The communication blockade: Defendant Shayla Smith
        blocked Plaintiff's phone, Defendants Marquita and
        Simone Smith served as controlled intermediaries;

    (e) The mail interception (August 21, 2026): Defendant
        Marquita L. Smith intercepted Certified Mail Restricted
        Delivery at the Harper Woods post office — Defendant
        Leon A. Smith's workplace — preventing legal
        correspondence from reaching its addressees;

    (f) The cover-up (August 25, 2026): When Plaintiff warned
        Defendant Leon A. Smith about the mail felonies, Leon
        handed his phone to Defendant Shayla Smith, who
        responded with false legal information and intimidation
        — coordinated obstruction through a family phone chain;

    (g) The systematic use of the children as instruments:
        monitoring Plaintiff's calls through their devices,
        using their identities for benefits, enrolling them in
        schools without Plaintiff's knowledge, and withholding
        their documents as leverage.

75. Under Michigan law, civil conspiracy requires: (1) a
    concerted action; (2) by a combination of two or more
    persons; (3) to accomplish a criminal or unlawful purpose;
    (4) or to accomplish a lawful purpose by criminal or
    unlawful means. Admiral Ins. Co. v. Columbia Cas. Ins.
    Co., 194 Mich App 300 (1992).

76. The Smith household operated as a coordinated unit to
    extract Plaintiff's labor, control his documents, intercept
    his mail, monitor his conversations, and ambush him with
    litigation on Father's Day — all while Plaintiff was the
    primary caregiver for the minor children and all while
    Plaintiff's mother provided years of free childcare that
    enriched the Smith household.
