The Identity Problem

19. Throughout human history, the powerful have used the
    identities of the vulnerable as instruments. Colonial
    powers counted conquered peoples as subjects to claim

    be heard. Census rolls, tax records, benefit applications

    for the powerful.

20. These children's identities were used as currency.
    Defendant used their Social Security numbers, their dates
    of birth, and their names to apply for government benefits.
    She did not ask their father. She did not need their
    consent — they are children. She simply used them, the way

        from their father, and inaccessible to the children
        themselves.

23. A child's identity is not a tool. It is not a key to

24. Sahana is nine. Allanna is seven. They do not know what a

COUNT I — CONVERSION OF GOVERNMENT BENEFITS

32. Plaintiffs re-allege and incorporate Paragraphs 1-31.

33. Government benefits obtained in the minor Plaintiffs' names
    — SNAP, Medicaid, FIP, and any other public assistance —
    were designated for their care. These benefits represent
    a property interest belonging to the children, administered
    by their custodial parent.

34. During periods when the minor Plaintiffs resided with their
    father, DaSean Mitchell, Defendant continued to receive and
    use benefits designated for the children. The children
    received none of the benefit. Their father bore their
    expenses without any share of the government assistance
    issued in their names.

35. Defendant converted the children's benefits to her own use
    or to the use of the Smith household at 17370 Dresden.

36. Under Michigan law, conversion is any distinct act of
    dominion wrongfully exerted over another's personal property
    in denial of or inconsistent with the rights therein.
    Foremost Ins. Co. v. Allstate Ins. Co., 439 Mich 378
    (1992). Defendant's receipt and use of benefits designated
    for these children, during periods when the children did
    not reside with her, constitutes conversion of the
    children's property interest in those benefits.

COUNT II — UNAUTHORIZED USE OF PERSONAL IDENTIFYING
INFORMATION (MCL 445.65)

37. Plaintiffs re-allege and incorporate Paragraphs 1-31.

38. MCL 445.65 provides that a person shall not use another
    person's personal identifying information to obtain or
    attempt to obtain anything of value.

39. Defendant used the minor Plaintiffs' personal identifying
    information — including their names, dates of birth, and
    Social Security numbers — to apply for and obtain
    government benefits, including SNAP, Medicaid, and FIP.

40. The children did not consent. They are minors and cannot
    consent. Their father, DaSean Mitchell, was not informed
    and did not consent.

41. Defendant obtained things of value through the use of the
    children's information: food assistance, healthcare
    coverage, and cash benefits.

42. The children's identities are now tied to government records
    that may be fraudulent — records created by Defendant using
    the children's information at addresses where the children
    did not reside, with household compositions that did not
    reflect reality, and with income information that was false.

43. The harm to the children is ongoing: their Social Security
    numbers, their medical records, and their benefits history
    carry the mark of Defendant's fraud. They will carry it
    into adulthood unless it is remedied.

COUNT III — CONVERSION OF VITAL DOCUMENTS

44. Plaintiffs re-allege and incorporate Paragraphs 1-31.

45. The minor Plaintiffs' birth certificates, Social Security
    cards, immunization records, and any passports are the
    personal property of the children. They are not Defendant's
    property. They are documents issued by government agencies
    for the benefit of the persons named on them.

46. Defendant has retained sole possession of these documents
    since approximately February 2026, despite the children
    residing primarily with their father. Defendant has
    withheld them from DaSean Mitchell's access while using
    them for her own purposes (school enrollment, medical
    appointments, benefits applications).

47. Defendant's retention and exclusive use of the children's
    documents — while denying access to their custodial
    father — constitutes conversion.
