LEGAL STANDARD

Judicial Estoppel

3. Judicial estoppel prevents a party from assuming a position
   in a legal proceeding inconsistent with one previously
   asserted. The doctrine protects the integrity of the judicial
   process. Paschke v. Retool Indus., 445 Mich 502, 509 (1994).

4. Judicial estoppel applies when: (1) a party has taken an
   inconsistent position under oath; (2) the party succeeded
   in maintaining the first position (i.e., the court accepted
   or relied on it); and (3) the party would derive an unfair
   advantage by switching positions. Wausau Underwriters Ins.
   Co. v. Vulcan Dev., Inc., 323 F.3d 396, 402 (6th Cir. 2003)
   (applying Michigan law).

Equitable Estoppel

5. Equitable estoppel requires: (1) a party, by representations,
   admissions, or silence, intentionally or negligently induced
   another party to believe certain facts; (2) the other party
   justifiably relied on those representations; and (3) the
   other party was prejudiced by the reliance. Lothian v. City
   of Detroit, 414 Mich 160, 168 (1982).

FACTUAL BASIS

I. DEFENDANT'S OWN EXHIBITS ESTABLISH THE FOLLOWING FACTS

A. Defendant Had Access to Employer-Sponsored Health Insurance
   While Claiming Medicaid

6. Defendant submitted her own financial documents as exhibits.
   Among them is a Health Savings Account (HSA) card ending in
   8120 issued through Lake Trust Credit Union. An HSA is only
   available to individuals enrolled in a High Deductible Health
   Plan (HDHP).

7. Defendant simultaneously applied for and received Medicaid
   coverage for the minor children (MDHHS Doc 6). Medicaid
   eligibility in Michigan requires that the applicant lack
   access to adequate employer-sponsored health insurance.

8. Defendant's own exhibits prove she had employer-sponsored
   insurance (the HSA/HDHP) while claiming government-funded
   healthcare. She is estopped from denying access to employer
   health insurance because she submitted the proof herself.

B. Defendant Knew DaSean's Employment Status While Swearing
   She Did Not

9. Defendant's filing includes MDHHS Document 6, which contains
   five (5) employment-related notices about Plaintiff DaSean
   Mitchell:
   - Three (3) New Hire Reports
   - Two (2) Wage Match notices
   These documents were in Defendant's possession and submitted
   as her own exhibits.

10. On the FOC 23 (Verified Statement of Friend of the Court,
    sworn June 13, 2026), Defendant wrote "N/A" for Plaintiff's
    employment. She swore under oath that she had no information
    about Plaintiff's employment — while possessing five
    government documents proving his employment history.

11. Defendant is judicially estopped from claiming ignorance of
    Plaintiff's employment. She submitted the proof of her
    knowledge as her own exhibit, then swore the opposite on a
    verified statement. Both documents are in this Court's file.

C. Defendant Admitted Eavesdropping on Third-Party
   Communications

12. In her sworn filing (Doc 1, Factor J — Willingness to
    Facilitate Communication), Defendant stated that she
    "personally overheard" communications between Plaintiff
    and Kevin Mok (a non-party family friend) that were
    conducted via FaceTime through the minor children's
    devices.

13. Defendant used this information to argue that Plaintiff
    and Kevin Mok were coaching the children. However,
    monitoring private communications through a child's device
    constitutes eavesdropping under MCL 750.539c (a felony),
    and the content of eavesdropped communications is
    inadmissible under MCL 750.539e.

14. Defendant is estopped from:
    (a) Using the content of eavesdropped communications as
        evidence;
    (b) Denying that she monitored Plaintiff's communications;
    (c) Claiming she was a passive recipient of information the
        children volunteered — she swore she "personally
        overheard" conversations, not that the children reported
        them.

D. Defendant Claimed Communication "Works" While Blocking
   Plaintiff

15. In her sworn filing (Doc 1, Factor J), Defendant represented
    to this Court that communication with Plaintiff "works"
    through family intermediaries, including Defendant's mother,
    Marquita Smith, and sister, Simone Smith.

16. During the same period:
    (a) Plaintiff's phone number was blocked by Defendant;
    (b) Plaintiff was unable to reach Defendant directly
        regarding the children's schedules, medical needs, or
        educational decisions;
    (c) Kevin Mok — the family friend Defendant claims she
        communicates through — was the only channel Defendant
        would respond to, and those communications are
        documented in text and Messenger records.

17. Defendant is estopped from claiming communication "works"
    while maintaining a communication blockade. She chose the
    intermediaries. She blocked direct contact. She cannot now
    claim the arrangement she created is adequate or that
    Plaintiff failed to communicate.

E. Defendant Used Plaintiff's Mother's EBT Benefits and
   Claimed Credit

18. Defendant submitted Walmart invoices (Exhibits P-2 and P-3)
    bearing the name "Deanna Mitchell" — Plaintiff's mother.
    These invoices document grocery orders made using Plaintiff's
    mother's Electronic Benefits Transfer (EBT) card.

19. Defendant submitted these invoices to this Court as evidence
    of HER provision for the children — claiming credit for
    spending a grandmother's government food stamps.

20. Defendant is estopped from:
    (a) Claiming she independently provided for the children's
        food needs — her own exhibits show she used someone
        else's benefits;
    (b) Denying she had access to or used Deanna Mitchell's EBT
        card — she submitted the receipts herself;
    (c) Claiming she did not know Deanna Mitchell's address —
        she told Kevin Mok on June 26, 2026 that she "didn't
        know" Deanna's new address while ordering deliveries
        TO that address using Deanna's EBT.

F. Defendant Enrolled Children in School Without Plaintiff's
   Knowledge or Consent

21. On or about July 22, 2026, during Plaintiff's custody week,
    Defendant enrolled the minor children at Purpose Charter
    Academy (PCA) in Detroit without notifying Plaintiff. In her
    sworn filing, Defendant stated "DaSean is aware girls are

22. Plaintiff was not aware. Plaintiff was not consulted.

23. Defendant is estopped from:
    (a) Claiming Plaintiff was "aware" — she swore it, and it

    (b) Claiming she attempted to communicate about the

    (c) Making any future unilateral educational decisions

G. Defendant Made Custody-Level Decisions from Senegal Without

II. EQUITABLE ESTOPPEL — DETRIMENTAL RELIANCE

A. Custody Exchange Location

27. Prior custody exchanges between the parties took place at
    Plaintiff's mother's home on Cornerstone Blvd, Detroit —
    a neutral, familiar location for the children. Defendant
    changed the exchange location to XXXXX Dresden St, Detroit,
    on Father's Day (June 21, 2026) without explanation.

28. Plaintiff relied on the established pattern of Cornerstone
    exchanges. When Plaintiff arrived at Dresden as directed,
    Defendant's sister Simone Smith served him with a custody
    complaint at the front door — in front of the children.

29. Defendant induced Plaintiff's presence at a specific
    location for the purpose of ambush service. Plaintiff's
    reliance on the good faith of a custody exchange was
    exploited.

30. Defendant is equitably estopped from:
    (a) Claiming the Father's Day service was routine — she
        engineered the location change;
    (b) Using any argument that Plaintiff was properly served
        "at the exchange" — the exchange location was changed
        specifically to facilitate service;
    (c) Claiming cooperative parenting while using a custody
        handoff as an ambush.

B. Representation of Financial Responsibility

31. Throughout the nine-year relationship, Defendant represented
    herself as the household's financial manager and
    "breadwinner" (her own word). Plaintiff relied on this
    representation by working at multiple jobs (Workbox, Vascor,
    Refined, Expedient, Uber) and allowing his earnings to flow
    into a household economy controlled by Defendant.

32. As a result of this reliance, Plaintiff:
    (a) Built no credit in his own name during the nine-year
        relationship;
    (b) Accumulated no savings;
    (c) Has no retirement account;
    (d) Has two collections on his credit report — both

    (e) Had his DD-214, Social Security card, and birth

33. Defendant is equitably estopped from:
    (a) Claiming Plaintiff is financially irresponsible —
        Defendant controlled the finances;
    (b) Claiming Plaintiff cannot provide for the children —
        Defendant extracted his earning capacity for nine years

    (c) Using Plaintiff's current financial position against him

35. Defendant simultaneously used these documents for unilateral
