FACTUAL BACKGROUND

The Mitchell Household — What Plaintiff Had

12. Plaintiff Deanna Mitchell is a grandmother living in Section
    8 housing. Her resources are limited. Her home is small. Her
    income is fixed. She does not own property. She does not
    have savings. She depends on government housing assistance
    to keep a roof over her head.

13. What Plaintiff does have — what she has always had — is her
    family. Her home on Cornerstone has been the gathering
    place for the Mitchell family and extended family for years.
    She has opened her door to her son, her grandchildren, her
    son's partner's family, and neighborhood children, feeding
    them, caring for them, and providing a safe and loving
    environment on a fraction of the resources available to the
    Smith household.

The Smith Household — What Defendants Had

14. The Smith family resides at XXXXX Dresden St, Detroit — a
    home they OWN, mortgaged through Lake Trust Credit Union
    ($68,000, recorded June 30, 2025). The household includes
    four working-age adults: Defendant Leon A. Smith (USPS Mail
    Carrier, federal salary), Defendant Marquita L. Smith
    (co-owner), Defendant Shayla Smith (banking professional),
    and Defendant Simone Smith.

15. By every objective measure, the Smith household has
    substantially greater resources than Plaintiff: owned
    property, federal employment, banking employment, four adult
    incomes, employer health benefits (including Defendant
    Shayla Smith's HSA through Lake Trust CU), and access to
    credit (at minimum nine different payment cards identified
    in Defendant Shayla Smith's own court exhibits).

The Flow — From the Mitchells to the Smiths

16. Throughout the nine-year relationship between DaSean Mitchell
    and Defendant Shayla Smith, the flow of labor, resources,
    and benefits moved in one direction: from the Mitchell
    family to the Smith family. The wealthier family took from
    the poorer family. The family with the owned home, the
    federal salary, and the bank career extracted labor and
    resources from the grandmother in Section 8 housing.

17. This is not metaphor. This is the documented record:

    (a) Plaintiff provided YEARS of free childcare for Sahana
        and Allanna at her home on Cornerstone. She fed them.
        She bathed them. She put them to bed. She woke them up.
        She cared for them while her son worked — at jobs that
        paid wages that flowed into a household economy
        controlled by Defendant Shayla Smith.

    (b) Plaintiff also provided free childcare for Defendant
        SIMONE SMITH's children — Amir and Journey. Simone
        Smith sent her children to Cornerstone for Plaintiff's
        care. Plaintiff fed them. Plaintiff supervised them.
        Simone Smith never compensated Plaintiff.

    (c) Defendant Shayla Smith's career at Lake Trust Credit
        Union — her salary, her 401(k), her HSA, her credit
        history — was built on a foundation of free childcare
        provided by Plaintiff and by Plaintiff's son. Without
        Plaintiff's labor, Defendant would have needed to pay
        for professional childcare or reduce her work hours.
        She did neither. She used Plaintiff instead.

    (d) Defendants Leon A. Smith and Marquita L. Smith
        benefited directly from Plaintiff's labor. Their
        household at 17370 Dresden — the home they OWN —
        functioned as a family unit where childcare costs were
        eliminated by Plaintiff's unpaid labor. Leon's federal
        salary was not reduced by childcare expenses because
        Plaintiff bore them. Marquita's homeownership was not
        burdened by childcare costs because Plaintiff
        absorbed them.

    (e) The Smiths — with their owned home, their federal
        salary, their banking career — never once compensated
        Plaintiff for childcare. Never once sent money to
        Cornerstone. Never once offered to share the wealth
        that Plaintiff's labor helped create.

The Household Economy

18. The Smith household at 17370 Dresden operates as a single
    economic unit. Defendant Leon A. Smith earns a federal
    salary. Defendant Marquita L. Smith co-owns the home.
    Defendant Shayla Smith earns a banking salary. All four
    Defendants reside at the same address. Their finances are
    intermingled — the mortgage is through Shayla's employer,
    the mail is collected by Marquita, the household's legal
    affairs are managed collectively.

19. Plaintiff's free labor subsidized this entire household
    economy. When Plaintiff watched the grandchildren so that
    DaSean could work, and DaSean's wages flowed to Shayla,
    and Shayla's expenses were reduced by the Dresden mortgage,
    and Leon's salary covered the Dresden bills — Plaintiff's
    labor was the invisible foundation that made it all work.

The Pattern — Historical Context

20. The pattern described above is not new. Throughout history,
    vulnerable people have been exploited by those with greater
    resources — their labor extracted, their identity controlled,
    their compensation withheld, and their dignity dismissed.

    Plaintiff's situation mirrors the kafala system practiced in
    parts of the Middle East, where a sponsor controls a
    worker's documents, movement, and financial autonomy. The
    worker labors. The sponsor profits. Defendant Shayla Smith
    controlled the family's documents — including Plaintiff's
    son's DD-214, Social Security card, and birth certificate
    — in a locked safe with a single key. She controlled the
    family's finances. She controlled access to government
    benefits. She controlled communication by blocking her
    partner's phone and routing messages through family
    intermediaries — including through Plaintiff herself.

    Plaintiff was not a worker in a foreign country. She was a
    grandmother in her own home. But the mechanics were the
    same: labor without compensation, control without consent,
    enrichment without reciprocity.

21. Plaintiff's son, DaSean Mitchell, worked for nine years. He
    earned wages at Workbox, Vascor, Refined, Expedient, and
    Uber. Every dollar went into a household economy controlled
    by Defendant Shayla Smith. He built nothing in his own name.
    No credit. No savings. No retirement. No documents. When
    Defendant was done, she took everything and left him with
    less than nothing — two collections on his credit report,
    both originating from her obligations (insurance on her car,
    debt from her wedding).

    Plaintiff watched this happen to her son. And while it
    happened, Plaintiff's own labor — her childcare, her home,
    her food, her EBT benefits — was being extracted by the
    same person, for the same family, with the same result:
    the Smiths grew wealthier, and the Mitchells grew poorer.

The EBT Exploitation

23. SNAP benefits are issued for the benefit of the children
    named on the case. Under Michigan MDHHS policy, SNAP
    follows the children — when the children are in the care
    of a household member (such as their grandmother), the
    food assistance is intended to feed them in that household.
    When Plaintiff Deanna Mitchell watched the children at
    Cornerstone, the SNAP benefits should have been feeding
    those children at Plaintiff's table. Instead, Defendant
    Shayla Smith controlled the EBT card and used it remotely
    — ordering groceries to addresses of her choosing, on her
    schedule, under her direction.

24. Defendant submitted Walmart invoices as her own exhibits
    in the custody proceeding (Case No. 26-108221-DC, Exhibits
    P-2 and P-3). These December receipts — filed by Defendant
    herself — reveal a pattern of multi-address, multi-name
    ordering that documents the exploitation:

    (a) Orders placed to the Bradford address (37055 Bradford,
        Sterling Heights) under DEFENDANT'S name, paid by a
        combination of EBT and credit card;

    (b) Orders placed to Plaintiff's address (Cornerstone Blvd,
        Detroit) under DEFENDANT'S name, paid by a combination
        of EBT and credit card;

    (c) An order placed to Plaintiff's address (Cornerstone)
        under PLAINTIFF'S name — "Deanna Mitchell" — paid by
        EBT.

25. Defendant's own exhibits therefore prove, at minimum:

    (a) Defendant had access to and used Plaintiff's EBT card
        or account (the Cornerstone order under "Deanna
        Mitchell");

    (b) Defendant was ordering to multiple addresses she did
        not live at, routing government food benefits to
        locations of her choosing;

    (c) Defendant used BOTH her own credit cards AND government
        EBT on the same orders — a banking professional with
        nine payment methods was supplementing her personal
        purchases with a grandmother's food stamps;

    (d) Defendant controlled the ordering for Plaintiff's
        household — deciding what food arrived, when it
        arrived, and how it was paid for — while Plaintiff
        provided the childcare labor that the food was
        supposedly supporting.

26. This Court should take Defendant's own exhibits at face
    value. Defendant filed these receipts as evidence of her
    provision for the children. She chose them. She organized
    them. She swore to them. The receipts show orders under
    two different names (Shayla Smith and Deanna Mitchell) to
    two different addresses (Bradford and Cornerstone) using
    two different payment methods (EBT and credit). Every
    element of this pattern is documented by Defendant herself.

27. Plaintiff is a Section 8 resident on limited income. Her
    EBT benefits exist because she cannot afford adequate food
    on her own resources. Those benefits are issued to
    Plaintiff for Plaintiff's household. Defendant Shayla Smith
    — a banking professional with an HSA, an American Express
    card, a Visa, three MasterCards, and access to at least
    nine different payment methods — used a poor grandmother's
    food stamps and then claimed credit for it in court.

28. Defendant further told Kevin Mok (a longtime family friend)
    on June 26, 2026: "I don't know her new address" —
    referring to Plaintiff. This statement was made while
    Defendant was ordering grocery deliveries TO Plaintiff's
    address using Plaintiff's EBT. She claimed not to know
    where Plaintiff lived while ordering food to her front door
    with her benefits card.

The Risk to Plaintiff's Housing

29. Defendant's exploitation of Plaintiff's benefits has placed
    Plaintiff's Section 8 housing at risk. If the Michigan
    Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS) or the
    Office of Inspector General investigates the benefits fraud
    committed by Defendant and discovers that Plaintiff's EBT

    benefits case and housing voucher.

30. Plaintiff did not commit fraud. Plaintiff is the victim of
    fraud. But Defendant's actions have created a cloud over
    Plaintiff's benefits history that Plaintiff must now
    defend — using resources Plaintiff does not have, against
    consequences Plaintiff did not create.

31. The loss of Section 8 housing would be catastrophic for
    Plaintiff. It would eliminate the safe home where she cared
    for her grandchildren, where she opened her doors to the
    community, and where she provided the labor that enriched
    the Smith family for years.

The Father's Day Ambush

32. On June 21, 2026 — Father's Day — Defendant Shayla Smith
    lured Plaintiff's son, DaSean Mitchell, to 17370 Dresden

33. Defendant Marquita L. Smith was inside the house during

34. Prior custody exchanges had taken place at Plaintiff's home
    on Cornerstone. Defendant Shayla Smith changed the location
    to Dresden specifically to facilitate this ambush, in the
    presence of the same grandmother whose labor she had
    exploited for years.

COUNT I — UNJUST ENRICHMENT
(Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

35. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-34.

36. Plaintiff conferred substantial benefits upon Defendant
    Shayla Smith, including:
    (a) Years of free childcare for Sahana and Allanna
        Mitchell at Plaintiff's home on Cornerstone;
    (b) Meals, supervision, and daily care for the children
        during Plaintiff's son's working hours — work that
        generated income Defendant controlled;
    (c) A safe, stable, loving environment that enabled
        Defendant to maintain full-time employment at Lake
        Trust Credit Union without paying for professional
        childcare;
    (d) The use of Plaintiff's home as a family gathering
        place for Defendant's children, Defendant's sister's
        children, and extended family — at Plaintiff's
        expense.

37. Defendant received and retained these benefits. Every hour
    of childcare Plaintiff provided was an hour Defendant did
    not pay for. Every meal Plaintiff served was a meal
    Defendant did not buy. Every day Defendant worked at Lake
    Trust while Plaintiff watched her children was a day
    Defendant's salary, 401(k), and career advanced — on the
    back of Plaintiff's unpaid labor.

38. Defendant never compensated Plaintiff. Not once. Not a
    dollar. Not a meal in return. Not a gift. Not an
    acknowledgment. Plaintiff provided labor worth thousands
    of dollars per year — at fair market childcare rates in
    Wayne County ($8-$12/hour per child), Plaintiff's
    childcare over multiple years represents tens of thousands
    of dollars in value — and received nothing.

39. Under Michigan law, unjust enrichment requires: (1) receipt
    of a benefit by the defendant from the plaintiff; (2) an
    inequity resulting to the plaintiff because of the retention
    of the benefit by the defendant. Barber v. SMH (Pontiac),
    202 Mich App 366 (1993). Both elements are satisfied.

COUNT IV — CONVERSION OF GOVERNMENT BENEFITS
(Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

50. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-34.

51. Plaintiff's EBT benefits are government assistance issued
    to Plaintiff for Plaintiff's use. They are Plaintiff's
    property — a chose in action representing Plaintiff's
    entitlement to food assistance under Michigan law.

52. Additionally, SNAP benefits issued in connection with the
    minor children follow the children — not Defendant. Under
    Michigan MDHHS policy, when the children are in the care
    of a household (such as Plaintiff's home on Cornerstone),
    the food assistance designated for those children is
    intended to feed them in that household. When Plaintiff
    watched the children, the SNAP benefits should have been
    feeding them at Plaintiff's table, in Plaintiff's home, at
    Plaintiff's expense of time and labor. Instead, Defendant
    controlled the EBT card remotely and ordered groceries to
    addresses of her choosing. Defendant was not feeding the
    children — she was stealing food from the grandmother who
    was.

53. Defendant Shayla Smith exercised unauthorized dominion and
    control over Plaintiff's EBT benefits by:
    (a) Obtaining access to Plaintiff's EBT card or account
        information;
    (b) Using Plaintiff's benefits to order grocery deliveries
        without Plaintiff's full knowledge of the scope and
        frequency of use;
    (c) Ordering groceries under PLAINTIFF'S name ("Deanna
        Mitchell") to Plaintiff's address using EBT — proving
        Defendant had access to and used Plaintiff's card;
    (d) Ordering groceries under DEFENDANT'S name to both the
        Bradford address and to Plaintiff's address using a
        combination of EBT and credit — mixing government
        benefits with personal spending across multiple
        addresses;
    (e) Submitting ALL of these invoices as Defendant's own
        exhibits in a custody proceeding (Case No. 26-108221-DC,
        Exhibits P-2 and P-3), claiming credit for spending a
        grandmother's food stamps;
    (f) Retaining the benefit of Plaintiff's food assistance
        while contributing nothing to Plaintiff.

54. Under Michigan law, conversion is any distinct act of
    dominion wrongfully exerted over another's personal property
    in denial of or inconsistent with the rights therein.
    Foremost Ins. Co. v. Allstate Ins. Co., 439 Mich 378
    (1992). Defendant's use of Plaintiff's EBT benefits and
    submission of Plaintiff's receipts as her own evidence
    constitutes conversion.

COUNT VI — CIVIL CONSPIRACY
(Against All Defendants)

61. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-34.

62. Defendants Shayla Smith, Leon A. Smith, Marquita L. Smith,
    and Simone Smith acted in concert to exploit Plaintiff's
    labor, benefits, and vulnerability for the benefit of the
    Smith household. Their coordinated conduct includes:

    (a) Accepting years of free childcare from Plaintiff
        while never compensating her — a household-wide
        decision, not the act of any single individual;

    (b) Defendant Shayla Smith's use of Plaintiff's EBT
        benefits, which reduced costs for the Smith household
        economy that all Defendants share;

    (c) The Father's Day ambush (June 21, 2026): Defendant
        Shayla Smith orchestrated the location change to
        Dresden, Defendant Simone Smith served the papers,
        and Defendant Marquita L. Smith watched from inside
        the house — all while Plaintiff continued to provide
        childcare for the Smith family's children;

    (d) The suppression of communication: Defendants routed
        messages through intermediaries (including Plaintiff
        herself) to control Plaintiff's son's access to his
        children and family, isolating the Mitchell household
        while extracting its resources;

    (e) The interception of legal mail (August 21, 2026):
        Defendant Marquita L. Smith intercepted Certified
        Mail Restricted Delivery at the Harper Woods post
        office — the workplace of Defendant Leon A. Smith —
        preventing legal correspondence from reaching its
        addressees, correspondence that could have facilitated
        resolution of the custody dispute affecting
        Plaintiff's grandchildren.

63. Under Michigan law, civil conspiracy requires: (1) a
    concerted action; (2) by a combination of two or more
    persons; (3) to accomplish a criminal or unlawful purpose;
    (4) or to accomplish a lawful purpose by criminal or
    unlawful means. Admiral Ins. Co. v. Columbia Cas. Ins.
    Co., 194 Mich App 300 (1992).

64. The Smith household operated as a coordinated unit to
    extract labor, benefits, and resources from the Mitchell
    household — specifically from Plaintiff, the most
    vulnerable member — while contributing nothing in return,
    while controlling communication, while intercepting mail,
    and while using the legal system as a weapon against
    Plaintiff's son and grandchildren.
