DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR TEMPORARY ORDER
Pursuant to MCR 3.207(B) and MCL 722.27(1)

Defendant DaSean Mitchell, appearing in propria persona, moves
this Court to enter a temporary order establishing custody,
parenting time, and mutual protections pending final hearing,
and states as follows:

1. NO EXISTING ORDER. The parties were never legally married. No
prior custody order exists. Neither parent has court-ordered
custody, and no structure governs the children's care, access to
vital documents, or travel. Both parents and the children require
the certainty and protection of a temporary order while this case
proceeds.

2. STATUS QUO. Defendant has been the primary caregiver of the
minor children since birth. The children currently reside with
Defendant at [REDACTED_ADDR_2], Lansing, MI 48910. They have been
there continuously since on or about June 13, 2026. The children
have neighborhood friends, participate in outdoor activities,
and are integrated into the community. Defendant has a documented
residential lease at this address. Defendant also maintains
access to his mother's residence at XXXX Cornerstone Blvd,
Detroit, MI 48201 (Deanna Mitchell, paternal grandmother), where
the children previously resided with Defendant and where
Defendant's VA-funded classes at DSDT College are located.
Defendant has stable housing in both Lansing and the Detroit
metro area. A temporary order preserving this established
custodial environment is in the children's best interest.

3. CAREGIVING HISTORY. Throughout the parties' nine-year
relationship, Defendant served as the full-time caregiver while
Plaintiff was the household breadwinner employed at Lake Trust
Credit Union. Defendant managed daily routines including meals,
bedtime, school preparation, and medical appointments. This was
a mutually agreed arrangement. The children's primary attachment
has been to Defendant as the consistent at-home parent.

4. PATTERN OF CONTROL. Throughout the relationship, Plaintiff
maintained exclusive control over household finances and vital
documents. Plaintiff stored the children's birth certificates,
Social Security cards, and all vital records in a safe to which
Defendant did not have functional access. Plaintiff controlled the
family vehicle and charged Defendant for its use for work commutes
and the children's transportation. Plaintiff used institutional
systems -- including police (two calls at Bradford Street,
Sterling Heights, zero charges, zero arrests) -- as instruments
of control. Plaintiff's filing of this Complaint follows this
same pattern: the Complaint was signed the day after Plaintiff
gathered intelligence about Defendant's plans and was served on
Father's Day while Defendant waited to see his children.

5. INTERNATIONAL TRAVEL CONCERN. Plaintiff traveled internationally
to Senegal in early June 2026 without informing Defendant.
Plaintiff told Defendant she was "out of state" -- a false
statement. Plaintiff's Complaint requests passport authority for
the minor children. No custody order currently exists to prevent
removal of the children from the jurisdiction.

6. DOCUMENT CONTROL. Plaintiff is in sole possession of the minor
children's birth certificates, Social Security cards, and vital
documents. Defendant does not have access to these records and
requires them to enroll the children in school, obtain medical
care, and exercise his parental rights. Throughout the
relationship, Plaintiff maintained exclusive control of all vital
documents. Notably, Plaintiff provided Defendant's Social Security
number on the Verified Statement filed with this Court --
demonstrating Plaintiff has access to Defendant's personal
identifying information -- while the children's Social Security
numbers, birth certificates, and all other vital records remain
withheld from Defendant. Plaintiff filled in Defendant's
information completely while leaving her own fields and the
children's information blank or inaccessible. This one-way
disclosure underscores the need for a court order compelling
surrender and sharing of all vital documents.

7. FINANCIAL CONCEALMENT. Plaintiff left her own employer and
income fields entirely BLANK on the Verified Statement while
writing "N/A" for Defendant's -- despite Plaintiff's current and
verifiable employment at Lake Trust Credit Union. Plaintiff also
concealed the minor children's SNAP benefits from the Court. This
pattern of selective disclosure on a sworn document raises
concerns about Plaintiff's candor with the Court.

8. COOPERATION BY DEFENDANT. Since the filing of this action,
Defendant has voluntarily facilitated the children's time with
Plaintiff, including sending the children to spend time with
Plaintiff when she has requested it. Defendant is not seeking to
exclude Plaintiff from the children's lives. Defendant seeks a
temporary structure that protects both parents' rights and the
children's stability.


RELIEF REQUESTED

WHEREFORE, Defendant respectfully requests this Court enter the
following temporary orders pending final hearing:

A. Temporary joint legal custody, with Defendant having final
decision-making authority on major decisions (health, education,
welfare) after consultation with Plaintiff, pending final
determination.

B. Temporary primary physical custody to Defendant at House 2
, Lansing, MI 48910, preserving the children's
current established custodial environment.

C. Parenting time for Plaintiff on an alternating-week basis
(every other week), with shared holidays per Friend of the Court
guidelines. Transportation provided by Plaintiff.

D. Mutual passport restriction: neither parent shall apply for,
obtain, or possess passports for the minor children without
written consent of the other parent or further order of the
Court. Any existing passports shall be surrendered to the Court.

E. Neither parent shall remove the minor children from the State
of Michigan without written consent of the other parent or Court
order.

F. Plaintiff shall surrender and share all vital documents in her
possession related to the minor children (birth certificates,
Social Security cards, medical records, immunization records,
school records) within fourteen (14) days. Defendant shall receive
original or certified copies sufficient to exercise parental
rights including school enrollment and medical care.

G. Neither parent shall disparage the other parent in the presence
of the children, interfere with parenting time, or destroy,
conceal, or alter documents or evidence relevant to this
proceeding.

H. Such other and further relief as this Court deems just and
equitable.
