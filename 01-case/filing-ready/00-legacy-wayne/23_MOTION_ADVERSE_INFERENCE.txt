LEGAL STANDARD

6. Under Michigan law, when a party destroys evidence after
being on notice of its relevance to pending or anticipated
litigation, the Court may impose an adverse inference — i.e.,
the Court may instruct that the destroyed evidence is presumed
to have been unfavorable to the party who destroyed it.
Bloemendaal v. Town & Country Sports Center, Inc., 255 Mich App
207, 211 (2002).

7. The elements for an adverse inference are:
   (a) The party had control over the evidence;
   (b) The evidence was relevant to the claims or defenses;
   (c) The party was on notice of the evidence's relevance;
   (d) The evidence was destroyed or made unavailable.
