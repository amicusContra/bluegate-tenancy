COUNT I — PROMISSORY ESTOPPEL (Against Defendant Shayla Smith)

35. Plaintiff re-alleges and incorporates Paragraphs 1-34.

36. Defendant Shayla Smith made clear and definite promises to

37. Defendant Shayla Smith expected or should have reasonably

    serving as primary caregiver, contributing household income,
    and forgoing independent financial development.

38. Plaintiff actually and reasonably relied on Defendant Shayla

39. Injustice can be avoided only by enforcement of Defendant
    Shayla Smith's promises. Defendant retains all financial
    benefits of the partnership (career, 401(k), credit,
    employment). Plaintiff retains none.

40. Under Michigan law, promissory estoppel requires: (1) a
    promise; (2) that the promisor should reasonably have
    expected to induce action of a definite and substantial
    character on the part of the promisee; (3) which in fact
    produced reliance or forbearance of that nature; and (4)
    circumstances requiring enforcement to avoid injustice.
    Nikkel v. Saline Area Schools, 258 Mich App 567 (2003).
    All four elements are satisfied here.
