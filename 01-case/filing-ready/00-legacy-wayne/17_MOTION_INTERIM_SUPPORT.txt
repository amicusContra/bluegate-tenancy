DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR INTERIM CHILD SUPPORT
WITH RETROACTIVE APPLICATION

    Defendant DaSean Mitchell, appearing pro se, respectfully
moves this Court to enter an interim child support order
requiring Plaintiff Shayla Smith to pay child support for the
minor children, retroactive to February 2026, and to reimburse
Defendant for the value of public benefits Plaintiff received
for the children during periods when Defendant bore the
children's daily care costs. Defendant states as follows:

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

1. Defendant has been the primary caregiver of the minor
children, Sahana Saige Mitchell (DOB [date of birth withheld]) and Allanna
Star Mitchell (DOB [date of birth withheld]), for approximately nine years.
The children currently reside primarily with Defendant at House 2
, Lansing, MI 48910.

2. Plaintiff admits in her Answer filed August 12, 2026 that
she has been the "breadwinner" throughout the parties'
relationship (Doc 1, ¶4). Plaintiff is employed full-time at
Lake Trust Credit Union but has never disclosed her income in
any filing with this Court.

3. Plaintiff's Verified Statement (FOC 23) filed June 17, 2026
leaves her employer, occupation, and income fields entirely
blank. She has not filed an amended statement.

4. Defendant filed a IV-D Application (DHS-1201D) with his
pleadings on July 10, 2026, requesting child support services.

THE FEBRUARY 2026 SPLIT

5. Plaintiff acknowledges that the parties separated in
approximately February 2026. Plaintiff paid the final month
of rent on the shared apartment at Bradford and left with
the children. The separation was Plaintiff's unilateral
decision — Defendant was not consulted, and no agreement was
reached regarding financial responsibility for the children
going forward.

6. From February 2026 through the present, Defendant has borne
the primary daily financial responsibility for the children's
needs — food, clothing, housing, transportation, and activities
— on a significantly lower income than Plaintiff's. Defendant's
current income consists of self-employment through Uber.
Plaintiff, the self-admitted "breadwinner" with full-time
employment at a financial institution, has contributed nothing
to Defendant for the children's support since the split.

7. Since approximately June 2026, the parties have operated
under an alternating-week parenting arrangement. Plaintiff
herself acknowledges this arrangement in her Answer (Doc 1,
Factor j). During Defendant's custody weeks, Defendant bears
100% of the children's expenses with no contribution from
Plaintiff.

BENEFITS PLAINTIFF RECEIVED WHILE DEFENDANT BORE COSTS

8. Plaintiff's own MDHHS records (Doc 6) show she received the
following public benefits for the minor children:

    (a) SNAP food assistance (Bridge Card) through approximately
        March 1, 2026 — during which period Defendant was
        providing daily meals for the children;

    (b) Full Medicaid coverage for both children (Allanna,
        Beneficiary ID 1272434853; Sahana, Beneficiary ID
        1272434862) approved April 21, 2026 and effective
        May 1, 2026 — obtained at the Bradford address Plaintiff
        had already vacated in February;

    (c) Cash assistance (FIP) and potential eligibility for
        free school meals and WIC, as documented in MDHHS
        benefits information pages (Doc 6, pp.19-20).

9. Under the alternating-week arrangement, Defendant has
provided care for the children approximately 50% of the time.
Plaintiff received 100% of all public benefits designated for
the children's support. Plaintiff has shared none of these
benefits with Defendant — not food assistance during his weeks,
not Medicaid cards or insurance information for his use, not
any portion of cash assistance. Defendant has been paying for
the children's daily needs out of pocket during his weeks while
Plaintiff collected benefits designated for the same children.

10. To the extent Plaintiff received SNAP, Medicaid, FIP, or
other benefits for the children during periods when Defendant
had physical custody and bore the children's daily living
expenses, Plaintiff was receiving a public subsidy for costs
she was not incurring. Defendant is entitled to reimbursement
for the value of those benefits, or an equivalent credit
against future support.

RETROACTIVE SUPPORT TO FEBRUARY 2026

11. MCL 552.605b(4) provides that support may be ordered
retroactive to the date of filing. However, MCL 552.605b(3)
further provides that the court may consider equitable factors
in determining the support obligation. The equitable factors
here overwhelmingly support retroactive application to the
date of the acknowledged separation:

    (a) Plaintiff chose to leave in February 2026 without
        making any arrangement for support;

    (b) Plaintiff continued to receive public benefits for
        the children while contributing nothing to Defendant;

    (c) Plaintiff concealed her income on her FOC 23 to
        prevent calculation of support;

    (d) Plaintiff filed for custody on June 13, 2026, but
        even after filing, made no voluntary support payments
        to Defendant for the children's care;

    (e) Plaintiff applied for new benefits (Medicaid) on
        April 21, 2026 at a false address — benefits she
        used exclusively while Defendant bore the children's
        daily costs;

    (f) The children's needs existed from the date of
        separation, not the date of filing.

12. From February 2026 through the present (approximately
six months), Defendant has provided food, housing, clothing,
transportation, and activities for the children on Uber
self-employment income while the household's admitted
"breadwinner" contributed nothing and collected public benefits
for children in Defendant's care.

INCOME DETERMINATION

13. The precise amount of support should be calculated using
Plaintiff's actual income, which Plaintiff has refused to
disclose. The Michigan Child Support Formula requires support
to flow from the higher-income parent to the lower-income
parent, proportional to income and parenting time. Defendant
requests that this Court:

    (a) Order Plaintiff to disclose her income for purposes of
        calculating interim support, including wages from Lake
        Trust Credit Union, any secondary employment, and the
        value of all government benefits received;

    (b) Impute income to Plaintiff based on available evidence
        (Lake Trust Credit Union employment, "breadwinner"
        admission, MDHHS records showing employment
        notifications processed) if Plaintiff refuses to
        disclose; or

    (c) Set interim support at a reasonable amount pending
        income disclosure and final calculation by the Friend
        of the Court.

DESIGNATED SUPPORT ACCOUNTS

14. Defendant has established the following accounts at Michigan
State University Federal Credit Union (MSUFCU), Lansing,
Michigan, for the purpose of receiving, managing, and
transparently accounting for child support payments on behalf
of the minor children:

    (a) A dedicated Child Support Operating Account (checking),
        into which all court-ordered child support payments
        shall be deposited. This account is used exclusively
        for the children's current expenses — housing at House 2
, food, childcare, school costs, medical
        expenses, clothing, and transportation. This account
        is separate from Defendant's personal checking account
        and personal income.

    (b) UTMA custodial savings for Sahana Saige Mitchell
        (DOB [date of birth withheld]), DaSean Mitchell as custodian.

    (c) UTMA custodial savings for Allanna Star Mitchell
        (DOB [date of birth withheld]), DaSean Mitchell as custodian.

    All accounts were established on August 22, 2026, and are
    maintained at a federally insured credit union in the
    community where the children reside.

15. Defendant's financial architecture separates personal income
from child support. Uber and wage income deposits into
Defendant's personal checking account. Court-ordered child
support deposits into the Child Support Operating Account. All
child-related expenses draw from the operating account, and
surplus funds at month-end transfer into the children's UTMA
accounts. Annual account statements for all three accounts
shall be made available to Plaintiff and this Court upon
request. This arrangement ensures full transparency,
accountability, and compliance with Michigan's recognition
that child support is the child's right, not the parent's.
Evink v Evink, 214 Mich App 172 (1995).

PRODUCTION OF CHILDREN'S DOCUMENTS

16. Plaintiff is in sole possession of the minor children's
birth certificates, Social Security cards, immunization records,
medical records, and school enrollment documents. Plaintiff has
refused to provide copies of these documents to Defendant despite
repeated requests.

17. Defendant requires these documents to enroll the children in
school, establish medical care, complete the UTMA accounts, and
fulfill his obligations as a parent. Defendant has been compelled
to seek replacement documents independently from Michigan Vital
Records, the Social Security Administration, and the Ingham
County Health Department — at his own expense — because Plaintiff
will not share them.

18. Plaintiff's refusal to produce these documents impairs
Defendant's ability to exercise his parental rights and is
inconsistent with her stated position that "both parents should
be involved in decisions" (Doc 1, Exhibit M-13).

ADDITIONAL FINANCIAL CONCERNS

19. Plaintiff possesses the children's Social Security numbers
and all identifying documents. Plaintiff is employed at Lake
Trust Credit Union, a financial institution, and has professional
knowledge of financial instruments and accounts. Defendant is
concerned that the children's identifying information may have
been used to obtain benefits, open accounts, or establish credit
without Defendant's knowledge or consent. Defendant requests
that this Court order Plaintiff to disclose any and all accounts,
benefits cases, or financial instruments that exist in the
children's names or Social Security numbers.

20. Plaintiff's own exhibits (P-2, P-3) contain Walmart grocery
invoices bearing the name of Defendant's mother, Deanna Mitchell,
for EBT purchases — government food assistance. Plaintiff
submitted these invoices as evidence of her own provision of food
while simultaneously receiving her own public benefits for the
same children. This raises concerns about the use of third-party
government benefits and whether Plaintiff was double-collecting
food assistance — her own benefits plus unauthorized use of
Deanna Mitchell's EBT.

RELIEF REQUESTED

WHEREFORE, Defendant respectfully requests:

A. That this Court enter an interim child support order requiring
   Plaintiff to pay child support to Defendant, retroactive to
   February 2026 (the date of Plaintiff's acknowledged unilateral
   separation), or alternatively to June 13, 2026 (the date
   Plaintiff filed this action, which she initiated the day after
   returning from international travel to Senegal), to be
   deposited into the children's UTMA accounts at MSUFCU;

B. That this Court order Plaintiff to reimburse Defendant for the
   value of public benefits (SNAP, Medicaid, FIP, and any other
   assistance) Plaintiff received for the minor children during
   periods when the children resided with Defendant and Defendant
   bore the children's daily living expenses, said reimbursement
   to be deposited into the children's UTMA accounts;

C. That this Court order Plaintiff to disclose her income,
   including but not limited to wages from Lake Trust Credit
   Union, any secondary employment, and any government benefits
   received, for purposes of calculating support under the
   Michigan Child Support Formula;

D. That this Court order Plaintiff to immediately produce to
   Defendant copies of the following documents for both minor
   children: certified birth certificates, Social Security
   cards, current immunization records, medical and dental
   records (including insurance information), and all school
   enrollment and academic records;

E. That this Court order Plaintiff to disclose any and all
   accounts, benefits cases, loans, or financial instruments
   that exist in the minor children's names or Social Security
   numbers;

F. That retroactive support from February 2026 through the date
   of this Order, plus ongoing interim support, be calculated
   under the Michigan Child Support Formula using Plaintiff's
   actual or imputed income;

G. Such other and further relief as this Court deems just and
   equitable.
