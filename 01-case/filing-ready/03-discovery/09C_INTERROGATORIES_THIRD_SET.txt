INTERROGATORIES

=== THE VEHICLES ===

1.  Prior to the vehicle purchased with your 401(k), the
    parties shared a Mazda. That Mazda was titled in YOUR
    name. The insurance on that Mazda was in DEFENDANT'S name
    through Allstate. Defendant now carries a $347 Allstate
    collection on his credit report — insurance on YOUR car.

    (a) State when the Mazda was acquired and who made the
        payments;
    (b) State why the vehicle was titled in your name while
        the insurance obligation was placed in Defendant's
        name;
    (c) State whether you were aware that Defendant carried
        an insurance obligation on a vehicle titled to you;
    (d) State whether you have made any effort to resolve the
        Allstate collection for insurance on a car that bore
        your name on the title;
    (e) State the current location and disposition of the
        Mazda — whether it was sold, traded, repossessed, or
        remains in your possession.

2.  A later vehicle was purchased using funds from your 401(k).
    That vehicle was sold by Defendant to cover rent on the
    Bradford apartment — a jointly leased apartment you were
    abandoning before the lease was up.

    (a) State the amount withdrawn from your 401(k) for the
        vehicle purchase;
    (b) State whose name the vehicle was titled in;
    (c) State how much Defendant received when he sold it;
    (d) State the final month's rent on Bradford and who paid
        it — and whether you "swooped in" to pay the final
        month after Defendant had already sold the car to
        cover the months you refused to pay;
    (e) After the sale, with only one vehicle remaining —
        yours — Defendant Ubered to and from his job at
        Vascor, working a seven-day supervisor schedule.
        State how much you charged Defendant to use your car,
        how often, and whether you understood that charging
        a man to drive to work — in a relationship where he
        was also the primary caregiver — made his ability to
        earn income contingent on paying you.

=== THE WEDDINGS ===

3.  The parties participated in TWO commitment ceremonies.

    (a) The first was in approximately 2022 in Hawaii.
        Defendant paid for the trip and the wedding. You and
        Defendant split the airline tickets. Defendant now
        carries approximately $2,000 in debt from this
        ceremony — $792 of which is currently held by
        Portfolio Recovery Associates and appears as a
        collection on his credit report.

        State what YOU paid for the Hawaii ceremony. State
        what debt YOU carry from it. State whether the debt
        is $0.

    (b) A second, LOCAL ceremony was held because your family
        — Leon and Marquita Smith — refused to travel to
        Hawaii. Defendant bore the costs of accommodating
        your family's refusal. Your family received TWO
        ceremonies. Defendant received two debts.

        State what your parents contributed to either
        ceremony. State whether they refused to attend
        Hawaii and demanded a local alternative.

    (c) In your sworn Answer (Doc 1), you told this Court
        that the two ceremonies "simply [don't] matter to
        the case." The man who paid for both ceremonies —
        and still carries the debt — disagrees. State why
        a commitment made in front of family and friends,
        celebrated twice, at Defendant's expense, should be
        dismissed as legally irrelevant when determining
        the nature of the parties' relationship.
