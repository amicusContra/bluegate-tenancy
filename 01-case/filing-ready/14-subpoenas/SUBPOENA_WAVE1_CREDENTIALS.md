# Subpoena Wave 1 — Credential Discovery
## Case: Mok v. Banks, 2026-4349-CZ (Ingham County Circuit Court)

**Authority:** MCR 2.305 (Subpoena for Production of Documents)
**FERPA Exception:** 34 CFR § 99.31(a)(9) — disclosure pursuant to lawful subpoena
**Relevance:** Defendant's professional credentials are directly at issue in the complaint. Defendant represents himself publicly as "Dr. Brian Banks, J.D., Ph.D." and claims attorney status on public filings. Plaintiff's complaint alleges credential fraud and unauthorized practice of law.

## PROCEDURE

1. **Draft subpoenas** using Ingham County Circuit Court form (MC 11 / MC 11a)
2. **File with court** — no motion required for document subpoenas under MCR 2.305
3. **Serve** via first class mail on each institution with $20-25 attendance fee (check Ingham County requirements)
4. **Allow 14 days** for production (standard under MCR 2.305)
5. Banks will be notified by the institutions (FERPA requirement) — he can move to quash, but must explain why his own credentials aren't relevant to a case where credential fraud is alleged
6. **If quashed:** The motion to quash itself becomes evidence — "defendant sought to prevent disclosure of his own academic records in a case alleging credential fraud"

## COST

- Filing: $0 (subpoenas don't require a filing fee in Michigan)
- Service: First class mail postage × 3-4 institutions
- Attendance/copying fees: ~$20-25 per institution

## STRATEGIC NOTE

Banks's only move is to try to quash. But quashing requires him to argue that his law school grades, bar exam history, and PhD dissertation are IRRELEVANT to a case where the plaintiff alleges he committed credential fraud. That argument self-destructs on contact.

If he doesn't quash, the records arrive in 14 days. Either way, Kevin wins.
