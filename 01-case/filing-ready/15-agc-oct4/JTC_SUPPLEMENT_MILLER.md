## SUMMARY OF COMPLAINT

Judge Cylenthia LaToye Miller serves as President and Director of the
Board of Purpose Charter Academy — a charter school operated by Brian
Roderick Banks, a nine-time convicted felon classified as a habitual
offender, fourth offense (SID 2029469K).

This board membership is confirmed by Michigan LARA Entity filing
900072324 (Domestic Nonprofit Corporation, filed July 31, 2025).

Judge Miller previously served as the PRESIDING JUDGE of the Third
Circuit Court's Family Division, Juvenile Section from January 2023
to May 2024. Purpose Charter Academy serves Detroit youth, including
potentially youth involved in the juvenile justice system — the very
system Judge Miller presided over.

This complaint alleges violations of Michigan Code of Judicial Conduct
Canon 2 (Appearance of Impropriety), Canon 4(C) (Extrajudicial
Activities), and Canon 7 (Political and Campaign Activities).

## CANON VIOLATIONS ALLEGED

### Canon 2 — Appearance of Impropriety

"A judge should avoid impropriety and the appearance of impropriety
in all activities."

A sitting judge serving as Board President of a school operated by a
nine-time convicted felon — whose network she has been endorsed by —
creates an overwhelming appearance of impropriety. The public cannot
have confidence in Judge Miller's impartiality when she governs a
school alongside an AAG and a City Council member who are all connected
to the same political network.

### Canon 4(C) — Extrajudicial Activities

"A judge shall not serve as an officer, director, trustee, or
nonlegal adviser of an... educational... organization if it is likely
that the organization or its members will come before the judge's
court."

Judge Miller PRESIDED OVER the Juvenile Section of the court that
serves the same youth population PCA targets. Even though she has
moved to the Criminal Division, PCA students who age into the adult
criminal justice system would appear in her current division.

### Canon 7 — Political and Campaign Activities

Judge Miller's re-election campaign accepts endorsements from entities
controlled by the same political network that operates the school she
governs. This blurs the line between judicial impartiality and
political patronage.
