## SUMMARY OF COMPLAINT

Gregory M. Meihn served as board counsel for MacDowell Preparatory Academy and testified as a character witness at the TCR 22-12 administrator certificate hearing (February 9, 2023, MOAHR) in support of Brian Roderick Banks — a nine-time convicted felon (six felony, three misdemeanor) classified as a habitual offender, fourth offense.

Mr. Meihn's testimony was a material factor in Banks receiving a Michigan school administrator certificate over the objections of both the Michigan Department of Education and the Attorney General.

This complaint alleges violations of MRPC 3.3 (Candor Toward the Tribunal), MRPC 8.4(c) (Conduct Involving Dishonesty), and MRPC 1.13 (Organization as Client).

## FACTUAL BASIS

### 1. The TCR 22-12 Testimony (February 9, 2023)

Mr. Meihn testified before MOAHR that Banks demonstrates "integrity, hard work, honesty, transparency, and compliance." He described Banks as a "rare find." He testified to weekly meetings with Banks in his capacity as MacDowell board counsel.

This testimony was delivered on behalf of a man with nine criminal convictions including uttering and publishing, false pretenses, financial transaction device fraud, and NSF check schemes across Oakland, Wayne, and Eaton counties. Banks's 2016 indictment was investigated by an FBI Detroit Area Public Corruption Task Force special agent (SA Peter Ackerly). Banks was classified as a habitual offender — fourth offense.

As board counsel, Mr. Meihn was either:
- (a) Aware of Banks's full criminal history when he testified that Banks demonstrates "integrity" and "honesty" — in which case his testimony was misleading to the tribunal; or
- (b) Unaware of his client's superintendent's full criminal history despite weekly meetings — in which case his representation of the board was inadequate.

### 2. The Management Agreement — §5.2 False Representation

Mr. Meihn testified that he meets with Banks weekly and reviews school operations. In this capacity, he would have reviewed the Management Agreement between The Purpose Group, LLC (Banks's single-member company) and MacDowell Preparatory Academy.

Section 5.2 of the Management Agreement contains a "No Related Parties" representation — stating that no related parties exist between the management company and the school entity.

This representation is false. Banks's co-officer Reginald Dewayne Holland (SID 2321035P, MDOC #443789) — who has six felony convictions including delivery/manufacture of a controlled substance (cocaine) and five personal bankruptcies — appears in all four officer positions (President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer) of The Purpose Foundation, the 501(c)(3) entity that operates MacDowell. Holland and Banks are co-officers of the same nonprofit entity that contracts with Banks's private LLC.

As the attorney who reviews this agreement in weekly meetings, Mr. Meihn either:
- (a) Knew the §5.2 representation was false and took no corrective action — MRPC 8.4(c); or
- (b) Failed to identify the false representation despite weekly review — professional negligence.

### 3. Post-Testimony Conduct by Banks (2023–2026)

Since Mr. Meihn testified that Banks demonstrates "integrity, hard work, honesty, transparency, and compliance," the following has occurred:

- September 9, 2026: Banks told Grosse Pointe Woods police officers "I am a lawyer" (Report #26-7615). He has never been admitted to any bar. The State Bar of Michigan member directory returns zero results.
- August 29, 2026: Banks denied a father's authorized representative access to Purpose Charter Academy using fabricated legal authority.
- September 28, 2026: Banks filed a retaliatory police report (GPW #26-7615) against the complainant.
- Banks's PowerVoter.us candidate filing lists "Attorney, Law Firm of Rodnick, Unger and Kaner, Professional Corporation, present" — a firm that his own 2016 AG indictment identified as fictitious.
- MacDowell operates at 3% math proficiency (statewide rank 1,443 of 1,488) while 72.67% of its $4.9M annual revenue ($4,285,201 in FY2025) flows to Purpose Group LLC — Banks's single-member company.

### 4. Conflict — Organization as Client (MRPC 1.13)

Mr. Meihn's client is the MacDowell Preparatory Academy Board of Directors — not Brian Banks personally. Yet he testified at TCR 22-12 in support of Banks's personal credential, not the school's institutional interests.

The school's institutional interest is in having a qualified, ethical administrator. Mr. Meihn's testimony helped install an administrator who produces 3% math proficiency while extracting 72.67% of revenue through a private LLC. His testimony served Banks's personal interest, not the organization he represents.

## MRPC VIOLATIONS ALLEGED

### MRPC 3.3(a)(1) — Candor Toward the Tribunal
"A lawyer shall not knowingly make a false statement of material fact or law to a tribunal."

Mr. Meihn's testimony that Banks demonstrates "integrity, hard work, honesty, transparency, and compliance" was either knowingly misleading (if he knew Banks's full history) or recklessly delivered without adequate investigation (if he did not).

### MRPC 8.4(c) — Dishonesty, Fraud, Deceit, or Misrepresentation
"It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation."

The §5.2 false "No Related Parties" representation exists in a document Mr. Meihn reviews weekly. His failure to identify or correct it constitutes participation in a misrepresentation.

### MRPC 1.13(b) — Organization as Client
"If a lawyer for an organization knows that an officer, employee, or other person associated with the organization is engaged in action... that is a violation of a legal obligation to the organization, or a violation of law which reasonably might be imputed to the organization... the lawyer shall proceed as is reasonably necessary in the best interest of the organization."

Banks's self-dealing (72.67% revenue extraction through sole-member LLC), false §5.2 representation, and unauthorized practice of law are violations that can be imputed to MacDowell. As board counsel, Mr. Meihn had a duty to act in the organization's interest. His testimony and continued representation served Banks's personal interest instead.
