## SUMMARY OF COMPLAINT

Lamar D. Moreland is a sitting Assistant Attorney General for the State of Michigan. He simultaneously serves as Vice Chair of the Board of Directors for Purpose Charter Academy — a charter school operated by Brian Roderick Banks, a nine-time convicted felon classified as a habitual offender, fourth offense.

Mr. Moreland's office — the Michigan Attorney General — ARGUED AGAINST Banks receiving an administrator certificate at the TCR 22-12 hearing (February 9, 2023). The AG's position was that Banks's criminal record disqualified him. The State Superintendent overruled the ALJ and imposed seven restrictive conditions.

Mr. Moreland now sits on the board of the school operated by the same man his office tried to prevent from holding a school administrator certificate. This creates an institutional conflict that compromises the AG's ability to investigate or prosecute Banks.

This complaint alleges violations of MRPC 8.4(d) (Conduct Prejudicial to the Administration of Justice), MRPC 1.11 (Special Conflicts of Interest for Former and Current Government Officers), and MRPC 1.7 (Conflict of Interest — Current Clients).

## FACTUAL BASIS

### 1. The AG Opposed Banks's Certificate

At the TCR 22-12 hearing (February 9, 2023, MOAHR), the Michigan Attorney General argued against issuing Banks a school administrator certificate. The MDE Department also objected. The Administrative Law Judge recommended no conditions (rubber stamp). State Superintendent Rice overruled the ALJ and imposed seven restrictive conditions.

The AG's position was clear: Banks's criminal record — including eight felony convictions at the time — disqualified him from holding a school administrator certificate.

### 2. Moreland Now Sits on Banks's Board

Despite his office's opposition to Banks's certificate, Mr. Moreland serves as Vice Chair of the Board of Directors of Purpose Charter Academy — the new school Banks founded after receiving the certificate his office opposed.

This is publicly listed at purposecharteracademy.com/boardofdirectors and was reported by Chalkbeat Detroit (September 8, 2025).

### 3. The Institutional Conflict

Mr. Moreland's board position creates the following conflicts:

**Investigative conflict:** If the AG's office receives a referral to investigate Banks (as the complainant has submitted), an AAG sitting on Banks's board creates an institutional conflict. The AG's office cannot investigate its own employee's affiliated organization without a conflict of interest.

**Prosecutorial conflict:** If criminal charges are brought against Banks in any matter within the AG's jurisdiction, Mr. Moreland's board membership taints the office's independence.

**Supervisory conflict:** Mr. Moreland's supervisors at the AG's office — including the Attorney General personally — must now evaluate whether their employee's outside activity compromises their institutional capacity to act.

### 4. The Pattern

Mr. Moreland is not alone on this board. PCA's Board of Directors includes:
- Hon. Cylenthia LaToye Miller (3rd Circuit Court judge) — Board Chair
- Hon. Latisha Johnson (Detroit City Council member) — Secretary
- Mr. Moreland (AAG) — Vice Chair

Three sitting public officials — a judge, an AAG, and a council member — sit on the board of a school operated by a habitual offender. This concentration of public authority on a private board creates the institutional capture documented at detroit.primals.eco/analysis/institutional-capture/.

## MRPC VIOLATIONS ALLEGED

### MRPC 8.4(d) — Conduct Prejudicial to the Administration of Justice
"It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to engage in conduct that is prejudicial to the administration of justice."

An AAG sitting on the board of an entity his office opposed at an administrative hearing prejudices the AG's ability to act on future matters involving that entity. It creates an appearance of impropriety that undermines public confidence in the AG's independence.

### MRPC 1.11 — Special Conflicts of Interest for Current Government Officers
A government lawyer should not participate in a matter in which the lawyer participated personally and substantially while in government service, or use confidential government information to the disadvantage of the government.

Mr. Moreland has access to AG investigative priorities, pending matters, and institutional decision-making. His simultaneous service on Banks's board creates a channel for confidential government information to reach the subject of potential investigation.

### MRPC 1.7 — Conflict of Interest (TRIPLE CONFLICT)
Mr. Moreland owes duties to THREE principals:
(1) the State of Michigan as his employer (AAG),
(2) Purpose Charter Academy as a board fiduciary (Director), AND
(3) Brian Banks as a litigation client (private counsel).

These duties are irreconcilable. As an AAG, his duty is to the public interest.
As a PCA Director, his fiduciary duty is to the school. As Banks's attorney,
his duty is zealous advocacy for a nine-time convicted felon. When the State's
investigative and prosecutorial functions may be directed at both Banks AND the
school Mr. Moreland governs AND the client Mr. Moreland represents, the conflict
is not theoretical — it is structural and ongoing.

His board membership is now confirmed by Michigan LARA Entity filing 900072324
(filed July 31, 2025). This is a state record, not merely a website claim.
