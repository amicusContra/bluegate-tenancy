I. THE INCIDENT

On August 29, 2026, I attended PCA's Back to School Bash at
10101 E. Canfield Street as the authorized representative of
DaSean Mitchell, the father of two minor children enrolled at PCA.
Mr. Mitchell authorized me in writing to attend on his behalf
because he was at work. The authorization referenced the active
Wayne County Circuit Court custody case (Case No. 26-108221-DC)
and included the children's names, dates of birth, and
Mr. Mitchell's identification and contact information.

Brian Banks, who introduced himself as "Dr. Banks," reviewed
my authorization for approximately ten seconds and stated:
"I'm a lawyer."

Banks used this false claim to initially deny me access to the
school event. He evaluated a parental authorization document,
rendered a legal opinion on its sufficiency, and imposed a
consequence — blocking a father's authorized representative from
the school.

Banks has never been admitted to the Michigan Bar. He has never
held a license to practice law in any jurisdiction.

II. BANKS' CRIMINAL HISTORY AND CERTIFICATE CONDITIONS

Banks pled guilty to eight (8) felony counts of fraud in Wayne
County Circuit Court in 2017, arising from a Michigan Attorney
General indictment in 2016. He was originally charged as a
habitual offender.

Despite this history, MDE issued Banks a School Administrator
Certificate on August 7, 2023, with special conditions including
a prohibition on handling cash. As detailed in a concurrent
complaint I have filed with MDE, Banks is in active violation of
this condition — he exercises direct financial control over
approximately $7-8 million in annual public education funds
across MacDowell Preparatory Academy, PCA, and The Purpose Group
Michigan LLC.

III. PATTERN OF FALSE ATTORNEY CLAIMS

Banks' August 29, 2026 claim is not an isolated incident. He has
a documented twenty-year pattern of falsely claiming to be an
attorney:

- 2005-2012: False attorney claims while serving as MI State Rep
- 2010: Fabricated pay stubs from fictitious law firm
- 2012-2017: "JD, Criminal Law" on legislative biography
- 2019, 2023: Reported by M.L. Elrick (Detroit Free Press/Fox 2)
- 2024-present: LinkedIn profile lists "Law" as active skill;
  consulting firm advertises legal services
- August 29, 2026: Told me "I'm a lawyer" at PCA

IV. PARENTAL RIGHTS CONCERN

Mr. Mitchell's children were enrolled at PCA by their mother,
Defendant Shayla Smith, on July 22, 2026 — eight days after
Mr. Mitchell filed a Counterclaim for Custody. Mr. Mitchell was
never informed of the school's name or location. When his
authorized representative arrived at PCA, Banks used false legal
authority to deny access.

MCL 380.1137 guarantees parental access to school records and
information. A school administrator has no authority to render
legal opinions on the validity of a parental authorization — and
certainly no authority to do so while falsely claiming to be a
lawyer.

V. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR DPSCD

DPSCD authorized PCA's charter. DPSCD appoints PCA's board
members as public officials. DPSCD is responsible for monitoring
PCA's compliance with its charter contract and all applicable laws.

The man who founded PCA, who operates it through his management
company (The Purpose Group Michigan LLC), and who runs its
day-to-day operations has:
- 8 felony convictions for financial fraud
- A 20-year history of falsely claiming to be an attorney
- An administrator certificate with a cash-handling prohibition
  he is violating
- Used false legal authority to deny a father's representative
  access to the school

DPSCD collects a 3% authorization fee from PCA (estimated
~$65,000 in Year 1). This fee comes with an oversight obligation.
