## WHAT THIS HEARING IS

This is a **pre-judgment FOC conference/hearing** on Shayla's
Complaint for Custody. There is no custody order yet. This is
the court's first look at the dispute.

**Who runs it:** A FOC referee (Biggar), not a judge. The referee
makes a **recommendation** to the judge. It does not become an
order unless the judge signs it. Either party can object within
21 days and get a **de novo hearing** before the judge.

**What happens:**
1. DaSean states his name, that he represents himself
2. DaSean states what he's asking for and why
3. Shayla (or her side) responds
4. Referee asks questions
5. Referee issues a recommendation

**DaSean's goal is NOT to win custody today.** The goal is:
- Establish legal standing (AOP)
- Get the **FOC custody investigation (F.A.M.E.) referred** —
  DaSean has ALREADY requested this in writing before the hearing
- Request temporary parenting time pending investigation
- Show the referee that one parent has been making all decisions
  unilaterally, and the children's situation needs professional
  investigation before any custody determination
- Keep GAL in pocket as escalation if needed — but lead with FOC

## THE GAL MOTION — HOW TO GET IT LIVE

### When to raise it

**After establishing standing, before discussing custody details.**
The GAL request is the pivot — it shifts the hearing from
"tell me why you should get custody" to "let's investigate before
anyone gets custody."

### The trigger language

MCL 722.24(2) says the court may appoint a GAL when it
"determines that the child's best interests are **inadequately
represented.**"

DaSean must give the referee specific reasons why the children's
interests are inadequately represented. Not vague — specific.

### What DaSean says

"Your Honor, I am requesting the appointment of a Guardian Ad
Litem under MCL 722.24 to represent my daughters' interests in
this proceeding. I believe their best interests are inadequately
represented for the following reasons:"

**Reason 1 — Unilateral school change:**
"After the Plaintiff filed this action, she enrolled both
children in a different school — Purpose Charter Academy —
without my knowledge, without my consent, and without a court
order. I was not informed of this change. I was not consulted.
The children's educational environment was altered by one parent
acting alone."

**Reason 2 — Unilateral residence change:**
"The Plaintiff also changed the children's primary residence
after filing. I was not informed or consulted. The children are
now living at a different address than where they lived when both
parents were co-parenting."

**Reason 3 — Access denied:**
"Since the Plaintiff filed, my regular access to my daughters
has been cut off. I have been their father every day for nine
years. The Plaintiff has unilaterally eliminated my parenting
time without a court order."

**Reason 4 — No investigation has occurred:**
"No custody investigation has been conducted. No professional
has interviewed the children. No one has visited either home.
No one has assessed the children's wishes or needs. Every
decision about these children's lives has been made by one
parent acting alone, and no neutral party has evaluated whether
those decisions serve the children's best interests."

**The close:**
"Your Honor, I am not asking the court to take my word over the
Plaintiff's. I am asking for an independent investigation. I am
confident in what that investigation will find. A GAL will visit
both homes, interview both parents, speak with both children,
and give this court the information it needs to protect Sahana
and Allanna. That is all I am asking for today."

### If the referee says "I'll take it under advisement"

That's not good enough. Push back — politely but firmly:

"Your Honor, I understand. But while this is taken under
advisement, my daughters are living under a custodial arrangement
that was created unilaterally by one parent, without
investigation, without my input, and without a court order. Every
day that passes without a GAL or investigation is another day the
children's interests go unexamined. I respectfully ask the court
to act today."

### If the referee says cost is an issue

MCL 722.24(4) says costs are assessed "after a determination of
ability to pay." DaSean can say:

"Your Honor, I understand there are costs associated with a GAL
appointment. I am willing to contribute to those costs within my
ability to pay, and I ask the court to make that determination.
The cost of a GAL is far less than the cost to my daughters of
having their lives decided without independent investigation."

"Your Honor, I also request that the Friend of the Court conduct
a custody and parenting time investigation under MCL 552.505.

is my right under the statute."

**Why both GAL and FOC investigation?**
- The FOC investigation is the standard process — the court
  expects it
- The GAL is the elevated request — it gives the children their
  own attorney
- Requesting both shows the referee that DaSean isn't trying to
  avoid scrutiny — he's inviting it
- If the referee grants the FOC investigation but not the GAL,
  that's still a win — the investigation visits both homes

## TEMPORARY PARENTING TIME

After the GAL/investigation request, DaSean asks for temporary
parenting time pending investigation:

"Your Honor, while the investigation is pending, I am requesting
temporary parenting time consistent with the arrangement that
existed before the Plaintiff filed this action. For nine years,
I was an active, daily co-parent. I am asking for structured
parenting time — every other weekend, one weeknight, and
alternating holidays — so that my daughters maintain their
relationship with their father while the court gathers the
information it needs."

**Why structured, not "reasonable":**
The Michigan Parenting Time Guideline recommends specific
schedules over "reasonable" parenting time. A specific schedule
prevents Shayla from defining "reasonable" to mean "never."

## WHAT CAN GO WRONG

### Shayla claims DaSean abandoned the children
"Your Honor, I did not abandon my children. I was co-parenting
until the Plaintiff cut off my access after filing. I am here
today asking for that access to be restored."

### Referee says "this is just a preliminary conference"
"I understand, Your Honor. I am not asking for a final custody
determination today. I am asking for three things: a GAL
appointment, a FOC investigation, and temporary parenting time
while those proceed. All three can be ordered today."

### Shayla brings surprise witnesses (Leon, Marquita, etc.)
Object: "Your Honor, I provided my witness list to the Plaintiff
and the FOC on [date]. I received no witness notice from the
Plaintiff. I object to testimony from any witness I was not
given proper notice of."
If overruled — note it, write down the name, preserve for de novo.
The contrast still works: DaSean followed the rules, Shayla didn't.

### Shayla has an attorney and DaSean doesn't
Don't be intimidated. The referee has seen hundreds of pro se
parents. Be prepared. Be specific. Cite the statutes. Let the
lawyer talk — then respond to what they actually said, not what
they implied.

### Referee denies the GAL
Ask for the FOC investigation instead — MCL 552.505(1)(g). That's
the fallback. The investigation still visits both homes.

If both are denied, ask for the basis of the denial on the record.
Then file a written objection within 21 days and request a de
novo hearing before the judge.

### Referee grants "reasonable parenting time" with no specifics
Push for specifics: "Your Honor, I respectfully request a
specific schedule rather than 'reasonable' parenting time. The
Parenting Time Guideline recommends specific schedules to reduce
conflict. Given the current communication difficulties, a
specific schedule will best serve my daughters' stability."
