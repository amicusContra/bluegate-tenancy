## THE STATUTES

### 1. AOP Establishes Full Parental Rights — MCL 722.1003 + 722.1004

**MCL 722.1003(1):** "If a child is born out of wedlock, a man is
considered to be the natural father of that child if the man joins
with the mother of the child and acknowledges that child as his
child by completing a form that is an acknowledgment of parentage."

**MCL 722.1004:** "An acknowledgment that complies with this act
and is filed with the state registrar establishes parentage and is
**the equivalent to an adjudication of parentage** of the child
and **confers on the acknowledged parent all rights and duties of
a parent**, and the acknowledgment may be the basis for court
ordered child support, custody, or parenting time **without
further adjudication** under the paternity act."

"The child who is the subject of the acknowledgment bears the
same relationship to the birth parent and the acknowledged parent
**as a child born or conceived during a marriage** and has the
**identical status, rights, and duties** of a child born in lawful
wedlock effective from birth."

**What this means for DaSean:**
- He signed the AOP at the hospital for both Sahana (2017) and
  Allanna (2019)
- He is the legal father. Full stop.
- His rights are IDENTICAL to a married father's rights
- No further adjudication of paternity is needed
- He can seek custody and parenting time on the same footing as
  the mother

### 2. Mother's "Initial Custody" — MCL 722.1006

**MCL 722.1006:** "After completion of an acknowledgement of
parentage... the mother has initial custody of the minor child,
**without prejudice to the determination of either parent's
custodial rights**, until otherwise determined by the court or
otherwise agreed upon by the parties in writing and acknowledged
by the court. **This grant of initial custody to the mother does
not, by itself, affect the rights of either parent** in a
proceeding to seek a court order for custody or parenting time."

**What this means:**
- YES, the statute gives the mother initial custody on separation
- BUT this is a DEFAULT — it exists "until otherwise determined by
  the court"
- It does NOT mean sole custody. It does NOT mean permanent custody.
- It does NOT diminish DaSean's right to seek custody or
  parenting time
- The statute explicitly says it does not "by itself, affect the
  rights of either parent"
- It is a placeholder — not a ruling

**What Shayla will argue:** "I have initial custody under MCL
722.1006, therefore I have sole authority."

**Why that fails:** MCL 722.1006 says "without prejudice" and
"does not, by itself, affect the rights of either parent." The
initial custody grant is a starting point, not an endpoint. It
exists to prevent a legal vacuum when parents separate — it does
not award sole custody, and it does not bar the father from
exercising parental rights or seeking a custody order.

### 3. Established Custodial Environment — MCL 722.27(1)(c)

**MCL 722.27(1)(c):** "The court shall not modify or amend its
previous judgments or orders or issue a new order so as to change
the established custodial environment of a child unless there is
presented **clear and convincing evidence** that it is in the best
interest of the child."

"The **custodial environment of a child is established** if over
an **appreciable time** the child naturally looks to the custodian
in that environment for **guidance, discipline, the necessities of
life, and parental comfort.** The age of the child, the physical
environment, and the inclination of the custodian and the child as
to permanency of the relationship shall also be considered."

**Key case law:**

- **Foskett v Foskett, 247 Mich App 1 (2001):** An established
  custodial environment (ECE) **can exist with BOTH parents.** It
  may exist with one, both, or neither parent.

- **Berger, 277 Mich App 706:** An ECE "may be established **as a
  result of a temporary custody order, in violation of a custody
  order, or in the absence of a custody order.**"

- **Vodvarka v Grasmeyer, 259 Mich App 499 (2003):** The movant
  bears the burden of proving proper cause or change of
  circumstances by a preponderance of the evidence BEFORE the
  court can even consider whether to change custody. These initial
  steps are "intended to **erect a barrier against removal of a
  child from an established custodial environment** and to minimize
  unwarranted and disruptive changes of custody orders." (citing
  Baker v Baker, 411 Mich 567, 577 (1981)).

- **Pierron v Pierron, 486 Mich 81 (2010):** The court is
  "required to determine whether there is an established custodial
  environment with one or both parents **before making any custody
  determination.**"

- **Shimel v McKinley (COA Docket 329144):** A school change that
  alters the ECE requires the moving party to prove by **clear and
  convincing evidence** that the change is in the child's best
  interests.

## THE BURDEN OF PROOF — WHO BEARS IT

| Scenario | Burden | Standard |
|----------|--------|----------|
| No prior custody order exists | Court determines best interests | Preponderance |
| Prior order exists, no ECE | Movant proves change is in best interests | Preponderance |
| Prior order exists, ECE exists with one parent | Movant proves change is in best interests | **Clear and convincing** |
| Prior order exists, ECE exists with BOTH parents | Movant proves change is in best interests | **Clear and convincing** |

**In DaSean's case:**
There is no prior custody order. This is an initial custody
proceeding. The court must FIRST determine whether an ECE exists
and with whom, then apply the appropriate standard.

If the court finds an ECE existed in BOTH homes (DaSean's and
Shayla's) — which it did — then any party seeking to CHANGE that
arrangement must prove by **clear and convincing evidence** that
the change serves the children's best interests.

## THE SCHOOL CHANGE — WHY IT MATTERS

Under Pierron and Shimel, a unilateral school change that alters
the established custodial environment triggers the clear and
convincing standard.

**Timeline:**

## THE GAL MOTION — WHY IT WINS

### The Statute

**MCL 722.24(2):** "If, at any time in the proceeding, the court
determines that the child's best interests are **inadequately
represented**, the court **may appoint a lawyer-guardian ad litem**
to represent the child."

**MCL 712A.17d(1)(c):** The GAL's duty includes "conducting an
**independent investigation** including, but not limited to,
**interviewing the child, social workers, family members, and
others** as necessary, and reviewing relevant reports."

**MCL 712A.17d(1)(d):** The GAL must "meet with or observe the
child and **assess the child's needs and wishes**."

**MCL 722.24(4):** Costs "may be assessed against one or more of
the parties" after "a determination of ability to pay."

### Why DaSean Demands the GAL

The GAL is the strategic centerpiece. Here's why:

1. **It shifts the investigation from DaSean to a professional.**
   DaSean doesn't have to prove everything himself. A licensed
   attorney investigates both homes, both parents, interviews the
   children, and reports to the court. DaSean just has to show up
   and be himself.

2. **It exposes Shayla's home.** The GAL visits BOTH homes. The
   GAL sees DaSean's setup at [REDACTED_ADDR_2] (stable, furnished,
   children's beds, neighbor friends, Lansing schools) vs.
   whatever is happening at Dresden (Leon and Marquita's house,
   Leon's assault history, Banks's involvement).

3. **It gives the children a voice.** Sahana is 9. She can tell
   the GAL who her father is, who takes care of her, who she
   feels safe with. The GAL is legally obligated to assess the
   children's wishes — MCL 712A.17d(1)(d).

4. **It forces the court to apply the best-interest factors.**
   MCL 722.23 lists 12 factors. Once a GAL is appointed, the
   court gets a professional analysis of ALL 12 — not just
   whatever Shayla claims in her filings.

5. **It neutralizes Shayla's document control.** Shayla has
   controlled every piece of paper — school enrollment, medical
   records, benefits applications. The GAL has independent
   authority to obtain and review documents directly. Shayla can't
   gatekeep the GAL the way she gatekeeps DaSean.

6. **DaSean has nothing to hide.** He was the stay-at-home
   parent. He has housing. He has a vehicle. He has a job. He has
   children's savings accounts. He has a support network. A GAL
   investigation can only help him.
