IV. THE CLAIMS ARE LEGALLY SUFFICIENT — MCR 2.116(C)(8)

21. Under MCR 2.116(C)(8), the Court accepts all well-pleaded
    factual allegations as true and determines whether the complaint
    states a claim as a matter of law. The Court does not weigh
    evidence or credibility. Maiden v. Rozwood, 461 Mich 109, 119
    (1999).

   A. COUNT V — CONVERSION AND FRAUD (MAIL)

22. Defendants argue that 18 USC §§ 1702 and 1708 do not provide
    a private cause of action. Plaintiff acknowledges this and has
    reframed Count V in the SAC under Michigan common law:

    (a) **Conversion** — Defendant Leon Smith exercised unauthorized
    dominion and control over Plaintiff's mail by signing return
    receipts in the names of other persons, defeating the Restricted
    Delivery service Plaintiff purchased. Thoma v. Tracy Motor Sales,
    Inc., 360 Mich 434, 438 (1960) (conversion is any distinct act
    of dominion wrongfully exerted over another's personal property).

    (b) **Fraud** — Defendant Leon Smith knowingly misrepresented
    his identity on USPS return receipts, representing that proper
    delivery had occurred. Plaintiff relied on the forged receipts.
    Plaintiff suffered damages (false service proof, additional costs
    for alternative service).

    (c) **Tortious interference with legal proceedings** — The mail
    contained Requests for Admission, the complaint, and summons.
    The forgery interfered with Plaintiff's ability to prove service.

    (d) **MCL 600.2907** — Civil action for damages caused by
    criminal conduct. The forgery of signatures on USPS documents
    constitutes forgery (MCL 750.248).

23. Defendant Marquita Smith is jointly liable: she admits that
    Leon signed "with their consent" (Answer ¶36) and that she is
    aware mail at the address is accessible to Leon (RFA #23).

   B. COUNTS I-II — EAVESDROPPING AND INTRUSION UPON SECLUSION
