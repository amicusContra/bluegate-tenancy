KEY EXCERPTS RELEVANT TO ADMINISTRATOR CERTIFICATE CONDITIONS:

"Banks had been trying to get back into elected office since he
resigned as a state representative in 2017 as part of a plea deal
that allowed him to avoid up to 14 years in prison for defrauding
a credit union. He ran for state Senate in 2018, but failed to win
the Democratic nomination. On Aug. 2, 2022, he was defeated in his
bid to be the Democratic nominee for the Wayne County Commission's
1st District. Ten days later, on Aug. 12, 2022, Banks applied for
a school administrator certificate, according to the Michigan
Department of Education."

"Banks' school administrator's permit was due to expire at the end
of this month. But he doesn't have to worry about that, because
his school administrator's certificate was issued on Aug. 7. That
certificate is good for five years. Banks now uses the title
'principal.'"

MDE SPOKESMAN BILL DiSESSA ON CERTIFICATE CONDITIONS:

"In this case, the required background checks were completed and
reported to (Michigan Department of Education's) Office of
Educator Excellence. Dr. Banks noted his convictions on his
administrator certificate application. But as is typically the
case when an applicant has a record of convictions, the OEE held
a compliance conference to determine whether he qualified for a
school administrator certificate. Dr. Banks then requested a
hearing before an (administrative law judge), who ruled in favor
of his application, with a determination that he had turned his
life around."

"DiSessa said state Superintendent Dr. Michael Rice ultimately
decided that enough time had passed since Banks' most recent
conviction — which was six years ago — and that 'his efforts to
improve his life in the interim' merited granting Banks a school
administrator certificate 'with conditions.'"

"THOSE CONDITIONS INCLUDE AVOIDING MISDEMEANOR OR FELONY
CONVICTIONS FOR FINANCIAL CRIMES AND FORBIDDING HIM TO HANDLE
CASH."
