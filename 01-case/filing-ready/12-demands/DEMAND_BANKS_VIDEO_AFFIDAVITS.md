## III. LEGAL BASIS

### A. Broad Scope of Discovery

Under **MCR 2.302(B)(1)**, parties may obtain discovery regarding any
matter not privileged that is relevant to the subject matter of the
pending action. You have placed your conduct, your claimed evidence,
your knowledge of the case, and your relationship with Shayla Smith
directly at issue by your statements to law enforcement and to the Court.

### B. You Referenced This Evidence in Court

At the September 29, 2026 PPO hearing, you told Judge Koenig you possess
witnesses with signed affidavits and video. You also made these claims
to DaSean Mitchell in person. Having affirmatively referenced evidence
in a judicial proceeding, you cannot withhold it from discovery.
**MCR 2.302(E)** requires supplementation of responses when prior
responses are incomplete.

### C. Authentication Requirements

Under **MRE 901(a)**, evidence must be authenticated as a condition
precedent to admissibility. For video recordings, this requires
testimony establishing the accuracy and reliability of the recording.
*People v Berkey*, 437 Mich 40 (1991). I will challenge the
authenticity, chain of custody, and completeness of any video produced.

### D. Your "Unauthorized" Defense Is Moot

You claimed I was not authorized to be at the school. However:

- DaSean Mitchell is the **legal father** of both enrolled students,
  established by Affidavit of Parentage under MCL 722.1003 and
  MCL 722.1004.
- I carried **written authorization** from the legal father and
  documentation of his parental rights.
- You **admitted full knowledge of the custody case** at the September 29
  hearing.
- You **knew or should have known** that DaSean Mitchell had legal
  parental rights and that I was acting at his direction.
- You chose to call police, file a false report, claim to be an
  attorney, and obstruct a legal parent's access to his children's
  school.

A school superintendent who admits full knowledge of a custody dispute,
who knows the father has legal parental rights, and who nonetheless
calls police on the father's authorized representative is not acting in
good faith protection of students — he is acting as an agent of one
parent against another.
