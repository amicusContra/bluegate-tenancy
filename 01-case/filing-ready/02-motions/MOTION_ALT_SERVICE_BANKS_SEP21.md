## I. LEGAL AUTHORITY

Michigan Court Rule **MCR 2.105(J)** provides:

> *"On a showing that service of process cannot reasonably be made as provided by this rule, the court may by order permit service of process to be made in any other manner reasonably calculated to give the defendant actual notice of the proceedings and an opportunity to be heard."*

The standard for alternative service requires only that the method be "reasonably calculated" to provide actual notice. *Richards v. Tibaldi*, 272 Mich App 522, 531 (2006). Where a respondent affirmatively evades service or refuses to accept certified mail, alternative methods including email, fax, and first-class mail are appropriate. *Holliday v. Townley*, 189 Mich App 424 (1991).

For Personal Protection Orders, MCR 3.705(A)(2) provides that service may be made by any method "reasonably calculated to give the respondent actual notice." The standard is actual notice, not formal perfection.
