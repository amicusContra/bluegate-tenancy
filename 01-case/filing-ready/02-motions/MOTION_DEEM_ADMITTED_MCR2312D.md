II. LEGAL STANDARD

5. MCR 2.312(B)(1) provides:

   "If a matter is not admitted, the answer must specifically deny
   the matter or set forth in detail the reasons why the answering
   party cannot truthfully admit or deny the matter."

   (Emphasis added.)

6. A bare denial that does not "specifically deny the matter" or
   "set forth in detail the reasons" for the inability to admit or
   deny is insufficient. The answering party must "fairly meet the
   substance of the request." MCR 2.312(B)(1).

7. MCR 2.312(D) provides:

   "If a party fails to admit the genuineness of a document or the
   truth of a matter as requested under this rule, and if the party
   requesting the admission later proves the genuineness of the
   document or the truth of the matter, the party requesting the
   admission may move for an order requiring the other party to pay
   the reasonable expenses incurred in making the proof, including
   reasonable attorney fees."

8. Additionally, the court has inherent authority under MCR 2.312
   to deem matters admitted when a party's response fails to comply
   with the rule's requirements, as an insufficient response is
   treated as no response at all. See Janczyk v. Davis, 125 Mich
   App 683 (1983) (evasive or insufficient answers to discovery may
   be treated as failures to respond).

    (a) **RFAs are not subject to proportionality objections.**
    MCR 2.312 governs Requests for Admission independently of the
    general discovery proportionality provisions of MCR 2.302(B)(1).
    An RFA asks a party to admit or deny a specific fact. The
    responding party must do so or explain why it cannot. A bare
    "relevance" objection is not authorized by MCR 2.312.
