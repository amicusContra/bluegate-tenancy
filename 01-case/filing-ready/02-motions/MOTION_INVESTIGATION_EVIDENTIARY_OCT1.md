DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR FRIEND OF THE COURT
CUSTODY INVESTIGATION AND EVIDENTIARY HEARING

NOW COMES Defendant DaSean Eddie Mitchell, appearing pro se,
and respectfully moves this Court for entry of an order:

  (1) Directing the Friend of the Court to conduct a custody
      and parenting time investigation pursuant to
      MCL 552.505(1)(g); and

  (2) Scheduling an evidentiary hearing on custody pursuant
      to MCR 3.210(C)(1).

In support, Defendant states as follows:

FACTS

1. Plaintiff and Defendant are the parents of two minor
   children: Sahana Mitchell (DOB [date of birth withheld]) and Allanna
   Mitchell (DOB [date of birth withheld]).

2. Defendant signed the Acknowledgment of Parentage at the
   hospital for both children. Plaintiff's own Complaint at
   Paragraph 6 states the parties "acknowledged paternity by
   signing an affidavit of parentage." Plaintiff's sworn
   Answer confirms: "Paternity was acknowledged." Both birth
   certificates list Defendant as the father.

3. For approximately nine years, the parties co-parented the
   children without a court order. Both parents provided daily
   care. An established custodial environment existed in both
   homes. The children looked to both parents for guidance,
   discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort.

4. After Plaintiff filed this action for sole custody,
   Plaintiff unilaterally and without court order:

   a. Changed the children's school enrollment;
   b. Changed the children's primary residence; and
   c. Terminated Defendant's regular parenting time.

5. None of these changes were ordered by a court. None were
   agreed to by Defendant. Defendant was not informed or
   consulted before any of these changes were made.

6. The school in which Plaintiff enrolled the children — without
   Defendant's knowledge or consent — has since become the
   subject of regulatory inquiries by multiple state agencies
   and professional oversight bodies. The operator's
   qualifications, board governance, and compliance with
   charter authorization requirements are under review. These
   concerns further underscore the need for a professional
   custody investigation that includes assessment of the
   children's school environment.

7. No custody investigation has been conducted. No professional
   has visited either home, interviewed either parent, or
   assessed the children's needs or wishes.

ARGUMENT

I. THE COURT SHOULD ORDER A FOC CUSTODY INVESTIGATION

8. MCL 552.505(1)(g) provides that the Friend of the Court
   shall "investigate all relevant facts" and make a written
   report and recommendation on contested custody matters.

9. MCL 552.505(1)(g) further provides that the investigation
   "shall include a meeting with the party who requested the
   investigation, unless that party does not attend a
   scheduled meeting."

10. Defendant requests that the Friend of the Court conduct a
    full custody and parenting time investigation, including
    home visits to both residences, interviews with both
    parents and the children, review of all relevant facts,
    and assessment of the children's current school
    environment.

11. Defendant requests that the investigation include a meeting
    with Defendant, as is his statutory right.

12. Defendant further requests referral to the Family
    Assessment, Mediation and Evaluation (F.A.M.E.) program
    for a comprehensive assessment of the children's best
    interests under the twelve factors set forth in
    MCL 722.23.

II. THE COURT MUST HOLD AN EVIDENTIARY HEARING

13. MCR 3.210(C)(1) provides: "the court may not enter an
    order resolving the contested matter that changes a
    child's established custodial environment without first
    holding an evidentiary hearing."

14. An established custodial environment existed in both
    parents' homes. The children looked to both parents for
    guidance, discipline, the necessities of life, and
    parental comfort. See Berger v Berger, 277 Mich App 700,
    707 (2008) (ECE can exist without a court order); Foskett
    v Foskett, 247 Mich App 1, 10 (2001) (ECE can exist in
    both homes simultaneously).

15. Plaintiff's Complaint for sole custody, if granted, would
    change the established custodial environment. Under
    Pierron v Pierron, 486 Mich 81, 92-93 (2010), the court
    must first determine whether an ECE exists, and if so,
    may only change it upon clear and convincing evidence that
    the change serves the children's best interests.

16. MCR 3.210(C)(1) further provides that "a hearing on the
    matter must be held within 56 days" of the filing of the
    motion or response raising the contested issue.

17. Defendant has fact witnesses with personal knowledge of
    Defendant's parenting, including a witness who maintained
    a contemporaneous parenting journal with timestamped
    entries over the past year. These witnesses can only be
    heard at an evidentiary hearing.

RELIEF REQUESTED

WHEREFORE, Defendant respectfully requests that the Court:

  A. Order the Friend of the Court to conduct a custody and
     parenting time investigation under MCL 552.505(1)(g),
     including a meeting with Defendant;

  B. Refer this matter to the F.A.M.E. program for
     comprehensive assessment;

  C. Schedule an evidentiary hearing on custody and parenting
     time within 56 days pursuant to MCR 3.210(C)(1);

  D. Pending investigation and hearing, enter a temporary
     order preserving Defendant's parenting time consistent
     with the arrangement that existed before Plaintiff's
     unilateral changes — specifically, every other weekend,
     one weeknight, and alternating holidays; and

  E. Grant such other relief as the Court deems just and
     equitable.

## INTERNAL NOTES — NOT FILED

### What this does strategically

This motion puts TWO things on the court record BEFORE Oct 7:

1. **FOC Investigation** — the referee sees DaSean already
   requested it formally. MCL 552.505 says "shall investigate."
   The statute makes it mandatory once requested. DaSean isn't
   asking the referee for a favor — he's citing the statute that
   requires it.

2. **Evidentiary Hearing** — MCR 3.210(C)(1) says the court
   CANNOT enter a custody order changing ECE without one. By
   filing this motion, DaSean triggers the 56-day clock. The
   court must schedule the hearing within 56 days of this filing.
   That's the hearing where Kevin, Deanna, and the full witness
   bench testify.

3. **School environment** — ¶6 puts the school concerns on the
   record WITHOUT naming Banks or making claims the court would
   need to verify. It says "regulatory inquiries" and "under
   review" — both factually true. This tells the F.A.M.E.
   investigator to look at the school environment, which is
   their job under MCL 722.23 Factor (h)/(l) anyway.
