III. CANONS OF JUDICIAL CONDUCT IMPLICATED

This conduct implicates Judge Miller's extrajudicial obligations under the
Michigan Code of Judicial Conduct:

    Canon 1 — A judge shall uphold the integrity and independence of the
    judiciary. Chairing the board of a school where the operator falsely
    claims attorney status to law enforcement undermines public confidence
    in judicial integrity.

    Canon 2 — A judge shall avoid impropriety and the appearance of
    impropriety in all activities. A sitting circuit judge lending governance
    authority to a convicted felon's school — where that felon commits
    unauthorized practice of law on campus — creates an appearance of
    impropriety that cannot be reconciled with judicial office.

    Canon 4 — A judge's extrajudicial activities shall not cast doubt on
    the judge's capacity to act impartially, demean the judicial office, or
    interfere with the proper performance of judicial duties. Judge Miller's
    role as Board Chair of PCA is not a neutral community service; it is
    direct governance of the institution where this misconduct occurred.

This is the equivalent of a hospital board chaired by a licensed physician
and a state health official, allowing an unlicensed person with a medical
degree but no license — and a prior conviction for misconduct — to perform
examinations on patients while wearing a lab coat, under the implied
authority of the board's credentials. No medical board would tolerate this.
