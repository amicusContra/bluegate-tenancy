## II. CORRECTION — AFFIDAVIT OF PARENTAGE

During the hearing, the Court raised the issue of paternity
acknowledgment under MCL 722.1003, and Petitioner testified that
he did not have an Affidavit of Parentage. **Petitioner
respectfully corrects this testimony.**

### A. Why Petitioner Did Not Know

When Petitioner was served with Respondent Shayla Smith's Complaint
for Custody in Wayne County Case No. 26-108221-DC, the service
packet he received (the "1st Copy - Defendant") contained only:
the Summons, the Complaint (2 pages), the Parenting Time
Attachment, the Verified Statement (2 pages with addendum), and
the PII Addendum. **No Affidavit of Parentage was included in the
papers served on Petitioner,** despite the Complaint stating that
the AOP "accompanies the complaint."

The Affidavit of Parentage was a document Petitioner was previously
unaware existed as a separate legal instrument. Petitioner and
Ms. Smith were in a committed relationship for nine years. They
held two wedding ceremonies. They raised two daughters together.
Petitioner was their primary caretaker. For nine years, Petitioner
had no reason to believe he needed a specific legal document to
prove he was their father — he trusted the relationship and the
life they had built together.

He signed paperwork at the hospital when his daughters were born
in 2017 and 2019, but did not know the formal legal name or
significance of that document. When the relationship ended and he
needed to establish his parental rights through the courts, he
obtained birth certificates from Oakland County — where both
children were born — because those were the documents he knew to
request. Petitioner has been trying to establish his rights through
proper legal channels since the beginning of this case.

Upon review of the case file, Petitioner has now confirmed:

### B. Shayla Smith's Own Filing States the AOP Exists

The Plaintiff's Complaint for Custody (Form CC-MLH 429), filed by
Shayla Smith in Wayne County, states at **Paragraph 6**:

> "The plaintiff and the defendant acknowledged paternity by
> signing an affidavit of parentage for the following minor
> child(ren): **Sahana Mitchell and Allanna Mitchell**"

The Complaint further states:

> "A copy of the affidavit of parentage for each minor child
> accompanies the complaint."

### C. Shayla Smith's Answer in This Court Confirms It

In her Answer to DaSean Mitchell's Counterclaim filed in Case No.
26-108221-DC, Shayla Smith stated at **Paragraph 1**:

> "Plaintiff admits that Defendant is the father of the minor
> children and that **paternity was acknowledged.**"

### D. Respondent Shayla Smith Withheld the AOP from Petitioner

Petitioner's copy of the complaint — the "1st Copy - Defendant"
served on him — does not include the Affidavit of Parentage.
Respondent Smith's complaint states that the AOP "accompanies the
complaint," yet it was not included in the papers served on
Petitioner. The served copy contains only: the Summons, the
Complaint (2 pages), the Parenting Time Attachment, the Verified
Statement (2 pages with addendum), and the PII Addendum. No
Affidavit of Parentage was served.

Petitioner signed the AOPs at the hospital at the time of each
child's birth — in 2017 and 2019. He had no reason to know the
formal legal name of that document or to retain a copy. Respondent
Smith controlled all parental paperwork and chose not to provide
the AOP to Petitioner, even as she referenced it in her own filing.

### E. Petitioner Is Now Aware Documents Were Withheld

Since the September 29 hearing, Petitioner has reviewed the
Respondent's filings and now understands the full scope of the
withholding:

1. Respondent Smith referenced the AOP in her Complaint (¶6) and
   stated it "accompanies the complaint."
2. Respondent Smith did NOT include the AOP in the papers served
   on Petitioner.
3. Respondent Smith confirmed under oath in her Answer that
   "paternity was acknowledged" — yet never provided the document
   to Petitioner.
4. Respondent Smith was present via Zoom on September 29 when
   the Court raised the AOP issue and Petitioner — who did not
   have the document because Respondent withheld it — stated he
   did not have one. Respondent remained silent.

**Petitioner has now taken the following steps to obtain the AOP:**

- Submitted a records request to the Wayne County Circuit Court
  Clerk for the complete case file in 26-108221-DC, including the
  AOP that Respondent claims was filed with the complaint.
- Submitted a request to MDHHS Division of Vital Records for
  certified copies of the Acknowledgments of Parentage for both
  children. Current MDHHS processing times are approximately
  8-12 weeks.
- **Served a formal demand on Respondent Smith** to produce the
  Affidavits of Parentage she referenced in her own Complaint and
  confirmed under oath. Respondent is in possession of these
  documents or knows their location. She can produce them
  immediately.

Petitioner should not be penalized for not possessing a document
that the opposing party referenced in her own filing, confirmed
under oath, and then withheld from service.

### F. Respondent Shayla Smith's Silence at the Hearing

Respondent Smith was present at the September 29, 2026 hearing via
Zoom. When the Court raised the Affidavit of Parentage issue and
Petitioner stated he did not have one, Respondent Smith — who had
filed the complaint referencing the AOP, who had withheld it from

Respondent Smith knew the AOP existed because she referenced it in

The Court's findings regarding paternity at the September 29

### G. Legal Effect

Under **MCL 722.1003(1)**, an Acknowledgment of Parentage signed
by both parents establishes the man as the legal father. The
Affidavit of Parentage for both Sahana Mitchell and Allanna
Mitchell:

- Was signed by both parents
- Was attached to Respondent Smith's own Wayne County complaint
  as required by law
- Is on file with the Michigan Department of Health and Human
  Services (Vital Records)
- Was independently confirmed by Respondent Smith in two separate
  sworn filings

Petitioner is a **legally recognized father** under Michigan law.
His testimony at the hearing that he did not have an Affidavit of
Parentage was made in error — he had signed the documents at the
time of each child's birth but did not recall the formal name of
the document during testimony.

## III. ROLE CLARIFICATION — KEVIN MOK

Petitioner respectfully acknowledges the Court's guidance regarding
the unauthorized practice of law concern. Petitioner offers the
following clarification:

### A. Who Kevin Mok Is

Kevin Mok is Petitioner's close friend. Both are United States Army
veterans. Mr. Mok is not an attorney and has never claimed to be
one. Mr. Mok is a witness to many of the events described in the
PPO petitions, including the September 2, 2026 assault and the
ongoing pattern of harassment.

Mr. Mok is also a pro se plaintiff in his own civil cases pending
in Ingham County (Case Nos. 2026-4301-CZ and 2026-4349-CZ),
arising from the same events and parties at issue in these PPO
proceedings.

### B. Document Preparation

Mr. Mok assists Petitioner with organizing facts, researching law,
and drafting filings. **All documents bearing Petitioner's
signature were reviewed by Petitioner, approved by Petitioner, and
submitted by Petitioner with his full knowledge and consent.** The
substance of every filing reflects Petitioner's own account and his
own decisions.

### C. The Filing Error

In preparing filings for his own Ingham County civil cases,
Mr. Mok drafted approximately three motions and supporting
affidavits. Mr. Mok then cross-filed this same set of documents
onto Petitioner's PPO cases, believing that because the facts
overlapped, the filings were relevant to both proceedings.

**This was wrong.** Those motions and affidavits were prepared for
Mr. Mok's own civil cases and should not have been filed in
Petitioner's PPO cases. The cross-filing was Mr. Mok's error —
a misunderstanding of the boundary between assisting a friend with
his own filings and filing documents in another person's case.
Mr. Mok did not consult Petitioner before cross-filing these
documents. It was not an intentional unauthorized practice of law,
but it was a line that should not have been crossed.

The Court correctly treated these cross-filed documents as unused
and moot at the September 29 hearing. They did not factor into the
Court's determination, which was the right call.

### D. What Was Proper and What Was Not

Petitioner and Mr. Mok developed the following working process:
Petitioner and Mr. Mok would discuss the facts and issues together.
Mr. Mok would help draft filings. Petitioner would review, revise,
and sign them. Mr. Mok would then deliver the signed filings to
the courthouse and arrange service, as Mr. Mok has a flexible
schedule and Petitioner works full-time.

**What was proper:**
- Mr. Mok helping Petitioner discuss and organize facts
- Mr. Mok assisting with drafting filings that Petitioner reviewed
  and signed
- Mr. Mok delivering Petitioner's signed documents to the court
  as a courier
- Mr. Mok mailing service copies on Petitioner's behalf

**What was not proper:**
- Mr. Mok cross-filing motions and affidavits from his own cases
  onto Petitioner's PPO docket
- Documents bearing Mr. Mok's name appearing in Petitioner's cases
  as though Mr. Mok were a party

This was the most recent series of filings in these cases, and
Petitioner acknowledges the error in full.

### E. Responsibility and the Court's Admonishment

Petitioner wants to be direct with this Court: the cross-filing of
Mr. Mok's documents onto Petitioner's cases was Mr. Mok's error.
Mr. Mok prepared those motions and affidavits for his own Ingham
County civil cases and filed them on Petitioner's PPO docket on his
own initiative, without Petitioner's knowledge or authorization.
Petitioner did not direct Mr. Mok to file in Petitioner's name and
did not know the filings had been made until the Court raised the
issue at the September 29 hearing.

When the Court admonished Petitioner regarding the unauthorized
practice of law concern, Petitioner accepted that admonishment —
and Mr. Mok, who was present, accepted it as well. Both understood
immediately that the line had been crossed and that Mr. Mok bears
responsibility for crossing it. This was Mr. Mok's mistake, and
Petitioner and Mr. Mok have addressed it directly between
themselves.

**Going forward, Petitioner wants and expects Mr. Mok's role to
be limited to that of a fact witness.** Mr. Mok will testify to
what he has personally observed — Petitioner's parenting, the
children's wellbeing, events he witnessed firsthand — and nothing
more. Mr. Mok will not file documents in Petitioner's cases, will
not appear as a party or submitter, and will not cross-file from
his own proceedings.

Separately, when Mr. Mok visited Purpose Charter Academy regarding
the enrollment of Petitioner's children, he did so carrying written
authorization from Petitioner — the children's legal father — and
documentation establishing Petitioner's parental rights. That visit
was at Petitioner's direction. Petitioner is prepared to provide
copies of the parental authorization letter to the Court upon
request.
