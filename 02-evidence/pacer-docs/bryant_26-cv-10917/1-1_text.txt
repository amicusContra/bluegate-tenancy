Definitions. The following terms as used in this Note shall have the meanings set forth below:
“Bank” means PNC Bank, National Association and its successors and assigns.

“Business Day” means any day other than a Saturday or a Sunday on which banks in Detroit,
Michigan are required to be open for business, and on which banks in London, England, settle payments.

“Default Rate” means the greatest of (i) the PNC Prime Rate plus 3.0%; (ii) the Overnight Bank
Funding Rate plus 3.5%; and (iii) 7.0%.

“Electronic Signature” means an electronic sound, symbol, or process attached to, or associated
with, a contract or other record and adopted by a Person with the intent to sign, authenticate or accept
such contract or record.

"Interest Payment Date" means the first Business Day of each month, commencing September 1,
2021.

"Interest Period" means (i) initially the period from the Date of Original Issue specified above
through August 30, 2021 and (ii) thereafter, the first Business Day of each calendar month to but not
including the first Business Day of the following month.

“LIBOR” shall mean, for each Reset Date, the interest rate per annum determined by PNC Bank,
National Association (the "Bank") by dividing (i) the rate which appears on the Bloomberg Page BBAM1
(or on such other substitute Bloomberg page that displays rates at which US dollar deposits are offered by
leading banks in the London interbank deposit market), or the rate which is quoted by another source
selected by the Bank as an authorized information vendor for the purpose of displaying rates at which US
dollar deposits are offered by leading banks in the London interbank deposit market (an “Alternate
Source”), at approximately 11:00 a.m., London time, two (2) Business Days prior to such Reset Date, as
the I-month, London interbank offered rate for U.S. Dollars commencing on such Reset Date (or if there
shall at any time, for any reason, no longer exist a Bloomberg Page BBAM1 (or any substitute page) or
any Alternate Source, a comparable replacement rate determined by the Bank at such time (which
determination shall be conclusive absent manifest error)), by (ii) a number equal to 1.00 minus the
LIBOR Reserve Percentage; provided, however, if LIBOR, determined as provided above, would be less
than zero then LIBOR shall be deemed to be zero. LIBOR shall be adjusted automatically without notice
to the Treasurer on and as of (a) each Reset Date, and (b) the effective date of any change in the LIBOR
Reserve Percentage.

“LIBOR Breakage Fee” means any liabilities, losses or expenses (including, without limitation,
loss of margin, any loss or expense incurred liquidating or employing deposits from third parties, and any

payment on the due date thereof, (ii) the Treasurer's revocation (expressly, by later inconsistent notices or
otherwise) in whole or in part of any notice given to the Bank to request, convert, renew or prepay the
Note, or (iii) the Treasurer's payment or prepayment on a day other than the regularly scheduled due date
therefor. A notice as to any LIBOR Breakage Fee payable pursuant to this paragraph given to the
Treasurer by the Bank shall, in the absence of manifest error, be conclusive and payable upon demand.
The Treasurer's obligations hereunder shall survive the payment in full of the Note and all other amounts
payable hereunder.

“LIBOR Reserve Percentage” shall mean, as of any day, the maximum effective percentage in

marginal and emergency reserve requirements) with respect to eurocurrency funding (currently referred to

“Overnight Bank Funding Rate” shall mean, for any day, the rate comprised of both overnight
federal funds and overnight Eurocurrency borrowings by U.S.-managed banking offices of depository
institutions, as such composite rate shall be determined by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York
(“NYFRB”), as set forth on its public website from time to time, and as published on the next succeeding
Business Day as the overnight bank funding rate by the NYFRB (or by such other recognized electronic
source (such as Bloomberg) selected by the Bank for the purpose of displaying such rate); provided, that
if such day is not a Business Day, the Overnight Bank Funding Rate for such day shall be such rate on the
immediately preceding Business Day; provided, further, that if such rate shall at any time, for any reason,
no longer exist, a comparable replacement rate determined by the Bank at such time (which determination
shall be conclusive absent manifest error). If the Overnight Bank Funding Rate determined as above
would be less than zero, then such rate shall be deemed to be zero. The rate of interest charged shall! be
adjusted as of each Business Day based on changes in the Overnight Bank Funding Rate without notice to
the Treasurer.

“Person” means any natural person, corporation, limited liability company, trust, joint venture,
association, company, partnership, Governmental Authority or other entity.

"Prime Rate” means the rate publicly announced by the Bank from time to time as its prime rate.
The Prime Rate is determined from time to time by the Bank as a means of pricing some loans to its
borrowers. The Prime Rate is not tied to any external rate of interest or index, and does not necessarily
reflect the lowest rate of interest actually charged by the Bank to any particular class or category of
customers.

“Reset Date” shall mean (i) the date of this Note, and (ii) subject to the proviso below, the first

calendar month, in which case the next preceding day that is a Business Day shall instead apply, and (b) if
any such day is a day of a calendar month for which there is no numerically corresponding day in certain
other months (each, a “Non-Conforming Month’), then any Reset Date that falls within a Non-
Conforming Month shall be the last Business Day of such Non-Conforming Month.

Credit overview

Wayne County’s financial position has improved materially since execution of the county's consent agreement and
recovery plan in 2016. Through this process the county was able to substantially reduce health care costs and
long-term liabilities and it has since continued to maintain structural balance, increase reserves, and improve its
pension and other postemployment funding position, while also addressing its exaninaljustiee facility needs. Wayne
County's strong management team put the county on a stable and sustainable financial footing, and over the past
seven years financial performance has improved materially.

Our rating on Wayne County remains constrained by long-term economic recovery prospects that are still somewhat
uncertain given declining population, an outsized dependence on the auto industry, historical economic volatility, and
above-average sensitivity to economic swings, especially in light of the county's limited ability to generate additional
revenue without tax base growth under the current funding framework and Headlee limitations.

Additional factors for the ‘A’ rating include our opinion of Wayne County's:

¢ Economy, marked by below-average incomes and property wealth, declining population, yet at the center of a broad
and diverse metropolitan statistical area (MSA) that is gradually improving;

¢ Strong financial performance supported by robust financial flexibility and liquidity;

* Strong management practices and policies;

° Weak debt and contingent liabilities profile and sizable, although much improved, pension and other
postemployment benefit liabilities (OPEB).

Environmental, social, and governance

The health and safety aspects of the COVID-19 pandemic caused a slowdown in revenue growth and are reflected as a
social risk in our analysis of environmental, social, and governance risks. We believe Wayne County faces higher social
risks than most in the sector, factoring in a long-term outmigration of the population, along with an ageing
infrastructure, with increasing number of roads and bridges in poor to critical condition. These conditions could limit
economic growth in the long term and thereby the county's budget. Although we view Wayne County's overall
governance as having strengthened since 2015 it is consistent with our view of the sector.

We see governance as a strength, with strong fiscal controls and commitment to a long-term vision that is keeping the
county's rebound on track. Various areas of the Wayne County have experienced increased flooding that we see as
more related to infrastructure than high environmental risks, and therefore we do not consider there to be high
environmental risk.

Stable Outlook

Upside scenario
If the county continues to sustain its very strong financial profile and economic conditions continue to improve or
remain favorable, we could consider a higher rating.

The Office of Budget and Finance is responsible for the processing and recording of accounting and financial

Accounts Payable

The Accounts Payable Unit is responsible for processing payments to all contractors and vendors that provide

Bureau (ITSB) to automate the payment processes for high volume requests from vendors who provide language
interpretation services and additional contractual services that are otherwise processed manually.

Implementing the automated payment system for these high-volume requests represents a significant technological
improvement, considering the Court has successfully implemented automated payment processes for per diem
court reporters, transcript fees and court officers.

Although the Accounts Payable Unit processes vendor, contractual, and attomey fee payments for ail Court
divisions, the payment of attomey fees for services rendered at the Criminal Division transitioned to Wayne
County’s Indigent Defense Services Department in fiscal year 2023-24.

Court Collections

The Collections Unit, which is responsible for the collection of court-imposed costs, fines, and fees, is one of the top
priorities of the Court. In February 2015, the Collections Unit devoted staff to the Criminal Division in order to
increase collection efforts. During fiscal year 2024, nearly $1.7 million was collected in court costs and late fees in
the Criminal Division. In addition, the unit mailed out 7,800 delinquency notices and established an additional 373
payment agreements; bringing the total payment agreements initiated by the Collections Unit to over 3,500. Court
collections transitioned to the new Criminal Justice Center near the end of fiscal year 2024.

A. MCR 6.310(B)(1): Withdrawal of Plea Before Sentencing

Under MCR 6.310(B)(1), a trial court must permit a defendant to withdraw a guilty plea
before sentencing if the defendant presents a “fair and just reason” for withdrawal and the
prosecution cannot demonstrate substantial prejudice resulting from reliance on the plea.
Michigan courts apply this standard liberally prior to sentencing, recognizing that the defendant’s
liberty interests and the integrity of the judicial process are paramount at this stage. See People v.
Davidovich, 463 Mich 446, 452 (2000) (holding that plea withdrawal should be allowed before
sentencing where a fair and just reason is shown and the prosecution cannot demonstrate
substantial prejudice).

A guilty plea entered without disclosure of material facts bearing on the neutrality of the
tribunal satisfies this standard as a matter of law. Due process requires that a plea be knowing,
voluntary, and entered before an impartial adjudicator. Where a defendant was not informed of
circumstances creating a potential structural conflict of interest affecting the presiding judge, the
voluntariness of the plea is necessarily undermined.

B. Due Process Prohibits a Financially Interested Tribunal

D. Structural Due Process Violation — No Harmless Error Analysis

Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279, 309-310 (1991) distinguishes ‘trial errors' from
‘structural defects’ that 'affect the framework within which the trial proceeds’ and 'defy analysis
by harmless-error standards.' In re Murchison, 349 U.S. 133, 136 (1955) established that '[a] fair
trial in a fair tribunal is a basic requirement of due process’ and that denial of an impartial
adjudicator is the paradigmatic structural defect. Weaver v. Massachusetts, 582 U.S. 286, 295
(2017) reaffirmed that structural errors 'affect the framework within which the trial proceeds,
rather than simply an error in the trial process itself’ and ‘are so serious that they are inherently
prejudicial.' A structural error requires automatic reversal because it ‘necessarily render[s] a
criminal trial fundamentally unfair’ regardless of the particular facts. Id. at 300. The financial
conflict in this case is not an isolated error — it infected every proceeding from arraignment
through plea acceptance. No harmless error analysis applies; vacatur and dismissal are

mandatory.
