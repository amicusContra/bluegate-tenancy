PRELIMINARY STATEMENT

1. This is a civil rights action under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 challenging a structural
institutional financial conflict of interest that pervades the criminal justice system of Wayne
County, Michigan, and — as the State’s own Judicial Council has acknowledged — every circuit

court in the State of Michigan.

2. Plaintiff Jeremiah Levi Bryant does not ask this Court to declare him innocent or to
overturn his conviction. He asks this Court to: (a) declare that the financial architecture under
which he was prosecuted violates the Due Process Clause; (b) order that any further proceedings
in his case take place before an unconflicted tribunal; and (c) award damages for the
constitutional injury of being subjected to a structurally biased process — regardless of the
outcome of his criminal case. See Wilkinson v. Dotson, 544 U.S. 74, 82 (2005) (§ 1983 claims
seeking new procedures, rather than immediate release, are not barred by Heck v. Humphrey),
Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247, 266 (1978) (damages available for deprivation of procedural due

process even absent proof of actual injury).

3. This complaint does not relitigate guilt or innocence. It asserts the foundational
principle that ‘[a] fair trial in a fair tribunal is a basic requirement of due process.’ In re
Murchison, 349 U.S. 133, 136 (1955). The right to an impartial judge is a structural guarantee
that ‘defy[ies] analysis by harmless-error standards’ because it ‘affect[s] the framework within

which the trial proceeds.’ Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279, 309-310 (1991).

THI TION IS N A Y HE Y

41. In Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477, 486-487 (1994), the Supreme Court held that a
§ 1983 plaintiff cannot recover damages for harm whose unlawfulness would render a conviction
or sentence invalid, unless the conviction has already been invalidated. This action is not

Heck-barred for four independent reasons.

42. First, Plaintiff seeks procedures, not release. This action seeks new procedures —
specifically, a hearing before an unconflicted tribunal — not release from custody or invalidation
of the conviction. In Wilkinson v. Dotson, 544 U.S. 74, 82 (2005), the Supreme Court held that §
1983 claims seeking ‘new eligibility review’ or ‘a new .. . hearing’ are not Heck-barred because
‘success . . . does not necessarily imply the invalidity of [the] conviction.’ A new hearing before

an unconflicted judge might result in the same plea, a different plea, or a trial — the outcome is

not predetermined. See also Skinner v. Switzer, 562 U.S. 521, 534 (2011) (claim does not

‘necessarily imply’ invalidity where relief could lead to either exoneration or reaffirmation).

43. Second, there is no final conviction. No sentence has been imposed. Heck’s
favorable-termination requirement applies to ‘conviction or sentence.’ 512 U.S. at 486-487.
Where no final conviction exists because sentencing has not occurred, Heck’s bar does not
attach. See Powers v. Hamilton County Public Defender Comm’n, 501 F.3d 592, 602-603 (6th

Cir. 2007).

44, Third, the due process claim is independent of guilt or innocence. The right to an
impartial tribunal exists independent of guilt or innocence. A defendant who is factually guilty
has the same constitutional right to a conflict-free judge as one who is factually innocent. Tumey,
273 U.S. at 532. Success on this claim does not ‘necessarily imply’ the conviction is invalid — it

implies only that the process was constitutionally deficient. See Heck, 512 U.S. at 487 n.7.

45. Fourth, the systemic claims are categorically outside Heck. The prospective
injunctive and declaratory relief claims against Boyd and Cavanagh (Counts V and VI) challenge
the ongoing statewide court funding system. These systemic claims are categorically outside

Heck because they do not challenge any individual conviction. Wilkinson, 544 U.S. at 81-82.

2. What can the City of Detroit and DAH do to better assist seniors in maintaining their homes and

DAH is an independent hearings tribunal and blight violation notices (BVNs) are adjudicated in
our Blight Administrative Hearings Bureau (a/k/a Blight Court). DAH’s main role in blight
enforcement is to conduct timely, impartial, and fair hearings on BVNs, using state and local law.
BVNs, which are issued by ticket issuing agencies (e.g., DPD, BSEED), are also referred to as blight
tickets. BVNs are based on the physical condition of real property—not on property owner
demographics such as age or ability, which are not included in deeds or other legal property
transfer documents.

While DAH defers to the executive and legislative branches and their partnerships with nonprofit
and advocacy organizations for the development of direct, “boots on the ground” assistance
programs for property owners such as seniors, veterans, single parents, and individuals with
disabilities, we remain supportive of such efforts. Although DAH, functioning as a Blight Court,
does not have the operational authority to implement property assistance programs, we do
share available resource materials with Respondents, including seniors. We welcome any
additional informational materials or public-private initiatives that help at-risk populations
address blight-related issues. DAH fully supports broader community efforts to assist vulnerable
groups in complying with property maintenance standards, avoiding blight violations, and
resolving existing blighted conditions.

Major Initiatives
Significant major initiatives include:

- Wayne County is prioritizing safe, attainable, affordable, and stable housing as a core focus of its ongoing budget
strategy. To advance this goal, the County has activated the Wayne County Housing Commission to lead a coordinated,
countywide approach to address housing challenges. The Commission is charged with aligning financial and
programmatic resources, shaping effective policy, and coordinating with local, state, and federal partners to expand
housing access and protect vulnerable populations.

- In Fiscal Years 2023 and 2024, the County made record-level investments in housing projects across all regions. These
commitments mark the foundation of a long-term strategy to increase housing stability, improve quality of life, and

- ensure every Wayne County resident has access to a safe and secure home. Continued budget allocations will support
the Housing Commission’s work and leverage additional funding to maximize impact in future years.

- Judicial expense increased $28.7 million mostly from increases in operating costs due to the compensation adjustments, retention

22. SIGNIFICANT TRANSACTIONS
Wayne County - Criminal Justice Center

The Criminal Justice Center (CJC), which opened in September 2024, was the largest construction preject in the-County’s history. Now
fully operational, the CJC is a state-of-the-art complex that serves as the centralized hub for the Ceunty’s criminal justice system. This
was a joint project between the County and Rock Economic Development Group (Rock). The new facility was built on the land parcels
obtained from the City of Detroit in a land swap agreement. The original agreement called for the County to contribute $401.3 million
cash and four properties (Division | jail, Division II jail, Juvenile Detention Facility, and Frank Murphy Hall of Justice); Rock was responsible
for any cost overruns, unless change was mandated by the County. Subsequent to the original agreement, the County amended it's contract
with Rock Development LLC and it's sister company Rock Parking Company LLC, to buy out Rock's interest in operating offsite parking lots.
Per the amended agreement, the County contributed additional monies of $28.0 million to the CJC Construction Fund to cover the cost of
the transaction. The $401.3 mitlion County cost was covered with the remaining 2010 bond proceeds ($57.5 million), proceeds from the
sale of Gratiot Avenue Unfinished Jail ($21.4 million), new bond issuance ($291.7 million) and General Fund revenue ($30.6 million). Due
to required design modifications, which resulted in additional change orders, the County's actual contribution was $444.3 million as of
September 30, 2024. The CJC includes 2,280-bed jail, 25 courtrooms and five hearing rooms, Sherriffs and Prosecutor's department offices,
and a 160-bed juvenile detention facility.

As part of the CJC campus, the County constructed a Central Utility Plant (CUP) to provide utilities to the CJC. The County negotiated
agreements with DTE and Rock for the construction of the CUP. The CUP facility has been built directly next to the CJC, which reduced
the construction costs. DTE was responsible for the building of the CUP except for the outer shell of the facility. As part of the agreements
with DTE, the County has signed multi-year agreements for utilities to be provided by DTE. In its agreement to build the CUP shell, Rock
has contributed approximately $2.5 million of the construction costs.

Opioid Settlement

Wayne County is a part of litigation brought by states and local political subdivisions against the manufacturers and distributors of opioids
as well as several pharmacy chains. During the year ended September 30, 2024, the County recognized $13.0 million of settlement
proceeds revenue and has a receivable of $42.5 million for amounts to be collected over the next 14 years. The County expects to receive
additional proceeds from settlements entered into after September 30, 2024, as weil as other amounts not yet recognized in accordance
with generally accepted accounting principles.

Extraordinary Optional Redemption Due to Tax Law Change

Due to tax law change, if the United States Department of Treasury or any agency of the United
States of America at any time cease to remit to the Authority all or any part of the interest rate subsidy
payable with respect to the Bonds in accordance with Section 54AA of the Code, for any reason other
than one attributable to the action or inaction of the Authority, the Authority has the nght to redeem and
retire all or any part of the principal amount of the Bonds then outstanding in multiples of $5,000 within a
single maturity in such order of maturity as the Authority shall determine and within a single maturity on
any date at a redemption price equal to the Extraordinary Redemption Price.

The "Extraordinary Redemption Price" is equal to the greater of:

(ld) the issue price of the Bonds set forth on the cover page hereof (but not less than 100%) of
the principal amount of the Bonds to be redeemed; or

(2) the sum of the present value of the remaining scheduled payments of principal and
interest on the Bonds to be redeemed to the maturity date of such Bonds, not including any portion of
those payments of interest accrued and unpaid as of the date on which the Bonds are to be redeemed,
discounted to the date on which the Bonds are to be redeemed on a semi-annual basis, assuming a 360-
day year containing twelve 30-day months, at the Treasury Rate (as defined below) plus 100 basis points,
plus in each case accrued interest on the Bonds to be redeemed to the redemption date.

The Authority will not exercise, the extraordinary optional redemption of the Bonds if the
Extraordinary Redemption Price is in excess of the maximum redemption price authorized by law
(currently 103%).

The "Treasury Rate" is, with respect to any redemption date for a particular Bond, the yield to
maturity as of such redemption date of United States Treasury securities with a constant maturity,
excluding inflation indexed securities (as compiled and published in the most recent Federal Reserve
Statistical Release H.15 (519) that has become publicly available at least two business days prior to the
redemption date or, if such Statistical Release is no longer published, any publicly available source of
similar market data) most nearly equal to the period from the redemption date to the matunty date of the
Bond to be redeemed; provided, however, that if the period from the redemption date to such matunty
date is less than one year, the weekly average yield on actually traded United States Treasury securities
adjusted to a constant maturity of one year will be used.

The redemption price of Bonds to be redeemed pursuant to the extraordinary optional redemption
provision described above will be determined by an independent accounting firm, investment banking
firm or financial advisor retained by the Authority at the County's expense to calculate such redemption
price. The Transfer Agent, the Authority and the County may conclusively rely on such determination of
redemption price by such independent accounting firm, investment banking firm or financial advisor and

TAX PROCEDURES

Article IX, Section 3, of the Michigan Constitution provides that the proportion of true cash value
at which property shall be assessed shall not exceed 50% of true cash value. The Michigan Legislature by
statute has provided that property shall be assessed at 50% of its true cash value, except as described
below. The Michigan Legislature or the electorate may at some future time reduce the percentage below
50% of true cash value.

On March 15, 1994, the electors of the State approved an amendment to the Michigan
Constitution permitting the Legislature to authorize ad valorem taxes on a non-uniform basis. The
legislation implementing this constitutional amendment added a new measure of property value known as
"Taxable Value.” Beginning in 1995, taxable property has two valuations -- State equalized valuation
("SEV") and Taxable Value. Property taxes are levied on Taxable Value. Generally, Taxable Value of
property is the lesser of (a) the Taxable Value of the property in the immediately preceding year, adjusted
for losses, and increased by the lesser of the inflation rate or 5%, plus additions, or (b) the property's
current SEV. Under certain circumstances, therefore, the Taxable Value of property may be different
from the same property's SEV.

When property is sold or transferred, Taxable Value is adjusted to the SEV, which under existing
law is 50% of the current true cash value. The Taxable Value of new construction is equal to current
SEV. Taxable Value and SEV of existing property are also adjusted annually for additions and losses.

1. All debt service on tax-supported bonds issued prior to December 23, 1978 or tax-
supported issues which have been approved by the voters for which the issuer has
pledged its full faith and-credit.

2. Operating purposes for a specified period of time if said increase is approved by a
majority of the qualified electors of the local unit.
