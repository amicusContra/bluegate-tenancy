**Business**: Homegrown (cannabis dispensary), Lansing, MI
**Reporter**: Kevin Mok (longtime customer, community member)
**Source**: DaSean Mitchell (employee, first shift June 30, 2026)

## Observed / Reported Conditions

| Issue | Detail | Legal Standard |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| **No water provided** | Employees must bring coolers from home. Employer does not provide potable water on-site. 90+ degree outdoor temps. | **VIOLATION: 29 CFR 1910.141(b)(1)** — "Potable water shall be provided in all places of employment." OSHA General Duty Clause also applies. |
| **Minimal shade** | Insufficient shade or cooling areas for employees working in heat. | **MIOSHA General Duty Clause (MCL 408.1011(a))** — employer must provide workplace free from recognized hazards. Heat + no shade + no water = recognized hazard. |
| **One 15-min break per 7-hr shift** | Single 15-minute break on a 7-hour shift. | For adults 18+: Michigan has no mandatory break law (legal but exploitative). **For minors under 18: VIOLATION of MCL 409.112** — requires 30-minute uninterrupted break after 5 consecutive hours. |
| **Phone confiscation** | Employees required to surrender/confiscate personal phones during shift. | No direct statute prohibits this for adults. However: confiscating communication devices in a heat hazard environment removes employees' ability to call 911 during a medical emergency. Compounds OSHA liability. |
| **No safety supplies** | No first aid, no cooling supplies, no heat illness prevention materials provided. | **MIOSHA Part 472, Rule 325.47201(2)** — employers must ensure first aid supplies are readily available and at least one person is trained in first aid. |

## Who This Affects

DaSean described the conditions as a "sweatshop." Kevin has been
a customer for years and observes that Homegrown employs many
young people. If ANY employee is under 18:

- The 15-minute break on a 7-hour shift violates Michigan law
- The Youth Employment Standards Act (MCL 409.112) is not
  optional — it's criminal liability for the employer
- Michigan Wage & Hour Division enforces this

Even for adult employees, the lack of water in 90+ degree heat
is a federal OSHA violation regardless of age, industry, or
state.

## Relevant Law

### Federal — OSHA

**General Duty Clause, Section 5(a)(1) of the OSH Act of 1970:**
> Employers are required to provide their employees with a place
> of employment that "is free from recognized hazards that are
> causing or likely to cause death or serious harm to employees."

**29 CFR 1910.141(b)(1) — Sanitation:**
> "Potable drinking water shall be provided in all places of
> employment."

**OSHA Heat Illness Prevention** (federal guidance):
- Provide water, rest, shade
- Plan for emergencies
- Train workers on heat illness symptoms
- Acclimatize new/returning workers

### Michigan — MIOSHA

**MCL 408.1011(a) — General Duty:**
> "An employer shall furnish to each employee, employment and a
> place of employment that is free from recognized hazards that
> are causing, or are likely to cause, death or serious physical
> harm to the employee."

**MIOSHA State Emphasis Program (SEP) on Heat:**
Michigan adopted federal OSHA's National Emphasis Program on
heat hazards. MIOSHA inspectors actively target heat-related
workplace violations. Employers can be cited under the General
Duty Clause for failing to provide water, shade, rest, training,
or emergency response in heat conditions.

### Michigan — Youth Employment

**MCL 409.112 (Youth Employment Standards Act):**
> "A minor shall not be employed for more than 5 hours
> continuously without an interval of at least 30 minutes for a
> meal and rest period."

If Homegrown employs anyone under 18, the current break policy
(one 15-min break per 7-hr shift) is a direct statutory
violation.

## Action Plan

### Phase 1: Employee Awareness (immediate)

Prepare a one-page employee rights sheet that DaSean (or Kevin)
can share with coworkers. Cover:

- Your right to potable water (federal law, not employer's
  choice)
- Break rights for minors under 18
- How to file a MIOSHA complaint (anonymous, online)
- Heat illness symptoms and when to stop working
- Your right to refuse unsafe work under OSHA Section 11(c)

### Phase 2: Owner Outreach (Kevin → Homegrown ownership)

Kevin is a longtime customer with standing in the community.
Approach is constructive, not adversarial:

- "I've been coming here for years. I want this place to
  succeed. Here's what I'm hearing from employees."
- Provide the legal requirements (water, breaks for minors)
- Offer to help — this isn't a threat, it's a correction
- Frame it as liability protection for the owner (MIOSHA
  fines, OSHA citations, workers' comp claims)

### Phase 3: Escalation (if owner is unresponsive)

- **MIOSHA complaint** (anonymous): michigan.gov/leo → MIOSHA
  → File a Complaint. Can be filed online or by phone.
  Phone: (800) 866-4674
- **Michigan Wage & Hour Division** (for minor break
  violations): (855) 464-9243
- **OSHA complaint** (federal): osha.gov/workers/file-complaint
  or call (800) 321-6742

### Phase 4: Documentation

- DaSean photographs conditions when safe to do so (no water
  station, no shade structures, etc.)
- Note dates, shift times, break times, temperatures
- Note ages of coworkers if known (minor labor law)
- Keep all pay stubs and schedules
