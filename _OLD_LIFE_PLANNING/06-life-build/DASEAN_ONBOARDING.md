### Liberal Consideration (Hagel/Kurta/Wilkie Memos)

Three DOD memoranda (2014, 2017, 2018) require review boards to give
**favorable presumption** to upgrade applications involving:

- PTSD
- TBI (Traumatic Brain Injury)
- Mental health conditions broadly
- Military Sexual Trauma

If DaSean experienced any mental health effects from service (even undiagnosed),
these memos apply. The board must "resolve doubts and inferences in favor of
the veteran." Cite all three memos in the cover letter.

Even a substance use issue can be framed through liberal consideration if it
was connected to service-related stress, command climate, or untreated mental
health conditions. An attorney or VSO should help frame this.

### Private-Pay Tinnitus Screening (Strengthen the Claim)

Get a private audiogram BEFORE filing the VA claim. This provides civilian
medical documentation of tinnitus that the VA can't dismiss.

| Provider | Location | Cost | Notes |
|----------|----------|------|-------|
| **AudioNova / Hear Michigan** | Multiple MI locations | **FREE** hearing screening | Free screening + tinnitus consultation. Not a full diagnostic. |
| **O'Connor Hearing** (Washtenaw) | Ann Arbor area | $67–$98 (hearing test) | Full diagnostic audiogram. Written results provided. |
| **General private audiologist** | Lansing/Detroit area | $67–$120 | Ask for "diagnostic audiogram with tinnitus evaluation" |

**What to ask for**: A diagnostic hearing test (audiogram) and a written note
documenting that the patient reports tinnitus. Tinnitus is subjective — there is
no machine that measures it. The audiogram shows hearing damage; the doctor's
note confirms the patient reports ringing. Together they support the VA claim.

**Budget**: $0 (free screening) to ~$120 (full diagnostic). Worth every dollar
as evidence for both the VA claim and the disability rating.

### How to File

1. **Create Intent to File** at va.gov — this locks in your effective date. You
   then have 1 year to submit the full application.
2. Complete VA Form 21-526EZ online
3. Attach DD-214, personal statement (21-4138), buddy statements, any medical records
4. VA will first run the **Character of Discharge determination**
5. If COD is favorable: proceeds to C&P exam for tinnitus
6. Processing: typically 2–4 months for tinnitus itself, but COD review adds time

### Personal Statement Tips

**Be specific, not vague.**

Bad: "I was around loud noise in the Army."
Good: "I served as infantry at [unit] from [dates]. During field exercises and
deployments, I was exposed to sustained gunfire, IED detonations, and heavy
vehicle noise without adequate hearing protection. I first noticed persistent
ringing in my [left/right/both] ears during [timeframe]. The ringing is
[constant/intermittent] and sounds like [high-pitched tone/buzzing/hissing].
It affects my ability to [sleep/concentrate/hear conversations]. It has been
present since service and has not improved."

### After the 10% — Secondary Claims

Once tinnitus is service-connected at 10%, DaSean can file for:
- **Hearing loss** (secondary to acoustic trauma) — rated 0%–100% based on audiogram
- **Sleep disturbance** secondary to tinnitus
- **Anxiety/depression** secondary to chronic tinnitus
- These stack using VA combined rating math. 10% + 10% + 10% = 27% (rounded to 30%)

An attorney or Veterans Service Organization (VSO) can help with secondary claims.
