### UTMA Accounts for the Girls

Michigan's Uniform Transfers to Minors Act (MCL 554.521) allows a
custodian (DaSean) to open savings accounts in each daughter's name.
DaSean controls the accounts until each girl reaches 18.

**How they work with child support:**

In Michigan, child support is the **child's right**, not the parent's
(*Evink v Evink*, 214 Mich App 172 (1995)). DaSean is the custodian of
that right. Child support serves two functions:

1. **Stabilization** — housing, food, childcare, school, medical. These
   current expenses draw from the Child Support Operating Account.
2. **Accumulation** — surplus flows to the UTMAs. Savings in the
   children's names. Transparent, auditable, dedicated.

DaSean uses the operating account for legitimate child expenses (rent at
Samantha, groceries, compensating Deanna for childcare, school supplies,
medical). The remainder splits evenly into Sahana's and Allanna's UTMAs.

**Paying Deanna from child support is clean.** She provides childcare
during DaSean's parenting time. That's a documented child-related
expense. Child support covering childcare costs is textbook.

**Paying Samantha rent from child support is clean.** The children need
housing. Rent is a child-related housing cost factored into every
Michigan child support guideline.

**Why this matters for custody**: DaSean is the parent actively saving
for his children's future while also transparently funding their current
needs. The contrast: Shayla collected SNAP, Medicaid, and FIP for
children in DaSean's care and can't produce a single receipt showing the
money went to them.

**Why this matters for the girls**: When Sahana turns 18, she has a
savings account her father started when she was 9 — the same summer
her mother tried to take her away from him. Even if it's $3,000 by
then, the lesson is more valuable than the money.

Start at $10 each. Pre-support, DaSean contributes $10/week from
personal income. Once support is ordered, surplus flows automatically.
The amount doesn't matter at first — the habit and the documentation do.

## Part 3: The Debit-to-Credit Transition

DaSean lives on his debit card right now. The goal isn't to replace
debit with credit — it's to run both in parallel so credit builds while
debit stays safe.

### The System

| What | How He Pays | Why |
|------|-------------|-----|
| **Phone bill** | Discover card (auto-pay) | Builds credit utilization history |
| **Gas/fuel** | Discover card | 2% cash back at gas stations |
| **One meal out/month** | Discover card | 2% cash back at restaurants |
| **Everything else** | Debit card from checking | Stay in budget, no overspending risk |

**Rule**: The Discover card is ONLY for the 2-3 recurring items above.
Total monthly charge: $50–80. Pay in full before statement close.
This keeps utilization under 40% on a $200 limit (good) and builds
cash back rewards (free money).

As credit limit increases (after Discover graduation), the card can
handle more — but the discipline stays the same. **The debit card
remains the primary spending tool.** The credit card is a
credit-building machine that happens to give cash back.

## Part 4: Credit Tradeline Strategy

Credit score is built on **payment history (35%)**, **credit utilization
(30%)**, **credit age (15%)**, **credit mix (10%)**, and **new accounts
(10%)**. To build from zero in 12 months, we stack multiple tradelines
that report to all three bureaus simultaneously.

### Tradeline 1: Secured Credit Card (Revolving Credit)

**Option A (preferred): Navy Federal cashRewards Secured** — if NFCU

| Military perks | SCRA protections if applicable |

**Option B (fallback): Discover it Secured** — if NFCU doesn't work out.

| Detail | Spec |
|--------|------|
| Deposit | $200 (refundable) |
| Annual fee | $0 |

| Reports to | Equifax, Experian, TransUnion (all three) |
| Auto-upgrade review | 7 months |
| Apply | discover.com or in-person |

**How to use either card**:
- Put ONE small recurring charge on it (phone bill, Netflix, gas fill-up)
- **Pay the balance in full BEFORE the statement closes** — this reports
  near-zero utilization. Under 10% utilization = maximum score benefit.
- Never carry a balance. Never pay interest. The card is a credit-building
  tool, not a spending tool.
- If $200 is tight right now: **Capital One Platinum Secured** requires
  only **$49 deposit** for a $200 credit line. No rewards, but lowest
  barrier to entry.

**Kevin fronts the $200 deposit.** DaSean pays it back from first

## Rules (Non-Negotiable)

1. **Never miss a payment.** Set everything to auto-pay. A single
   missed payment can drop a score 50-100 points and stays on the
   report for 7 years.

2. **Never use more than 10% of credit limit.** On a $200 Discover
   card, that means never carry more than $20 at statement close.
   Pay before the statement closes, not on the due date.

3. **Never close a credit account.** Closing reduces available credit
   (hurts utilization ratio) and eventually reduces credit age. Even
   if a card has a $0 balance, keep it open.

4. **Never apply for credit you don't need.** Each application is a
   hard inquiry that costs 5-10 points. Only open accounts that are
   part of this plan.

5. **Check score monthly, not daily.** Discover provides free FICO
   score in the app. Check once a month. Score watching daily leads
   to anxiety, not progress.

6. **Keep receipts for everything.** Mortgage underwriters will ask
   about large deposits, irregular transactions, and cash handling.
   Clean banking from day one makes underwriting smooth.
