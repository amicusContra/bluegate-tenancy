## GUIDANCE: HANDLING THE OTH DISCHARGE

### The Rules

1. **Never lie.** If a form asks for discharge characterization and the field is
   mandatory, write it accurately. Lying on a federal application is a crime.
   Lying on a private application is grounds for immediate termination if
   discovered later.

2. **Never volunteer.** No resume, cover letter, or interview opener should
   mention the discharge characterization. Lead with service, not paperwork.

3. **Know what employers actually check.** Most private employers verify dates
   of service and branch through a DD-214 or self-reported info. They rarely
   request discharge characterization. Federal employers (VA, DoD, USPS) DO
   check and it DOES matter.

### On the Resume

The polished resume says:

> "United States Army veteran — 3+ years active duty Infantry service"

This is factual. It does not claim Honorable. It does not say OTH. It states
service, duration, and branch. Nobody reading this sentence will ask a
follow-up question at a job fair.

**Do NOT put**: "Honorably discharged," "Honorable discharge," or "served
honorably." These are verifiable claims that will backfire.

**Do NOT put**: "Other Than Honorable" on the resume. The resume is a marketing
document, not a legal disclosure. The discharge characterization belongs on
application forms that specifically ask for it — not on a resume.

### On Job Applications

**If the form asks "Type of discharge" (mandatory field):**

Write: `Other Than Honorable — upgrade pending with Army Discharge Review Board`

The "upgrade pending" addition is factual (once the ADRB application is filed)
and signals that this is being actively addressed. It reframes the OTH from a
permanent mark to a process in motion.

**If the form asks "Type of discharge" (optional field):**

Leave blank. Or write: `General (upgrade pending)` ONLY if DaSean has reason to
believe an upgrade is imminent. Otherwise leave blank.

**If the form asks "Have you ever been discharged from the military other than
honorably?":**

Answer truthfully: Yes. If there's space for explanation, write:

> "Received OTH characterization in 2015. Currently pursuing discharge upgrade
> through the Army Discharge Review Board based on mitigating circumstances
> including command climate. 3+ years of honorable active duty Infantry service
> with awards including National Defense Service Medal and GWOT Service Medal."

### In Interviews

**If never asked**: Never bring it up.

**If asked "Tell me about your military service":**

> "I served three and a half years as Infantry at Schofield Barracks in Hawaii
> with the 25th Infantry Division. I was a Combat Lifesaver, responsible for
> tactical medical care. My unit experience included [field exercises, training
> rotations, etc.]. After the Army I moved into warehouse supervision and
> retail management."

Note: This answer is complete, positive, and true. It does not mention the
discharge. Most interviewers will move on.

**If directly asked "What was your discharge status?":**

> "I received an other-than-honorable characterization. The circumstances
> involved a command climate issue that I'm currently working to address through
> the Army Discharge Review Board with supporting statements from other members
> of my unit. It doesn't reflect my service — I have three years of solid
> performance, a National Defense Service Medal, and a GWOT Medal. Since then
> I've worked in management, I'm pursuing technology education through the VA's
> VET TEC program, and I'm building a stable home for my family."

**Key elements of this answer:**
- Acknowledge (don't dodge — dodging looks worse)
- Context (command climate, not personal failure narrative)
- Action (ADRB upgrade in progress — shows agency)
- Redirect (post-service record is strong)
- Forward momentum (education, employment, family)

### Employer Categories and Risk Level

| Employer Type | Asks About Discharge? | OTH Impact | Strategy |
|---------------|----------------------|------------|----------|
| **Private retail/warehouse/logistics** | Rarely | Minimal | Don't mention. Lead with experience. |
| **Staffing agencies** | Sometimes (form) | Low | Fill form honestly if asked, note "upgrade pending" |
| **Michigan Works / WIOA programs** | Veteran status only | None | Identify as veteran for priority of service |
| **Federal government (VA, DoD, USPS)** | Always (SF-171/OF-612) | High until upgrade | Apply after discharge upgrade is granted |
| **State/county government** | Sometimes | Moderate | Depends on position; note upgrade pending |
| **Uber/Lyft** | No | None | They check driving record, not military record |
| **VET TEC / education** | Already cleared | None | VET TEC approval means VA already cleared the COD |

### The Long Game

Once the ADRB upgrades the discharge to General Under Honorable (or Honorable):
- Update the DD-214 (the ADRB issues a new one)
- Update every application and form
- The OTH question disappears permanently
- Federal employment opens up fully
- The resume line becomes: "Honorably discharged veteran" (or "General Under
  Honorable Conditions")

Until then: lead with service, don't lie, don't volunteer, redirect to
post-service record and forward momentum.
